# Abschnitt 9: The Wet Column

**Memo from Tobias Renn to the maintenance log, posted at the boiler room hatch, 16 October, 07:14**

The condensate in the subfloor has reached the junction box at J-4. I am logging this because the drip is now rhythmic — one drop every four seconds, striking the cable tray above the relay, and the sound carries through the pipe chase like a metronome set to a tempo nobody chose. The insulation Maren left in the chase is damp through. Her handwriting has begun to bleed at the margins.

I have rerouted the secondary line to bypass the wet column. This is a temporary measure. The primary line remains compromised.

**Note from Sister Odile to the Warden, hand-delivered, 16 October, 07:31**

You will want to know that the east corridor lamps have stopped strobing. This is not a repair. This is the carrier tone finding its frequency. The flicker has become a hum, and the hum is steady, and the steady is worse.

I have stitched the ninth column for fourteen months. You knew this. You signed the requisitions for the thread. What you did not ask was why I chose mercerized cotton, why I matched the weave to the ledger binding, why I counted thirty-two threads per inch. You did not ask because you did not want the answer, and the answer is this: a shadow column must be legible to the audit, not to you.

The parameter you call Article Twenty-Six is drifting. I have watched it drift since the first frost. The calibration spec defines it as *minimum transmission threshold for instructional content* — the floor below which no signal may fall. You have been running it at zero since spring. The guests have been receiving nothing. The guests have been *taught* nothing. This is not a moral failure. This is a fault condition. The audit will read it as such.

**Letter from Maren Voss to Ilya Brandt, left in the pipe chase insulation, 16 October, 07:48**

Ilya —

The canister is clear. I scraped the particulate from the seal with a spoon from the kitchen, and the line opened, and the archive is receiving. I am telling you this because you will hear the relay click and you will think it is the storm. It is not the storm. It is the unsigned verification moving through the pneumatic tube, and it is carrying everything — the zeroed tolerances, the stitched column, the calibration data the Warden's hand was too unsteady to sign.

But the wet column is still dripping. Four seconds, every four seconds, and the bypass is holding but the primary is not. If the condensate reaches the relay before the canister clears the junction, the transmission will corrupt. I need you to watch the tray. I need you to tell me if the drip quickens.

Do not come to the relay. The Warden is in the east corridor. He is counting the hum.

— Maren

**Technical directive from Tobias Renn to all station clerks, posted at the admin corridor telegraph, 16 October, 08:02**

Parameter 26 (Instructional Transmission Threshold) recalibrated to minimum viable tolerance per calibration spec, subsection 9.1. All guest education logs to be restored to raw column format effective immediately. The wet column at J-4 is to be monitored at four-second intervals. If the drip rate exceeds one per three seconds, evacuate the relay junction and seal the chase.

The canister is in transit. Do not intercept.
