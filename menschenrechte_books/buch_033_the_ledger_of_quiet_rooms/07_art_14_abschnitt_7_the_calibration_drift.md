Maren —

The grinder noise carries through the chase wall. They are cutting the trunk line at JB-7, just as the log predicted. Forty-seven hertz. You asked what Parameter 12 measures. I think it measures *eligibility*. The checkbox in Room 4's row — the only one marked — corresponds to the forty-eight-hour gap. The gap *is* the parameter. When the count reaches zero, the system decides whether the subject qualifies for transfer. Or disposal.

**Memo from Tobias Renn to Sister Odile, slipped under the sacristy door, 16 October, 06:38**

Sister —

Your cassock hem. I remember the thread count — thirty-two per inch, mercerized cotton, the same weave the archive uses for ledger binding. You unpicked it at 05:55. The ninth column exists because you put it there, stitch by stitch, over fourteen months. A shadow ledger inside the binding.

The audit changes the calibration. Article Fourteen — the asylum parameter — has always been a theoretical column in the transfer protocol. "If subject demonstrates well-founded fear, suspend Disposition pending external review." Theoretical because no external review has ever been granted. The Warden's office treats it as a divide-by-zero error. They zero the field.

But the audit means the Central Registry will read the raw columns. They will see Parameter 12 unchecked in every row but one. They will see the forty-eight-hour gap in Room 4. They will ask why the dual-signature block remains unsigned.

The pneumatic tube canister at J-4 contains the Warden's calibration data. If we clear the particulate contamination — if we send it — the archive receives the unsigned verification. The audit finds a broken chain.

If we do not clear it, the canister sits in the throat. The audit finds a blocked line.

Either way, the strobe counts down.
