# Abschnitt 8: The Carrier Wave

**Letter from Maren Voss to Ilya Brandt, left in the pipe chase insulation, 16 October, 06:52**

Ilya —

The lamps in the east corridor are strobing again. Forty-seven flickers between each breath I count, and the ozone is so thick at the switch cabinet that my eyes water when I pass. You said the frequency matches the carrier tone. I believe you now. I believe the gap is the parameter. I believe the checkbox in Room 4 is a throat waiting to be cut.

You asked me once what Article Nineteen means in the calibration spec. I found it last night in the archive binding — the ninth column, the one Tobias says Sister Odile stitched by hand over fourteen months. Article Nineteen is not a right in this building. It is a *transmission tolerance*. The spec defines it as the permissible deviation for signal reception across closed boundaries. "Interference" is logged as a fault. "Seeking information" is logged as a breach. The Warden's clerks have been zeroing the column since spring.

But here is what the audit notice changes: the Central Registry reads raw columns, not processed ones. If the canister at J-4 reaches the archive with the unsigned verification, they will see the zeroed tolerances. They will see what we have been forbidden to transmit.

Tobias showed me the cassock hem this morning — thirty-two threads per inch, mercerized cotton, the same weave as the ledger binding. Sister Odile built the shadow column stitch by stitch. She knew the calibration would drift. She knew the audit would come.

The grinder is still cutting at JB-7. The condensate is rising. I am going to the relay junction now. If I clear the particulate from the canister, the line opens. If the line opens, the archive receives everything.

Do not wait for the dual signature. The Warden's hand shakes too much to sign.

— Maren
