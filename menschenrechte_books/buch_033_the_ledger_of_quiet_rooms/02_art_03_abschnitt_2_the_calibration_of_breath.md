# Abschnitt 2: The Calibration of Breath

**Memo from Sister Odile to the Warden, 15 October, 11:47**

Warden,

The audit notice arrived on the 09:00 supply boat before the storm severed the line. Central Registry. Three auditors. They will be here by the 22nd if the sea permits, and the sea does not permit. I have seen the requisition forms they require — Form 7-C, Form 12-A, the biometric annexes. Every guest file must be current to the hour. Every scale calibrated to the gram. Every pneumatic relay cleaned and certified.

I have moved the brass standard from the chapel to the kitchen scale. Eleven grams light. I have added the correction weight — a brass cylinder marked *11g* from the apothecary set — and the scale now reads true. I have logged the adjustment in the maintenance ledger, page 44, line 3. My signature is there. The scale is no longer wrong.

But the tube still breathes dust. I cleared the relay contacts again at 11:00. Grey dust on my sleeve by 11:15. Something is grinding in the walls, and it is grinding fine.

**Note from Maren Voss to Ilya Brandt, slipped under the door of Room 4, 15 October, 12:05**

Ilya —

Tobias told me about the spoons. Thirty-one spoken, thirty-two present. He said you closed your book before the count could be verified. He said the Warden stood outside your door and his pen moved.

I have been watching the kitchen staff weigh the midday bread. Each loaf passes the scale twice — once before the oven, once after. The Warden calls it *parameter verification*. Sister Odile calls it *protection of physical integrity*. I call it the only way to know the flour has not walked away in the night.

The audit changes everything. If the auditors find a discrepancy in the spoon ledger, they will trace it to the breakfast table. They will trace it to you. They will ask why the spoken number differed from the physical count, and they will not accept *I miscounted* as an answer.

I have started a second ledger. One for them. One for us. Do not ask which is which.

— M.
