# The Ledger of Small Mercies

The customs house leaned over the river like a man who had lost an argument with the wind. Inside, three clerks, two chairs, and one stove shared a room built for none of them.

Mara Voss ruled the ration desk with a stamp she never set down. "Name."

"Pell."

"Old Pell is not a name. It is a rumor."

"It is the only one I have left," Old Pell said, and held out his bowl.

Mara stamped it. The bowl was not on the list. The stamp did not care.

Tobin Ash kept the movement ledger and a private tally of who owed him favors. "You crossed the bridge at noon," he told a fisherman. "The ledger says you crossed at eleven."

"The ledger can swim, then."

Tobin wrote *insubordinate* and underlined it twice. Underlining was the only exercise he got.

Sister Iyari ran the spoken-word register, which meant she decided which sentences counted. "Say something," she told a woman at the window.

"I love my daughter."

"Too general. Say something smaller."

"I saved her a piece of bread."

Iyari wrote it down. "That counts."

The Auditor arrived on a Thursday, which was the day the master ledger was supposed to be balanced. He carried a satchel and a face like a closed account. He turned pages. He turned them again.

"Page four hundred and eleven is missing," the Auditor said.

Mara did not look up. "Then four hundred and twelve is now four hundred and eleven. The river does not notice."

"The river does not. The ledger does." The Auditor set a finger on a line. "This line is the only record of a resident. No page, no line. No line, no ration. No ration—"

"No person," Tobin said, and for once he sounded awake.

Old Pell put down his bowl. "Whose line?"

The Auditor read it aloud. The room went quiet in the way rooms do when the stove has gone out and no one wants to be the one to say it.

"That is my line," said Old Pell.

"It was," said the Auditor.

Sister Iyari closed the spoken-word register. "He said something this morning. I wrote it down. That counts."

"It counts toward speech," the Auditor said. "Not toward existence."

Mara set down her stamp. She had not set down her stamp in eleven years. "Then we find the page."

"And if we do not?" said Tobin.

"Then we argue about who decides," Mara said. "We are good at that. It is the only thing we do without a form."

The Auditor opened his satchel and took out a blank page. He laid it on the desk, square to the corner. "Begin," he said. "I will be taking notes."
