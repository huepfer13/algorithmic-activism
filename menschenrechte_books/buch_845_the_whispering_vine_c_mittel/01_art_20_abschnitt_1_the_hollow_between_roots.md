# Abschnitt 1: The Hollow Between Roots

The creature did not attack. It dissolved.

Where its eyes had glowed, there remained only a faint shimmer of heat, and where its voice had rolled like thunder through the glade, there was now the ordinary rustle of leaves. Kael stood with his fists still clenched, his breath shallow, staring at the empty space as though waiting for the shadow to reassemble itself and demand something more.

"It tested us," Aria said quietly. She had not moved from her place. Her hand rested flat against the trunk of a great oak, and her eyes were distant, reading something in the bark that the others could not see. "It wanted to know whether we would scatter."

Elian knelt where the creature had stood. The ground there was cold, unnaturally so, and the symbols that had guided them earlier were now half-erased, as if a thumb had been dragged across wet clay. He traced the remaining lines with his fingertip and frowned.

"These marks," he murmured, "they are not a map. They are a ledger."

Kael turned. "A ledger of what?"

"Of who passed and who did not." Elian rose and brushed the damp earth from his knees. "The vine keeps accounts."

They walked on because standing still felt like an invitation. The path narrowed, and the ancient trees pressed close, their roots rising from the soil like the ribs of some buried creature. Aria began to hum, softly, a tune without words, and the whispering of the vine seemed to answer her in a lower register, a kind of counterpoint that made the hairs on Kael's arms stand upright.

"The air tastes of iron," Kael said after a while. He stopped and pressed his palm against a rusted pipe that ran along the forest floor, half-swallowed by moss. The pipe was cold and dead, its joints sealed with a fine grey powder that clung to his fingers when he pulled them away. "Something has clogged the old lines. Paper dust, maybe. The relays down the way are grinding."

"Then we go around," Aria said.

"There is no around," Elian replied. He pointed to where the pipe vanished into a wall of thorns. "The vine grows over everything that tries to pass. It does not permit a second road."

Kael wiped his hand on his trousers and looked at the two of them, and something in his face shifted, a farmer's patience giving way to a farmer's stubbornness. "Then we go through. Together. No one walks ahead, no one falls behind."

Aria opened her mouth as if to argue, then closed it. She had learned, in the short hours since the glade, that Kael's silences were heavier than his words.

They linked arms, awkwardly at first, three strangers bound by nothing but the forest's refusal to let them part. The thorns did not tear them. The vine did not whisper. And somewhere beneath their feet, deep in the dark where the roots turned, a mechanism long dormant began, slowly, to turn.

Elian felt it first. He looked down, and his face went pale.

"Kael," he said. "The ground is breathing."
