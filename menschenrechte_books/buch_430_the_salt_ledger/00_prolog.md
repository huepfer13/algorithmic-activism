# The Salt Ledger

In the ninth year of the quarantine, the harbour bells of Vaernholm rang at dawn and at dusk, and between those two soundings the Salt Ledger ruled the city as surely as any crown.

The Ledger kept its offices in the old customs house above the fish quay, where the walls sweated salt in summer and frost in winter. From those rooms, a directorate of five keepers governed every entry, every exit, and every ration that passed the sea-gates. No ship might dock, no family might move between the inner wards and the outer wharves, no grain might be sold, without a clerk's mark in the great bound volumes. The clerks recorded not only goods but words — for in Vaernholm, a citizen's speech was taxable property, and a careless remark overheard at the market could be entered against a household's name and weighed like any other debt. A single disputed entry, unappealed within three days, could condemn a family to the outer wharves, where the plague ships anchored and the air hung thick with tar and sickness.

Maren Voss had kept the eastern register for eleven years. She wrote with a cramped and perfect hand, and she had never once in those years permitted an erasure; yet she kept, sewn into the lining of her coat, a private list of every entry she believed to be false. Elias Tamm, the youngest of the keepers, argued openly that the Ledger's books were not scripture but instruments, and might be corrected by living hands — a position that had cost him his father's name in the western volume. Sister Odile of the Grey Sisters tended the sick in the quarantine sheds and came each morning to certify deaths, and she had learned to read the Ledger's margins better than its text, for the margins told her which families would be turned out before the week was done. Corvin Hask, the directorate's eldest, had signed the first plague ordinance a decade ago and now walked the quay each night counting the moored ships as though the number might absolve him. Petra Lund commanded the harbour watch; she was a woman of few words and a long memory, and she kept her own tally of the Ledger's errors scratched into the stock of her crossbow.

On the fourteenth of March, a merchant vessel called the *Aurelia* entered the roads flying the white flag of clean passage, and the clerk on duty entered her cargo and her crew and her master's spoken declaration into the eastern register. The next morning, three of her sailors showed the salt-rash. The entry naming the *Aurelia* clean stood in Maren Voss's own hand, and by the law of the Ledger, the master and his crew were already condemned, and the family that had vouched for them with it.

*Entered this fourteenth day of March: the ship Aurelia, her cargo salt and hides, her passage declared clean. The wardens of the eastern gate are summoned to answer for the entry. The outer wharves are closed until the directorate convenes.*
