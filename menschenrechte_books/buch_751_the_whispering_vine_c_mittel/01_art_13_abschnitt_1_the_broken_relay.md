# Abschnitt 1: The Broken Relay

The shadow creature did not attack. It simply waited, its coal-bright eyes tracking each of them as though measuring the weight of their promise to one another. Then it dissolved into the underbrush, leaving behind a silence so complete that Elian could hear the sap moving in the vine's tendrils.

Kael took the lead without a word, his boots finding the etched symbols as though he had walked this path a thousand times. Aria followed, her poet's eyes cataloguing every shift of light. Elian brought up the rear, and it was he who first noticed the pipe.

It ran along the forest floor, half-swallowed by moss—a pneumatic line of dull brass, the kind that once carried messages between the old waystations. Now it was blocked. A clot of something dark and fibrous jammed the junction where two segments met, and when Elian knelt to touch it, his fingers came away dusted with a fine grey powder. Paper grit, he realized. The relays had been grinding themselves to dust for years, and no one had come to clear them.

"Leave it," Kael said, not turning. "The path bends left."

"The path bends where the forest says it bends," Aria murmured, but she followed.

They walked until the trees thinned into a hollow where three stones stood like broken teeth. Between them, the vine had woven a canopy, and beneath it the air was warm and wrong. Elian felt it first in his teeth—a vibration, low and constant, as though the earth itself were clearing its throat.

The creature returned at the hollow's edge, but it was not alone. Two more shapes emerged from the shadows, smaller, quicker, their forms less smoke than splintered light. They circled the three travelers in widening arcs, and each time they passed the brass pipe, they paused, as if listening to something inside it.

Kael drew his knife. Aria's hand found Elian's sleeve. The whispers of the vine rose to a single sustained note, and Elian understood, with a clarity that chilled him, that the pipe was not merely blocked—it was *waiting* to be cleared, and whatever waited inside had been patient far longer than any of them had been alive.

"Protection first," Kael said, and stepped between Aria and the nearest shape.

The shape laughed, a sound like wind through a cracked bell. "Protection is a door," it said. "You may leave any room. You may return to any room. But the door remembers every hand that touched it, and it does not forget the ones that fled."

Elian's fingers found the pipe again. The grey dust clung to his skin, and beneath it, faintly, he felt the pulse of something moving—slow, deliberate, coming toward the junction from the dark.

"We are not fleeing," Aria said, her voice steady though her grip on Elian's sleeve had tightened. "We are listening."

The three shapes stilled. The vine's whisper dropped to a hum. And from deep within the blocked pipe, a sound answered—a tapping, rhythmic and patient, as of a message long delayed finally finding its way to the surface.
