# The Ministry of Adequate Happiness

The basement level of the Ministry for Citizen Satisfaction smelled of wet paper and ambition that had given up somewhere around 1987. Fluorescent tubes buzzed in the key of bureaucratic despair, casting their light upon five desks arranged in a pentagon of mutual suspicion. Above each desk hung a speaking file, its cardboard lips flapping with the quiet insistence of a conscience that had been outsourced to stationery.

Ilse Brandt, Senior Officer for Smile Allocation, had not smiled since the incident with the mirror in 1994. She now sat before a form — Application for Spontaneous Joy, Form 7-B — and read it the way one reads a death sentence: slowly, and twice. The applicant, one Herr Wilfried Mohn, requested permission to smile at his granddaughter's birthday party. The form was properly triple-stamped, but the third stamp had been applied at an angle of eighty-seven degrees rather than the mandated ninety.

"Otto," she said, without looking up. "The angle."

Otto Pfister, Deputy Registrar of Complaint Complaints, adjusted his spectacles with the precision of a man who had once measured the distance between his own ears. "The angle is within tolerance," he said. "Tolerance was revised in March."

"Tolerance was revised *downward* in March."

"Tolerance was revised *sideways*," said Mara Voss from the far corner, where she maintained the Archive of Grievances About Grievances, a filing system so deep it had developed its own weather. "March was a sideways month. I have the minutes."

Herr Kleinschmidt, who had not spoken since Tuesday and who communicated primarily through the strategic placement of paperclips, moved a single clip three centimeters to the left. Everyone understood this to mean: *the stamp*.

The stamp. There was only one functioning stamp left in the entire sub-level. The others had been requisitioned, decommissioned, or had simply stopped stamping out of what the maintenance report described as "existential fatigue." The working stamp — Stamp 1, Serial 0001, "The Honest One" — sat in a locked drawer to which five keys existed and five keyholders did not trust one another.

Juno, the youngest and therefore most dangerous, because she still believed in the system, had the drawer key that week. She also had a theory.

"What if," Juno said, "we simply approve the smile, and the angle becomes retroactively correct through an amendment to the tolerance record?"

The speaking files gasped. One of them — the file on Ilse's desk, which had been listening with the smug attention of a witness — began to cough up a form.

"That," said Ilse, "would require a Form 12-C."

"A Form 12-C requires a stamp," said Otto.

"Which requires," said Mara, emerging from the Archive with a document that had been filed in 1974 and had aged accordingly, "a Form 12-C."

Herr Kleinschmidt moved the paperclip back.

The fluorescent tube above Juno's desk flickered, considered its options, and died. In the darkness, someone — no one would admit who — whispered the word *escalation*.

The file on Ilse's desk coughed again, more violently this time, and produced a single sheet of paper. It was blank except for a header: *Notice of Pending Review of the Review of the Pending Notice.*

The Ministry, it seemed, had noticed them noticing.
