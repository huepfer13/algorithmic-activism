# Abschnitt 5: The Calibration of Absence

The fifth-article scan began with Sector East, and by the third household the intercom system had developed a stutter. A high-frequency whine threaded through the speaker grilles, thin as a wire pulled taut, and Marek watched Tomas flinch at his station—the pen slipping again, this time leaving a mark that ran off the ledger's edge and onto the desk's scarred wood.

"Interference," the Inspector said, not looking up from her case. "The damping fields are cycling. Continue."

The whine rose and fell like a breath held too long. Marek pressed his palm against the unmarked channels in his pocket and felt them warm, as if the stone remembered something the room did not. He thought of the rods' hum from two days prior, the way it had climbed through the table legs and settled in his teeth, and how outside the rain had paused at the Ministry's roofline before drifting west—toward the bakery's dead ovens, toward Ilse's careful lines.

The intercom crackled. A woman's voice, filtered through static: "I have said nothing. I have said nothing at all."

"Speech-pressure baseline exceeds threshold by four units," Tomas read. His trembling hand hovered over the isolation protocol. "Article nine calibration requires verification of arbitrary detention risk. The subject's vocal signature indicates—" The whine spiked. Tomas's jaw tightened; his eyes lost focus for a heartbeat, then returned. "—indicates duress without cause."

The Inspector withdrew a second slip of paper, this one bearing a watermark Marek recognized from the Ministry's internal archives. "Article nine operates on a proximity principle," she said. "Detention is arbitrary when the subject's physical removal from their registered sector exceeds the calibrated radius. The outer districts have been redrawn. The radius has shrunk."

Marek's pen stopped above the ledger. The unmarked channels pulsed once against his thigh.

"The woman in Sector East," he said, and his voice surprised him by holding steady, "her sector boundary was moved last quarter. The new line runs through her kitchen."

The Inspector looked at him then, and the brass rods in her case hummed a single low note, like a question posed to the air itself.

"Then her removal would not be arbitrary," she said. "It would be administrative."

The intercom whined again, higher now, and somewhere beyond the Ministry's sealed windows Marek heard—or thought he heard—the sound of rain shifting, a whisper of water changing its mind.
