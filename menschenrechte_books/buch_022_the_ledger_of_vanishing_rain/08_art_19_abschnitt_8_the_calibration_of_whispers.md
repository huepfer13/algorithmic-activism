# Abschnitt 8: The Calibration of Whispers

The northern relay had not been touched in fourteen years, and the ozone smell hit Tomas first — sharp, electrical, like a struck match held too long beneath the nose. The fluorescent tubes above the relay rack stuttered at forty-seven cycles, throwing the corridor into a rhythmic dusk that made the brass fittings blink in and out of existence. He pressed his sleeve to his face and knelt beside the junction box.

"Article Nineteen parameter," he called back to the Inspector, who stood at the threshold with her case held against her ribs. "The conduit was never calibrated for bidirectional flow. Speech returns through the same pipe as water, and the Ministry's filters were built to catch only one."

The Inspector's jaw tightened. "Then we recalibrate. Freedom of reception and transmission — through any medium, across any boundary — that is not a privilege to be rationed. It is a channel, and channels must remain open."

Marek, still at the wall monitor, remembered the twin rods humming that single low note in her case weeks ago, the way the question had seemed to hang in the air unanswered. Now the rods lay silent, and the question had found its moment.

"The pressure differential is too steep," Tomas said. "If I open the northern valve at full aperture, the backflow will rupture the seals in Sector West. We lose the drinking line."

Ilse stepped forward, her map unrolled across the floor, the blue halo at the eastern contour now bleeding toward the northern arch. "Then we don't open it at full aperture. We calibrate to the asylum vector — nine liters per capita, but routed through the ledger's secondary branch. Speech and water share the same ledger line. If the Ministry's auditors read the flow as ration, they will not think to measure what travels with it."

Nadia appeared in the doorway, her own ledger pressed to her chest. "The audit team is at the western stair. They have already stamped three clerks for irregular filing."

The Inspector opened her case. The rods lay cold, but her hand hovered above them. "Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence," she said, and the ledger beside her hand recorded the words in a thread of ink that curled toward the northern arch. "Seal the western line. Divert everything to the northern relay. Let them audit an empty pipe."

Tomas turned the calibration screw. The needle trembled, then held — four units above baseline, then three, then two. The ozone sharpened, and the lights flickered once, twice, and steadied.

"Northern relay open," he said. "Reception and transmission calibrated to nine liters per capita. Awaiting first message."

The pipe hummed. Somewhere beyond the sealed arch, a voice began to travel.
