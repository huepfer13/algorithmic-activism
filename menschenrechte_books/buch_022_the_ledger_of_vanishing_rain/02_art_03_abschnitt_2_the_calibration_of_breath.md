# Abschnitt 2: The Calibration of Breath

The brass rods in the Inspector's satchel had begun to hum before dawn, a low vibration that Marek felt through the floorboards of the registration hall as he stacked the night's protocol sheets into their binders. The hum was not the usual faint tremor of accumulated fear—it was sharper, more insistent, the sound of instruments straining against a baseline they could no longer hold. He had seen the rods calibrated once, years ago, when the Ministry still published its tolerance tables: the third article's parameter, the one governing the preservation of life and bodily integrity, had been set to a fixed resistance of nine hundred and forty units, a threshold below which the rods would not register distress. Now the needles drifted past that mark before the morning ration call had even finished.

"The Central Registry is sending a revision team," Tomas whispered, his left hand shaking as he pressed a fresh seal onto a stack of requisition denials. "Word came through the tube an hour ago. They want the ledgers reconciled by nightfall."

Marek glanced toward the sealed window where the rain traced its silver line over the Ministry's roof—the same line Ilse had shown him on her contraband map three nights past, the thin blue ribbon that curled around the government quarter while the eastern alleys cracked and whitened under the permanent drought. He had memorized the alternative channels she had drawn, the asterisks marking empty cisterns, and he felt the weight of that knowledge now like a stone in his throat.

The Inspector entered without knocking, her boots leaving faint impressions in the dust that had settled overnight. She carried a leather case marked with the Registry's double seal, and she set it on the table between them with the careful precision of someone handling volatile material.

"The revision requires a full audit of speech-pressure records," she said, her voice flat. "Every entry from the past quarter must be re-verified against the third-article baseline. If the rods show deviation, the corresponding citizens will be scheduled for recalibration."

Tomas's pen slipped, leaving a blot on the ledger's margin. The Inspector watched the ink spread, then turned her gaze to Marek.

"You will assist," she said. "The cartographer's maps have been flagged for review. The Registry wants to know why certain channels appear in her sketches that do not appear in the official surveys."

Marek felt the hum of the rods intensify, a vibration that traveled up through the table legs and into his teeth. Outside, the rain hesitated at the edge of the Ministry's roof, then shifted a fraction toward the west—toward the abandoned bakery, toward the dry streets where Ilse had traced her lines.

*On this day, the instruments registered a deviation no ledger could contain.*
