# Abschnitt 9: The Calibration of Knowing

The northern relay breathed. Tomas watched the needle settle at two units above baseline, the memory of its trembling — four, three, two — still warm in his fingertips. Behind the junction box, condensate struck the cable trays in a metronome rhythm: *drip, pause, drip, pause*. The thermal drift in the raised floor had turned the conduit junctions cold, and the air tasted of copper and old paper.

Marek pressed his palm to the wall monitor. The ledger's secondary branch pulsed with a new frequency, not water-pressure but something denser, coded in syllables rather than liters. "The asylum vector carries more than speech," he said. "It carries the curriculum."

Ilse traced the blue halo on her map where it now pooled against the northern arch. "Nine liters per capita. Nine modules per cycle. The Ministry's auditors will read it as ration — they always do — but the ledger's secondary branch doesn't distinguish between hydration and instruction. It only measures flow."

Nadia's ledger, still pressed to her chest, recorded the conversation in ink that curled toward the eastern contour. Her fingers found the marginal notation she had made weeks ago: *Article Twenty-Six parameter — elementary stage compulsory, technical stage generally available, higher stage accessible on merit*. The words had seemed abstract then. Now they hummed in the pipe alongside the voice that had begun to travel.

The Inspector's hand no longer hovered above the rods. She had closed the case. "Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence," she repeated, and the ledger beside her recorded it again, a second thread of ink paralleling the first. "But the curriculum parameter was never calibrated for bidirectional flow either. The Ministry teaches only what serves the ration. What returns through the pipe — questions, corrections, the mathematics of dissent — has nowhere to go but back into the sender."

Tomas adjusted the gain. The voice beyond the arch clarified: a child reciting multiplication tables in a dialect the western sectors had not heard in forty years. Behind it, another voice. Then a chorus.

"Northern relay saturated," Tomas said. "The curriculum vector is backing up into the drinking line. We need a bypass. Or we need to decide which flow gets the pressure."

The Inspector opened her case again. The rods were warm now, humming a chord that made the condensate on the cable trays vibrate in sympathy.
