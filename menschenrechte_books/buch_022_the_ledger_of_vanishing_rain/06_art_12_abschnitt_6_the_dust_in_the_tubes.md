# Abschnitt 6: The Dust in the Tubes

Marek lingered by the relay rack, the fine paper dust that had settled on the contacts catching the dim light like ash from a burnt ledger. The pneumatic tube that usually carried the morning ration notices from Sector West to the Ministry’s core was jammed, a thin slit of cardboard wedged where the air should flow. He brushed a fingertip over the obstruction and felt the familiar tremor of the unmarked channels in his pocket, recalling how the whine had risen and fallen like a breath held too long two days before, as if the stone remembered a vibration the room could not name.

Ilse appeared at the edge of the corridor, her map spread on a metal table, ink lines trembling as though alive. “The rain’s vector has shifted again,” she said, voice low. “If we redraw the contour here, the runoff will reach the eastern cistern before the Ministry’s pumps can divert it.” She tapped a spot where the ink bled into a faint blue halo, a hue that only appeared when the atmospheric pressure dropped below the calibration threshold set by Article 12’s privacy‑interference monitor. The monitor, a brass box humming beside the relay, flickered as the dust disturbed its sensor, spiking the interference reading by three units.

Tomas, his pen still smudged from the previous night’s calibration, glanced at the ledger. The entry for Sector East showed a sudden dip in recorded speech, the numbers falling below the baseline that triggered automatic detention review. He looked up at the Inspector, whose case held the twin rods that had hummed a single low note when Marek had spoken of the woman whose kitchen lay on the redrawn boundary. “The interference is not arbitrary,” Tomas said, “it is a mechanical fault, not a political one.”

The Inspector’s eyes narrowed. She lifted the brass rods, feeling their resonance, and then pressed her palm against the sealed window where the rain clung to the glass in thin, silver threads. “Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence,” she stated, not as a moral claim but as an operational directive. She ordered the tube cleared, the dust blown away with a burst of filtered air, and the relay contacts polished with a soft cloth. As the obstruction gave way, a rush of water surged through the tube, striking the ledger’s edge and causing a fresh line of ink to bloom across the page—a line that traced a new path for the rain, one that curved toward the outer districts where the fields lay cracked and silent.

A distant crack echoed from the bakery’s dead ovens, and for a moment the whine returned, softer this time, as if testing whether the stone would remember again.
