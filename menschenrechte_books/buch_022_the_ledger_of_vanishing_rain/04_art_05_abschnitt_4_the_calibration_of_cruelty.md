# Abschnitt 4: The Calibration of Cruelty

The condensation had begun before the audit's second hour. Marek felt it first as a dampness against his collar, then as a steady rhythm—drip, pause, drip—falling from the thermal drift in the crawlspace above the registration hall, each drop striking the cable shafts with a sound like a metronome counting down to something no one had named.

Tomas had not moved from his station. His left hand still trembled, the blot on the ledger from the morning's slip now dried to a brown stain shaped vaguely like a bird in flight. He kept his eyes on the third-article baseline readout, where the speech-pressure scores scrolled in green columns, each number a citizen's breath measured against the minimum threshold for life and physical integrity.

"The fifth-article scan requires a different calibration," the Inspector said, her voice carrying no more weight than the dripping water. She had not closed her case. The brass rods inside still hummed, though the piercing note from earlier had subsided into something lower, more patient. "Cruelty registers as thermal residue. Inhuman treatment leaves a signature in the skin's electrical field. Degrading punishment—" she paused, consulting a slip of paper Marek had not seen her withdraw, "—manifests as a specific frequency in the vocal cords, detectable only when the subject speaks under duress."

Marek thought of the spike that had made the lamps stutter, the thin tone that had seemed to come from the walls themselves. He had felt it in his palm, a vibration that traveled up his arm and settled behind his eyes. Now, watching the rods' faint glow through the leather case, he understood: they had been calibrating for something. The fifth article was not a protection but a measurement, a way of recording how much a person could endure before their body began to register the damage.

"The baseline for article five is zero tolerance," Tomas said, reading from the protocol sheet. His voice caught on the word *tolerance*, as if the syllable itself had edges. "Any reading above zero requires immediate isolation of the affected citizen for recalibration."

The dripping continued. Marek watched a single drop form on the pipe above Tomas's station, swell, and fall. It struck the cable shaft with a sound like a small bell, and for an instant the lamps flickered—not the stutter of the ozone spike, but something softer, almost apologetic.

"Begin the fifth-article scan," the Inspector ordered. "We will proceed district by district. The outer sectors first."

Marek's pen hovered over the ledger. The unmarked channels pressed against his palm like a stone, and he thought of Ilse's map, of the rain's silver line shifting westward, of the parched streets waiting beyond the Ministry's roof.
