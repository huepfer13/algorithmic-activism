# Abschnitt 6: The Gauge Without a Hand

The pneumatic tube shuddered when Bramble pressed her paw to the speaking grille. Fine paper dust—gray as old ash—puffed from the relay contacts and coated her pads. She rubbed them together, feeling the grit. *Forty-seven, forty-eight.* The count lived in her claws still, a rhythm the auditors had left behind like a splinter.

"Blocked," Fenwick muttered, surfacing beside her. Mud streaked his flank. "The tube breathes, but no voice passes. Sable tried the upper reach. Her talons came back clean."

Sable dropped onto the dam's crown, wings mantling. "The Warden's roost is silent. The Ledger is blank. The gauge needle has not moved since the scream."

Pip stood at the water's edge, reed stem clenched between her teeth. She spat it out. "The shares. Who speaks them now?"

The question had no answer in the old way. The Warden's claw had held the pen; his eye had parsed the marks. Now the bark pages remembered nothing, and the intercom's whine had settled into a low, steady hum—like a heart beating behind a wall.

Bramble climbed the roost ladder. The rungs groaned under her weight. At the top, the Ledger lay open to the wind. She touched a blank page. No impression of claw, no ghost of ink. The erasure was absolute.

But beneath the Ledger's back cover, tucked into a slit in the bark, she found a second stack of pages. Smaller. Bound with reed fiber. Her claws turned them.

*Share of the beaver—three measures. Share of the otter—two measures. Share of the heron—one measure. Share of the mouse—one measure.*

The mouse. Pip. Written in the Warden's hand, but the ink was fresh. Still damp.

The intercom shrieked again—not a whine, a voice. Cracked and layered, as if many throats spoke at once: *"Calibration interrupted. Integrity parameter violated. Life-sign threshold: CRITICAL."*

The gauge needle jerked. Three measures. Two. One. One.

Pip's reed stem snapped between her teeth.
