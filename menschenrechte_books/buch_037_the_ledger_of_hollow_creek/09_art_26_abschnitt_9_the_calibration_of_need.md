# Abschnitt 9: The Calibration of Need

The relay cabinet's rhythm had not broken. Forty-seven beats. Forty-seven beats. Each pulse drove a drop of condensate from the raised floor's thermal drift, a steady *plick-plick-plick* onto cable ducts below. The sound echoed the hollow's older memory—silence pooling, lamps stuttering forty-seven, forty-seven, ozone coating every tongue.

Bramble's smaller ledger lay open on a flat stone, its reed-fiber binding still warm. The final page held fresh ink: not numbers, but names. Every field mouse. Every otter pup. Every heron fledgling. The Warden's great book counted measures; hers counted mouths.

"The audit measures depth," Fenwick said. He had not moved from the reeds. His sedge roots lay forgotten, the mud heavier than before. "But depth does not reach the burrow."

Sable's reflection still fractured at the reservoir's edge. "The gauge reads five measures. Yesterday it read four. The water drops regardless."

Pip stood at the lower tube's mouth. Her whiskers caught the carrier wave's hum—*expression parameter: UNRESTRICTED*. Something had changed in the static. The frequency carried new data packets, stripped of the Warden's authority stamps.

*"Access protocol: UNIVERSAL. Tier-one: MANDATORY. Tier-two: GENERAL. Tier-three: MERIT-BASED. Integrity check: LIFE-SUPPORT PRIORITY OVERRIDE."*

The words were not the Warden's layered voice. They were thinner. Older. Calibration code from before the ledger, before the dam, before the Warden's roost stood empty.

"The speaking grille," Bramble said. "It never stopped transmitting. We simply lacked the key."

Fenwick's head lifted. "The key was always here. In the mud. In the hunger. In the count of mouths versus measures."

The gauge needle spun wildly—five to three, three to six, six to two. The reservoir's surface trembled. Moonlight struck the water at a sharp angle, illuminating the intake grate where the lower tube breathed.

Pip's small paw pressed against the metal. "The audit begins at moonrise. The Warden's ledger will show what it shows."

"But the cabinet's ledger," Sable said, beak clicking, "shows what *is*."

The discharge lamps flared. The carrier wave strengthened. And from the lower tube, something rose—not water, not air, but light. A projection mapping the valley entire: every burrow, every nest, every measure of need against every measure of flow.
