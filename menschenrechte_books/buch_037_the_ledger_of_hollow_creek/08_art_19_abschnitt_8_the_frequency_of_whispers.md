# Abschnitt 8: The Frequency of Whispers

The discharge lamps above the relay cabinet stuttered at forty-seven beats per second, their sickly yellow pulse painting the mud flats in warning colors. Bramble pressed her nose to the intake grate, the ozone bite sharp enough to make her eyes water. The smell clung to the metal like frost to stone.

"The audit count begins at moonrise," Fenwick said. He had gathered the scattered sedge roots again, but his paws moved slower now, as if the mud had grown heavier. "They will measure what we have, not what we need."

Sable stood on one leg at the reservoir's edge, her reflection fractured by the trembling light. "The Warden's gauge shows four measures where three stood before. But the water drops. The ledger and the land disagree."

Pip emerged from the reeds, her fur damp with more than reservoir water. She carried no roots, no offerings. Her whiskers still twitched with the memory of whispers—how the field mice had spoken of a different accounting, one where the smudge beside their names might grow into something a body could survive on. The whispers had changed pitch in recent days. Less fear. More calculation.

"The lower tube," Pip said. "It carries more than audit notices now."

Fenwick's head turned sharply. "What passes through the lower tube is not our concern. The Warden reads what the Warden reads."

"The Warden's roost sits empty," Bramble said. The smaller ledger burned against her pads, its reed-fiber binding warm as fresh kill. "And the speaking grille coughs static. Who calibrates the gauge when the calibrator cannot be found?"

Sable's beak clicked once. "The relay cabinet. The discharge lamps. Something still speaks through them."

They gathered at the metal box, its surface hot with the stutter of forty-seven pulses. The ozone smell intensified, sharp enough to taste. Bramble pressed her ear to the casing. Words emerged between the static bursts—not the Warden's layered voice, but something thinner. A frequency stripped of authority.

*"Expression parameter: UNRESTRICTED. Interference threshold: NULL. Cross-boundary transmission: ACTIVE."*

Pip's small body trembled. "The whispers. They come from here."

Fenwick backed away, sedge roots scattering. "We should not hear this. The ledger—"

"The ledger says nothing about what passes through the lower tube," Bramble said. The needle on the gauge above them spun—four to five, five to four. The reservoir's surface dropped another hand's breadth. "The ledger only counts what the Warden chooses to see."

The discharge lamps flared once, twice, then steadied at their frantic rhythm. From the relay cabinet's speaking grille, a new sound emerged: not static, not voice, but something between—a carrier wave searching for receivers.

Sable lifted her head toward the darkening sky. "Moonrise approaches. The audit will begin."

"And the gauge will show what it shows," Bramble said. She opened the smaller ledger to its final page, where fresh ink still glistened. "Unless someone recalibrates the measure."

The lamps stuttered their forty-seven beats. The ozone thickened. And somewhere in the reeds, the whispers found new throats to speak through.
