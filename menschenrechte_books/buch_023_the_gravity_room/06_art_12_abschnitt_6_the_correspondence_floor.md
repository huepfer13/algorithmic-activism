# Abschnitt 6: The Correspondence Floor

Tobias knelt at the access panel, cutters biting through the detention circuit's main lead. The sheath parted with a sigh of severed insulation. Behind the board, the pneumatic tube that fed pressure telemetry to the lower deck sat pinched flat—crushed by a bracket that had walked loose during the last boost. Fine paper dust, pale as bone ash, coated the relay contacts where the tube's abrasion had ground the insulation into silt.

Tobias: "Tube's dead. Telemetry's been flying blind since the drift started."

Mirela didn't look up from the foil strip. Her thumb traced the same silver edge she'd smoothed in the drill, the lamp's flicker painting ozone into the air—same smell, same stutter. The memory sat in her fingers like a bruise.

Mirela: "Then the lower deck has no voice in the vent cycle. Article 12 becomes a hardware fault: no correspondence, no consent."

Oskar: "It's a crushed tube. Not a rights violation."

Vera: "It's both. If the recycler drops and the lower deck can't signal distress, the system decides for them. That's arbitrary interference with correspondence—technical definition."

Oskar's jaw tightened. The foil strip threw another hard flicker across his face.

Oskar: "We patch the tube. Restore the channel. That's the fix, not a constitutional amendment."

Tobias: "Patch takes twenty minutes we don't have. The bracket's welded to the frame. I'd need a torch and a new tube section."

Mirela: "Then we calibrate around it. The intercom carries voice—set a software floor: any deck can trigger vent review by speaking the override phrase. No sensor input required. Correspondence restored by protocol."

Vera: "And if the intercom fails? The damping fields are degrading."

Mirela: "Then we add a physical fallback. The hatch panel has a mechanical striker—three raps, pause, three raps. Morse for SOS. The recorder logs it. The vent logic reads the log. No tube, no intercom, no excuse."

Tobias wiped paper dust from the relay contacts, the gray smear darkening his fingertip.

Tobias: "Three raps. The recorder's on the same bus as the detention lock we just cut."

Vera: "Isolate the bus. Run a dedicated line from the striker to the vent controller. Direct wire. No shared circuitry."

Oskar: "That's a bypass of the central processor. Certification void."

Mirela: "Certification voided when the tube crushed. We're writing the floor now. Life support overrides procedure—your words, Oskar. Article 12 becomes a parameter: correspondence requires at least one working channel, always. Hardware, software, mechanical. Redundancy is the right."

The foil strip curled another tenth of a millimeter. The clock ticked. Tobias stripped the cut wire ends, copper bright in the failing light.

Oskar: "Fine. Direct line. Three-rap protocol. Software floor at one point two liters. But the striker—"

A new sound cut through the module. Not the intercom's whine. A low, rhythmic thumping from the hatch itself. Three raps. Pause. Three raps. Repeating.
