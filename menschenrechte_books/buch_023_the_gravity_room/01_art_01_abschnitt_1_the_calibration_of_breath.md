# Abschnitt 1: The Calibration of Breath

Mirela's foil strip caught the emergency light and threw a small hard glare across Oskar's face. He blinked, and for a moment the salesman's mask slipped — underneath was something tired and counting.

"Say it," she repeated. "All four. The rationing motion. Or the clock keeps its own counsel."

Vera had already turned back to the recycler housing. She ran a thumb along the intake seam and came away with a grey film. "Paper dust," she said. "Fine as flour. The pneumatic line to the relay bank is blocked — someone's packing slip shredded itself into the system before we ever got here." She wiped her thumb on her thigh. "That's why the gauge stutters. It's not lying. It's choking."

"Can you clear it?" Tobias asked, still folded on the deck.

"I can clear it. It buys us minutes, not air." Vera looked at the clock. "Eighty-one. We vote now or we breathe by accident."

Oskar pulled himself down along the rail until his boots found the deck. "Fine. I'll say it. Air by breath. Water by measure. Decisions by four voices." He paused, and the pause had teeth in it. "But I want the calibration written down. All four of us equal in dignity and rights — that's not a slogan in here, it's a *tolerance band*. If one voice drifts, the whole assembly fails. So we log every decision. Timestamped. Signed. That's my condition."

"Conditions are not the hatch's language," Mirela said.

"Neither is trust. Log it or I don't speak my fragment."

Vera straightened. "Logged. Mirela, you keep the record. Tobias, you speak last on every motion — that's your seat now, not your apology."

Tobias's jaw worked. "I said I'd go last because I didn't want to be the reason. That's not the same as being *given* the last word."

"Then take it or leave the seat empty," Vera said. "The hatch doesn't care about your reasons. It cares about your voice."

He was quiet. Then: "I'll take it."

Mirela pressed her foil flat against the bulkhead and began, with the edge of her nail, to scratch a column of marks. "Motion one. Rationing. Vera: aye. Oskar: aye, provisional. Tobias—"

"Aye," Tobias said, and it cost him something to say it before the others had finished.

Mirela scratched. "Mirela: aye. Motion carries. Four voices." She looked up. "Now we say the code. Aloud. Together. On three."

Oskar's fingers found his jacket seam one last time, open, close. "On three."

The module groaned beneath them, and the clock, which had been silent, clicked over to eighty.

"One," Mirela said. "Two—"

The recycler coughed. A puff of grey dust spat from the intake and hung in the air between them like a question nobody had asked yet.
