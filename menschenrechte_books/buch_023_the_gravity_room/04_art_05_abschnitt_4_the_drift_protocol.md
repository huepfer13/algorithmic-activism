# Abschnitt 4: The Drift Protocol

The humid chill settled in the lower deck, condensate tracing slow paths along the cable trays before dropping with a soft, regular plink.

Vera: "The recycler’s flow is down to 1.8 liters per hour. If we keep the variance floor at two, we’re already below threshold."

Oskar: "Then the pause protocol kicks in for everyone. We can’t afford a forced silence when the clock’s ticking."

Mirela: "Article 5 says we cannot subject anyone to cruel or degrading treatment. A forced pause that risks hypoxia could be read as degrading."

Tobias: "But the article is a legal shield, not a survival metric. We need a hard limit on stress, not on speech."

Vera: "Let’s treat Article 5 as a sensor: any rise in heart‑rate variability beyond fifteen percent triggers an automatic oxygen boost, bypassing the vote."

Oskar: "That turns a rights clause into a feedback loop. It’s smart, but who sets the sensor?"

Mirela: "We already have the patch from the foil strip. Remember how it caught the emergency light and threw a hard glare across my face? The metal’s resistance changes with temperature; we can calibrate it to read the cabin’s thermal drift."

Tobias: "The foil’s edge is curling again, a sign the alloy is fatiguing. If we bind it to the recycler’s inlet, its deformation will signal when the humidity spikes past the safe point."

Vera: "So the foil becomes a strain gauge for Article 5. When it bends past two millimeters, the system opens a vent, giving us extra air without a vote."

Oskar: "And if the vent fails?"

Mirela: "Then the pause protocol stays, but the Article 5 boost guarantees we never cross into cruel territory—life support overrides the variance count."

A brief silence, broken only by the rhythmic drip from the tray onto the cables, a metronome marking the seconds left before re-entry.

Vera: "All in favor of linking the foil strain gauge to the Article 5 boost?"

Oskar: "Aye."

Mirela: "Aye."

Tobias: "Aye."

Vera: "Motion carries. Four voices."

The lamp above the hatch flickered once, then steadied, casting a steady glow on the foil strip now pressed flat against the console, its surface catching the light and throwing a thin line across Oskar’s cheek—just as it had in the emergency flash, when his salesman’s mask slipped and revealed the tired count beneath.
