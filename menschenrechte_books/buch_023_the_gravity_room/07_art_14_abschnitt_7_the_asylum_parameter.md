# Abschnitt 7: The Asylum Parameter

The thumping didn't stop. Three raps. Pause. Three raps. The hatch's steel skin transmitted each impact as a vibration through the deck plates, up the stanchions, into the foil strip still curling in Mirela's grip.

Oskar didn't move toward the hatch. His eyes stayed on the recycler's pressure gauge, the needle trembling at 1.3 liters.

Oskar: "We don't open for ghosts. The code fragments—"

Mirela: "Are useless if the knocker dies on the other side. Article 14 as parameter: any sealed module broadcasting distress gains automatic hatch consideration. Persecution by vacuum counts."

Vera crossed to the intercom panel, slapped the talk key.

Vera: "Identify. State module and emergency."

Static. Then a voice, thin and wet with carbon dioxide: *Sector 4, Habitat Ring C. Recycler catastrophic. Two adults, one child. Oxygen at zero-point-four. We heard your three-rap protocol on the shared bus. We're requesting—*

Tobias: "Asylum. The word they didn't say."

The central registry's announcement echoed in memory—revisions coming faster than they could file. Oskar's fingers twitched toward the protocol binder, already thick with emergency addenda.

Oskar: "Four fragments. Four voices. We agree or we don't. Opening costs us three minutes of reserve. The child changes nothing."

Mirela: "The child changes the floor. Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence. That's not negotiation. That's the calibration floor."

Vera: "Three minutes. We recalibrate the vent cycle to one-point-zero. Share the hit equally. Four ways."

Tobias: "The knocker's module—Ring C—has no code fragments. They can't seal the hatch behind them. We'd be five in a four-person lifeboat."

The thumping paused. A single rap. Then two. A question.

Mirela's thumb found the foil's edge again. The ozone smell sharpened.

Mirela: "Then we write the exception. Asylum parameter: incoming souls receive temporary fragment—virtual, time-limited, revocable at re-entry. The hatch logic accepts five voices for the cycle duration. We file the revision now."

Oskar: "Central Registry audit in twelve minutes. They'll flag a virtual fragment as unauthorized architecture."

Vera: "Let them flag it. We'll be in the atmosphere by then. Or we won't. File it anyway."

She slammed the protocol binder open. The pages whispered—accelerated filing, nervous sector, ink bleeding into margins.

Tobias: "Direct wire from striker to vent controller. Add a second channel: asylum override. Five voices, one cycle. I'll splice it in ninety seconds."

The hatch thumped again. Three raps. Pause. Three raps.

Answering.
