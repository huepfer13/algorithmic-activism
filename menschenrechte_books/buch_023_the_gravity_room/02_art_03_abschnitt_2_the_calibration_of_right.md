# Abschnitt 2: The Calibration of Right

The foil strip still clung to Mirela’s fingertip, a thin silver thread that had once flung a hard glare across Oskar’s face. He remembered the blink, the moment the salesman’s mask slipped — underneath something tired and counting — and now the same metal caught the emergency light again, throwing a faint line across the bulkhead as if the module itself were trying to write a reminder.

“Registry just pinged,” Vera said, her voice low enough that the recycler’s hum swallowed the edges. “Unexpected revision. They’re auditing the O₂ thresholds. Protocol says we have to file the current calibration within ten minutes, or they’ll trigger a forced re‑entry sequence.”

Oskar’s jaw tightened. “So we’re not just negotiating air; we’re logging to satisfy a bureaucratic tremor.” He glanced at the column of scratches Mirela had made, each mark a vote, each timestamp a heartbeat.

Mirela lifted the foil, angled it toward the dim panel. “If we treat Article 3 as a setting — life, liberty, security — we can set the minimum safe O₂ at 19.5 % and tie the alarm to a liberty margin: any drop below that triggers a mandatory pause in decision‑making until the recycler recovers. It’s a technical guard, not a slogan.”

Tobias shifted his weight, the metal deck cold through his boots. “And the security margin? We could lock the hatch manual override if the pressure differential spikes beyond 0.2 psi, forcing a pause on all non‑essential power draws. That would buy us time without sacrificing the vote.”

Vera ran a thumb over the intake seam again, feeling the residual grit. “The paper dust is still there. If we clear it now, we regain roughly twelve minutes of buffer, but we risk disturbing the settled particles and causing a secondary clog. The calibration must account for that variable.”

Mirela’s nail scratched a new line beside the previous column. “Motion two. O₂ floor set at 19.5 % — Article 3 as technical parameter. Liberty alarm engaged at 19.0 %. Security lock‑out at 0.2 psi differential. All in favor?”

“Aye,” Oskar said, his voice edged with the awareness that every word now carried the weight of an impending audit.

“Aye,” Vera replied, already tapping the recycler’s control to initiate a brief purge.

Tobias hesitated, then nodded. “Aye.”

Mirela marked the tally. “Motion carries. Four voices.”

The recycler coughed, a puff of grey dust spiraling upward, catching the emergency light and turning it into a trembling veil. Somewhere beyond the hull, the central registry’s announcement echoed again, a reminder that revisions were coming faster than they could file.

Mirela glanced at Oskar, recalling the foil’s glare, the slip of his mask, the tired counting beneath. She felt the module’s groan sync with the ticking clock, each second a calibration of breath and bureaucracy.
