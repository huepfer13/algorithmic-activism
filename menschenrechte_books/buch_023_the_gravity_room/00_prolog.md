# The Gravity Room

The hatch sealed with a sound like a held breath.

Vera was first to speak, because Vera was always first to speak. "Nobody touch the panel." She had a commander's voice and a mechanic's hands, and those hands were already flat against the bulkhead, reading its cold. "That clock is counting down from ninety minutes. I did not set it. Did any of you set it?"

Oskar laughed — a short, dry bark that did not belong in a room with failing air. He floated a half-meter off the deck, one boot hooked under a rail, and he did not look at the clock. He looked at the water line. "I set nothing. I sell things. Right now I would like to sell someone the idea that we do not panic." His fingers found the seam of his jacket, opened it, closed it. Open, close. "The recycler is at forty-one percent. I read the gauge before you did, Commander."

"Don't call me that."

"Then don't act like it."

Mirela had not moved from the corner. She held her fragment of the code — a strip of foil no longer than a finger — and she held it the way you hold a photograph of someone dead. "The code needs all four," she said, quietly, to no one. "It says so. Aloud. Together. So we are already a government. We have been a government for six minutes and we have not yet agreed on a single thing."

Tobias sat on the deck with his knees drawn up, which was difficult in near-weightlessness and therefore deliberate. "I'll go last," he said. "On the vote. Whatever we vote on. I'll go last, I don't want to be the reason." He had said this before, in other rooms, to other people, and it had never once been true.

Vera turned from the bulkhead. "Then here is the first thing we agree on. Air is rationed by breath, not by rank. Water by measure, not by thirst. Decisions by all four voices or not at all." She looked at Oskar. "And no one sells anything in this room."

Oskar's fingers stopped on the seam. "Agreed. Provisionally."

"Provisionally is not a word the hatch accepts."

The module shuddered — a long, low grind that traveled up through the deck and into their teeth. The clock did not change. Outside, unseen, the curve of something enormous and patient began to lean toward them.

Mirela unfolded her foil. "Then say it. All four. Now."
