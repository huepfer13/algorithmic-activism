# Abschnitt 5: The Detention Threshold

The foil strip had begun to curl again, its silver edge lifting from the console like a tongue testing the air. Mirela pressed it flat with her thumb, feeling the alloy's resistance warm against her skin.

Mirela: "It's reading point three millimeters already. The cabin's thermal drift is accelerating."

Oskar: "Then we're burning margin we don't have. The vent cycle needs a trigger threshold that won't fire on every breath."

Tobias: "The problem isn't the trigger. It's the lockout protocol. If the system reads a variance spike during the boost, it defaults to detention mode—seals the lower deck until manual override."

Vera: "That's the arbitrary detention clause. Article 9. No one gets locked out of the air supply because a sensor misread a shiver."

Oskar: "Article 9 is about arrest and exile, not about a safety interlock. We're not detaining anyone—we're containing a fault."

Mirela: "Tell that to whoever's on the wrong side of the seal when the clock hits zero. A locked hatch is a locked hatch, whatever you call it."

The intercom above the hatch emitted a high, thin whine—a damping field bleeding through the circuit, sharp enough to make Tobias flinch and lose his place in the count he'd been tracking on his fingers.

Tobias: "That's the third interference spike this hour. The damping fields are degrading."

Vera: "Then we hardwire the override. No detention mode without a two-key physical release—one here, one at the hatch."

Oskar: "Two keys means two people. That's a quorum, not a safeguard."

Mirela: "It's a calibration. Article 9 becomes a parameter: detention requires consensus, not sensor tripline."

Oskar's jaw worked. The foil strip caught the stuttering light and threw a hard flicker across his cheek—the same silver flash from the emergency drill, when his mask had slipped and shown the tired count beneath.

Oskar: "Fine. But if the recycler drops below one liter, consensus goes to hell. We need an automatic bypass."

Vera: "Then we set the floor at one point two. Below that, the vent opens regardless of vote—life support overrides procedure."

Tobias: "And the detention lock?"

Vera: "Disabled. Permanently. We cut the circuit now, before the next spike."

Mirela: "All in favor?"

Oskar: "Aye."

Tobias: "Aye."

Mirela: "Aye."

Vera: "Motion carries. Four voices. Tobias, get the cutters."

Tobias crossed to the tool locker, his fingers already numb from the cold. The intercom whined again, higher this time, and the foil strip twitched against the console like something alive.

Vera: "Cut it clean. No residual charge."

Tobias: "On it. Give me thirty seconds."

The clock above the hatch ticked past the next marker. The foil curled another half-millimeter.
