# The Ledger of Ash Harbour

**Prologue: The Cordon Year, 1847**

*Entry the First — 3rd of March*

The fever came ashore with the herring fleet, and by the last week of February the provost's tribunal had sealed the harbour gates. I record this on the page issued to me at the cordon post: Ledger 4471, Maren Voss, fishwife, East Quay. My movements, my opinions, my very breath are to be entered here under penalty of exile to the salt flats. I write small, as instructed. I write nothing that matters.

*Entry the Second — 9th of March*

Rations cut again. The grain store at Wexford Row is watched day and night, and the queue winds past the customs house where the tribunal sits in judgment over every complaint. Corvin Haldt, the provost's clerk, counts the pages at each dusk inspection. He has a habit of wetting his thumb before he turns a leaf, and he reads each ledger as though it were a confession. Perhaps it is.

*Entry the Third — 14th of March*

Elias Thorne, the printer, came to the quay today under pretense of buying mackerel. He said little. But when he passed me my change, a slip of paper lay beneath the coins: *The margins are ours.* I burned it in the stove before the inspector's knock. Sister Agnes, who tends the fever ward at St. Brannock's, tells me the tribunal has begun exiling whole households on the evidence of a single careless line. She keeps her own ledger immaculate. She says nothing of what she thinks.

*Entry the Fourth — 21st of March*

The Quill came to me at the fish shed before dawn. I do not know his face; he wears the hood of the salt-carriers and speaks in a whisper worn smooth by use. He showed me how the forbidden words are written — not in the body of the page, where Haldt's thumb will fall, but in the margins, in the thin white space where the tribunal's rule does not reach. *Ash Harbour remembers,* he wrote. *Ash Harbour endures.* He asked if I would carry the words to the east gate. I said I would.

*Entry the Fifth — 28th of March*

Elias was taken at noon. They found a margin in his ledger — three words only — and Haldt read them aloud before the tribunal as though he were reading a death sentence. Exile to the salt flats, at the next tide. Sister Agnes wept in the ward, but she wrote nothing of it. The Quill has not come again. I keep my page clean and my margins ready.

*Entry the Sixth — 30th of March*

Grain stores will not last the month. The cordon tightens. And tonight, beneath the floorboard of the fish shed, I found a second ledger page — blank, unnumbered, waiting.

*The cordon holds; the fever spreads; the margins fill.*
