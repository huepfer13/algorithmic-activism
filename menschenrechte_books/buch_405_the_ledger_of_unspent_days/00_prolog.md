# The Ledger of Unspent Days

**Prologue: Demands of the Uncounted**

1. We begin with the arithmetic. The city of Quorum issues each resident a finite allotment of counted days, drawn from a central Ledger, enforced by turnstiles that open only for the properly debited, ration clocks that tick down the hours of labor and rest, and auditors who roam the corridors in gray coats with their ledgers chained to their wrists. This is the first fact of our lives: we are born into a balance we did not choose and cannot audit.

2. We demand the right to read our own accounts. Marisol Vane, who keeps the records office on the seventh tier and has spent eleven years reconciling the city's books, has found a discrepancy. The Ledger is reclaiming days from citizens who have committed no offense. The debits appear in the night, silent as frost, and the citizens wake to find their allotments shortened. Marisol cannot explain it. She has tried. The apparatus does not answer.

3. We demand that the arithmetic be challenged in the only place where challenge is possible. Oren Tabb, who runs the turnstile at the eastern gate and knows the weight of every worker who passes through, has begun keeping his own count. He marks the faces that no longer appear. Pell, who sweeps the corridors and hears what the auditors say when they think no one is listening, has heard them whisper of a correction that is not a correction but a subtraction. Sister Agatha Rill, who tends the sick in the lower wards, has watched patients die on days that were not theirs to die on. The Auditor, who answers to no one and carries a ledger that is not chained but sewn into the lining of their coat, has been seen at the records office door.

4. We demand the right to ask the question the apparatus cannot answer: who authorizes the reclamation of days from those who have committed no offense? The Ledger is not a god. It is a book. Books have authors. Authors have hands. Hands can be stayed.

5. We demand that the protection of life and physical integrity take precedence over the integrity of the count. A day reclaimed is a breath not taken. A breath not taken is a body in the street. The auditors will tell you the arithmetic is neutral. The arithmetic is not neutral. The arithmetic is a knife.

6. We demand that the records office remain open. We demand that the turnstiles be audited. We demand that the gray coats answer for the days they have taken. We demand that the question be asked until the apparatus answers or breaks.

— *Sealed by the unspent, in the name of the counted, on the day the Ledger began to subtract.*
