# The Ledger of Ash Harbour

**Prologue: The First Entry**

*Anno 17—, the ninth day of the Quarantine, as recorded by my hand, Maren Holt, clerk of the third district.*

---

On the ninth day I began to keep this account, for the official ledger of the Harbourmaster records only what the Harbourmaster wishes remembered. I write in secret, by a candle stub, in the loft above the chandlery where my brother Elias hides me.

The plague ship *Mercy of Tides* came to anchor on the first day, her crew already dead at their posts. By nightfall the Harbourmaster had sealed every gate. The walled city of Ash Harbour became a cage of five districts, each behind armed checkpoints, each citizen's worth entered into the great civic ledger by the clerks of the Harbour Office. Tobias Renn, the Harbourmaster's chief clerk, decides who eats and who is forgotten. He has decided that my name is worth little.

Water is rationed by written permit. A family of four receives one permit; a widow receives half. Sister Cora, who tends the sick at the Chapel of the Drowned Bell, has begun forging permits for those the ledger has struck out. "They will hang me," she told me calmly, "but they will hang me for the right sin."

Elias, my brother, is a fisherman who refused to surrender his boat to the Harbourmaster's requisition. He hides now, and I feed him from my own ration. This is the first of my quarrels with the ledger: my brother's life is not written in it, and so the city believes he does not exist.

On the seventh day, a child died at the eastern checkpoint because her mother's permit had been mislaid. The guards would not open the gate to fetch the physician. I watched from the chandlery window. I said nothing. This silence I carry like a stone.

The Harbourmaster walks the walls each evening at dusk, counting the ships that no longer come. He has not spoken to any citizen since the first day. His clerks speak for him. Tobias Renn speaks loudest.

On the eighth day, Sister Cora was summoned to the Harbour Office and questioned for three hours. She returned with her hands shaking and would not say what was asked. That night she burned her forged permits and began, instead, to copy the ledger in secret. "If they will not write us," she said, "we will write ourselves."

I have begun to copy it also. This is my entry, and my quarrel, and my stone.

---

*Ninth day of the Quarantine. Water permit denied to the household of Holt. Cause: insufficient civic worth. Recorded by T. Renn.*
