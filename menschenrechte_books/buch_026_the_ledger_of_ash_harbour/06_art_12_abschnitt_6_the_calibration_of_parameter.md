# Abschnitt 6: The Calibration of Parameter Twelve

*Anno 17—, the thirteenth day of the Quarantine, the eleventh hour and seventeen minutes.*

---

The ladder rungs were cold iron against my palms. I descended into the smell of wet brick and old oil, the permit heavy in my coat pocket. Above, the intercom whined again — louder this time, as if the damping fields had shifted — and Tobias's voice followed me down: "The pneumatic tube at junction four. Paper dust on the relays. It won't carry your report back."

I heard Elias mutter something about calibration windows. The hatch cover settled shut behind me, cutting the sound to a muffled hum.

The service tunnel ran straight beneath the holding yard's western wall. Water dripped from a cracked main, hitting stone with metronome regularity. My boots found a grate set into the floor — the observation port the engineers used when the pumps failed. Through the bars, I saw them.

Thirty-seven figures in the yard. Chained to the corroded rings. The boy from the smoke incident lay nearest the wall, his breath visible in the cold. A clerk in Harbourmaster livery walked the perimeter with a ledger, marking something against each name. His pen scratched. Scratched. Stopped.

Parameter Twelve. *Unlawful Interference*. The ledger architecture defined it as: no subject's correspondence, dwelling, or person accessed without verified warrant. The holding yard's design — open bars, no screens, the clerk's eyes on every movement, every whispered word between prisoners — constituted a continuous parameter breach. Six days of it.

I pulled the permit. The watermark gull stared back, wings folded.

The grate's hinges screamed when I lifted it. The clerk looked up. His face was vellum-pale in the lamp-light.
