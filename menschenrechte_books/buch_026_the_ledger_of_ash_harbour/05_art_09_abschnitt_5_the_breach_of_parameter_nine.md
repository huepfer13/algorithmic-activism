# Abschnitt 5: The Breach of Parameter Nine

*Anno 17—, the thirteenth day of the Quarantine, the eleventh hour.*

---

The intercom shrieked. A thin, mosquito whine — the damping fields bleeding through the speaker grilles, frequency drift in the copper windings. My jaw clenched. The sound always found the gap between teeth.

Tobias Renn was already moving. Not toward the checkpoint. Toward the service hatch behind the water-maintenance panel, the one the Harbourmaster's engineers used when the pumps failed. His fingers found the catch without light.

"Parameter Nine," he said. "The ledger architecture names it *Unlawful Retention*. No subject held beyond the calibration window without verified cause. The holding yard exceeds the window by six days."

"Six days," Elias repeated. He stood on the lowest loft rung, one hand on the rail. "The boy. The chains. The smoke."

"Parameter breach triggers automatic audit," Tobias said. The hatch gave. Cold air rolled out, tasting of rust and tide. "But the audit requires a witness of standing inside the perimeter. The clerk's pen must touch the vellum *there*."

The whine spiked — a sharp needle in the eardrum — then settled back to its drone.

I remembered the thirteenth day as recorded by my hand, Maren Holt, clerk of the third district. The ink still wet on that entry. The same hand now reaching for the hatch ladder.

"Then I go," I said.

Tobias looked up. The vellum-coloured eyes held no surprise.

"The permit," he said. He withdrew a folded slip from his coat — not the crossed permit, another, heavier stock, Harbourmaster watermark. "Field verification. Section twelve, paragraph three, sub-clause seven. *The witness enters at own risk. The ledger accepts no liability for integrity of person.*"

He held it out. The intercom whined again, louder this time, as if the damping fields had shifted.

My fingers closed on the paper. The watermark caught the lamp-light: a gull, wings folded, beak closed.
