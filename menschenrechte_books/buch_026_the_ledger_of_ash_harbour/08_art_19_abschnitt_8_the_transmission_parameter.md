# Abschnitt 8: The Transmission Parameter

*Anno 17—, the thirteenth day of the Quarantine, the eleventh hour and seventeen minutes.*

---

The flicker came first — 47 Hz, measured by the relay counter at junction four. Discharge lamps stuttered across the holding yard, casting the clerk's face in strobe-light slices. Ozone bit at the back of the throat, sharp as the sulphur matches Tobias used to test the pneumatic seals. *Anno 17—, the thirteenth day of the Quarantine, the eleventh hour and seventeen minutes.* The archive reference surfaced unbidden: the same timestamp logged in Sector Six when the western perimeter breached and the Harbourmaster's welders worked through the night.

"Parameter Nineteen," the clerk said. His pen hovered over the ledger's fresh folio. "Unverified transmission. The boy — smoke incident — claims message carriage beyond the outer wall. No permit. No sponsor. No calibration."

Elias's cough tore through the yard's acoustics. Sister Cora's habit brushed the chain linking the boy's wrist to the grating. Her fingers, I noted, were raw at the knuckles — lye burns from the water rationing vats.

"Central Registry audit," I said. "Two hours. All sectors." The words carried the tunnel's resonance. "Parameter Nineteen calibration suspended pending review. Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence."

The clerk's throat worked. His eyes flicked to the pneumatic tube at junction four. To the paper dust on the relays. To the boy, whose breath had stopped being visible.

"Accelerated filing," he said. "Parameter Nineteen... reclassified. Transmission vector: unverified. Carrier: unverified. Content —"

The lamps died. Total dark. The relay counter at junction four froze at 47.
