# Abschnitt 4: The Calibration of Silence

*Anno 17—, the thirteenth day of the Quarantine, continued.*

---

At the ninth hour the clerk's pen hovered over the vellum.

The lamps stuttered at their forty-seven beats. From the subfloor came the rhythmic dripping — condensation on the cable shafts, thermal drift in the crawl-space — each drop striking stone with the regularity of a metronome. I had heard it since the quarantine began, a background pulse beneath the ledger-work. Today it sounded like counting.

"I have a petition," I said again. "Two free householders. Myself and Elias Ward."

The clerk did not look up. "Petitions require signatures witnessed at the central registry. The registry is sealed."

"Then witness them here," I said. "The permit system allows field verification when checkpoints are impassable. Section twelve, paragraph three."

Tobias Renn's hand remained at the margin of the permit before him. The small mark beside the unread name had not moved. I remembered the ninth hour three days past — the chandlery counter, his small precise hands sorting permits, not looking up when I approached. The same stillness now.

"Section twelve," the clerk said. "Paragraph three. The *Calibration Clause*."

He turned the vellum page. The script was dense, abbreviated, written in a hand that had not trembled in decades.

"Calibration requires two witnesses of standing," he read. "And a third parameter: *No subject shall be held in conditions that degrade the integrity of the record.*"

The words were not law. They were specification. A technical tolerance built into the ledger's architecture.

"The holding yard," Elias said from the loft stairs. His voice carried the damp cold. "The smoke. The chains. The boy is nine."

"Parameter breach," the clerk said. His pen did not move. "If verified, the audit halts pending correction. The generator will be repaired. The lamps will stabilize."

"Then verify it," I said.

He looked at me then — first time. His eyes were the colour of the vellum.

"Verification requires entry," he said. "The holding yard is beyond the second checkpoint. The guards do not admit clerks without Harbourmaster signature."

"Then we go without admission," Tobias said quietly.

His hand finally left the margin. The mark beside the name was a cross, small and precise, in ink that had not been there three days ago.
