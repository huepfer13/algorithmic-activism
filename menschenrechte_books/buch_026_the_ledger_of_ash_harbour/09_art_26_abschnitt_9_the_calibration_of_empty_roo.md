# Abschnitt 9: The Calibration of Empty Rooms

*Anno 17—, the fourteenth day of the Quarantine, the sixth hour and forty minutes.*

---

The dark lasted eleven minutes by Tobias's pocket watch. When the discharge lamps coughed back to life, the clerk had vanished — his stool overturned, his pen still rolling across the ledger's open folio. The boy's chain hung slack against the grating. Sister Cora's raw knuckles pressed flat against the damp stone wall, feeling for the pulse of the relay counter.

"Forty-seven," she said. "Still frozen."

Elias had not moved from his crouch. His cough had quieted to a rasp, but his eyes tracked the junction box where the pneumatic tube had begun to tick — slow at first, then insistent, a metallic heartbeat in the wet cold. Condensate dripped from the ceiling joists in rhythm with it, each drop striking the cable shafts with a sound like a finger tapping a ledger's spine.

"The transmission," Elias said. "It's repeating."

I crossed to the relay counter. The tape unspooled in jerks, each advance marking a pulse of the 47 Hz carrier wave. But between the pulses, the tape held a second signal — weaker, buried in the noise floor. I traced the pattern with my finger. Numbers. Not coordinates. Ratios.

"Parameter Twenty-Six," I said. The words came flat, automatic. "Educational calibration. The old specification for compulsory instruction — elementary, technical, higher. The Registry used it to grade citizens before the ledger system replaced it."

Sister Cora turned from the wall. "They're transmitting curriculum codes?"

"They're transmitting *access* codes." I pulled the tape free and held it to the lamp. "Every district's ration permit is keyed to a literacy threshold. Parameter Twenty-Six required universal instruction. The Harbourmaster's clerks reclassified it as a privilege metric. Those who couldn't read the permits couldn't claim the water."

The words were not law. They were specification — the archive reference surfaced unbidden, a fragment from Chapter Four's marginalia. I had copied it myself during the first audit, before the quarantine sealed the districts.

Elias rose. His hand shook as he pointed at the tape. "The boy. He wasn't carrying a message. He was carrying a *key*."

The pneumatic tube ticked again. This time, the relay counter advanced — not to 47, but to 26. A new parameter. The lamps dimmed once, twice, then steadied. Somewhere above us, in the thermal drift of the crawlspace, water found a new path through the insulation and began to drip faster.

Sister Cora pressed her palm to the junction box. "They're recalibrating. If Parameter Twenty-Six goes live, every permit in the lower districts resets to zero. No literacy, no water."

The tape unspooled another inch. At its end, a single line of type:

**PROTECTION OF LIFE AND PHYSICAL INTEGRITY TAKES PRECEDENCE — OVERRIDE PENDING.**

Elias looked at me. "Whose integrity?"

The lamps flickered. The counter held at 26.
