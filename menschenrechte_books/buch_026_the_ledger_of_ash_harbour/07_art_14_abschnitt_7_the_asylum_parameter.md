# Abschnitt 7: The Asylum Parameter

*Anno 17—, the thirteenth day of the Quarantine, the eleventh hour and forty-three minutes.*

---

The clerk's pen did not resume. His fingers tightened on the ledger's edge — vellum-pale knuckles against vellum-pale skin. Behind him, the yard's far gate shuddered. New hinges, I noted. Installed after the eleventh day, when I had recorded the first breach of the western perimeter and the Harbourmaster had ordered the welds reinforced. *Anno 17—, the eleventh day of the Quarantine, as recorded by my hand, Maren Holt, clerk of the third district.* The memory surfaced unbidden: the smell of hot metal, the sound of Elias's cough echoing off the yard walls while Sister Cora bound the boy's wrist with strips of her own habit.

"Parameter Fourteen," the clerk said. Not a question. His voice carried the particular flatness of men who have memorized the architecture and fear its audit. "Unverified ingress. The boy — smoke incident — claims origin beyond the outer wall. No permit. No sponsor. No calibration."

The pneumatic tube at junction four. Tobias's warning. Paper dust on the relays.

I stepped from the grate, permit extended. The watermark gull caught the lamp-light. "Maren Holt. Third District. Witness to Parameter Twelve breach. Six days continuous. Holding yard design: open bars, no screens, clerk's eyes on every movement." My voice followed the tunnel's acoustics. "I require the calibration window for Parameter Fourteen. Asylum protocol. Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence."

The clerk's eyes flicked to the boy. To the chain. To the ledger.

"Central Registry audit announced," he said. "Unexpected. Two hours. All sectors." His throat worked. "Accelerated filing underway. Parameter Fourteen calibration... suspended pending review."

The boy's breath stopped being visible.
