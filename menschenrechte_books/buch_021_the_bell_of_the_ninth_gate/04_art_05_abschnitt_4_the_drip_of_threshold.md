# Abschnitt 4: The Drip of Threshold

The humid chill seeped from the interstitial floor, each bead of condensation striking the cable shafts with a steady, almost metronomic tick. Mira felt it on her neck as she paused beside the ninth bell, recalling how she had slipped through the maintenance shaft again, the paper dust still clinging to the relay contacts like ash on a grave, and how the Keeper’s thumb had traced a dark stain over the margin where his own name had been scraped away. That memory settled over her like a thin veil, reminding her that the ledger’s skin‑bound pages held more than numbers—they held the weight of every forbidden breath.

Corin’s bound arm throbbed, the flesh above the splint now a deep violet that pulsed with each faint tremor of the bell’s hum. Old Sable pressed her ear to the cold stone, listening for the subtle shift in the ozone‑laden air that signalled a change in the calibration cascade. Tobin, his hands steady now, watched the glyph that had formed on the relay wall—a hand, open, releasing—its lines trembling as the droplets fell.

The Keeper’s ledger lay open, the margin beside the water‑ration column still bleeding faintly where the Article‑4 parameter had been inverted. A new line, etched in a shaky hand, caught Mira’s eye: *Article‑5 limit — maximum permissible strain on Bell‑9 before degrading treatment.* It was not a proclamation but a technical guardrail, a numeric ceiling set to ensure that no soul would be subjected to torture or cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment. The protection of life and physical integrity, the ledger whispered, must outrank any arithmetic of passage.

Mira stepped forward, her palm flat against the bell’s cool bronze. “If we exceed the Article‑5 threshold, the metal will fatigue and the bell will crack,” she said, her voice low enough that the hidden listeners in the stone could not catch the exact words, though their ears twitched. Corin, despite his pain, lifted his good hand and placed it over hers, sharing the pressure. Old Sable nodded, her smile thin but resolute. Tobin adjusted the relay’s gain, turning a screw just enough to offset the rising humidity that threatened to push the strain beyond the limit.

The lamps flickered once more, casting sickly green shadows that danced across the dripping shafts. As the last drop fell, the glyph on the wall shifted: the open hand now clenched, fingers curled around a tiny, pulsing dot—an unseen weight pressing inward.

The bell’s hum deepened, a tone that seemed to ask whether the five would become the counterbalance or the breaking point. Somewhere below, the listeners pressed tighter to the rock, recording the silence that followed each droplet, waiting to see if the Article‑5 limit would hold or if the bell would demand a new payment.
