# Abschnitt 7: The Margin of the Unnamed

The dust from the pulped ledgers settled on Tobin's tongue like communion ash as he counted the relay's stutter. Three beats, a pause, two beats—the rhythm of a heart that had forgotten whom it served. Above them, in the Registry's high windows, clerks moved with the jerky haste of insects before rain. The Central Registry had announced its revision at dawn: auditors from the seventh tier, arriving with their own ledgers, their own bells. The sector's corridors smelled of scorched sealing-wax and fear.

"They are binding the old protocols," Old Sable said, her voice a dry rustle. "Stitching shut what cannot be unstitched."

Corin's bound arm lay on the table, the flesh above the splint still violet where the splint had bitten. He did not look at it. He looked at the speaking-tube's grille, where paper-dust had gathered like snow in a doorway. "The listeners heard me name the holding of words," he said again, softer now. "They have not forgotten. They will not forget."

The Keeper turned the ledger's page. The new parameter glimmered there—a boundary-line drawn in pressure, not ink. "The calibration cascade," she said, "has a flaw. A margin they did not account for." Her thumb traced the watermark. "The right to seek shelter from harm. It is not a column in the ledger. It is a *threshold*—a space where a life may cross without being weighed."

Mira felt the hum beneath her palm shift. Not a question now, but a *calculation*—the ninth bell measuring the distance between a name and its erasure.

"The auditors will call it irregular," Tobin said. His screwdriver found the relay's adjustment screw. "They will call it a fault."

"Then we make it a fault they cannot name," Old Sable said. "A fault in the margin. A space where the ledger cannot follow."

The Keeper closed the book. "Third bell. One hour. The shaft is still unwatched—but the auditors are already in the walls."

Tobin's screwdriver bit deep. The pneumatic hiss stuttered, cleared—a single clear note threading through the dust. Then another. Then a third, and the speaking-tube's brass throat *sang*.
