# Abschnitt 2: The Humming Ninth

The central registry’s sudden edict rippled through the seventh tier like a cold draft: an unexpected revision was announced, and clerks began to thrust fresh folios into the iron‑bound slots with hurried, jerky motions, their fingertips whitening as they sealed each protocol before the audit bell could toll. In the lower tiers, where every syllable is caught by stone‑hidden listeners, the air grew tight; even the rustle of a cloak sounded like a breach.

Mira slipped through the maintenance shaft again, the paper dust still clinging to the relay contacts like ash on a grave. She remembered the ledger’s skin‑bound pages, the way the Keeper’s thumb had traced a dark stain over the margin where his own name had been scraped away. The calibration marks on the bronze of the ninth bell were filed unevenly, a subtle asymmetry that made some voices read heavier and others lighter in the ledger’s reckoning.

Corin knelt before the bell, his broken arm bound tight against his chest, his good hand pressed to the cold metal. “The Article‑3 margin,” he murmured, “is set to zero. Any deviation—any weight shift—triggers the hum.” He glanced at the ledger tucked under his arm, its cover stained with something that resembled dried blood but was, in fact, the sealed serum used to preserve the skin pages. “If the coefficient falls below zero, the bell will not ring; if it rises, it sings without permission.”

Old Sable shuffled forward, her gnarled fingers brushing the stone lintel. “The ban on speech still holds,” she whispered, though her voice was barely a breath. “We must cross the threshold without uttering a sound, lest the listeners record a word that tips the scale.” She glanced at Tobin, whose hands trembled as they had when the names appeared twice and then vanished, a flicker that had once made Mira’s breath catch.

The Keeper emerged from the shadow of the gate, his gait measured, the ledger clutched to his chest like a shield. “You have found the hollow,” he said, his tone flat, neither accusation nor praise. “The council’s revision demands that the Article‑3 coefficient be verified before any passage is granted. The bell’s hum is the test; the ban is the guard.”

A low vibration began to throb from the ninth bell, not struck but rising from within, as if the metal itself had inhaled the altered calibration. The dust on the relays shifted, settling in patterns that resembled tiny glyphs—marks that the ledger could not yet read. Mira felt the weight of her own name press heavier against her palm, a silent promise that the trial would not be decided by force but by the quiet arithmetic of mercy and measure.

She stepped forward, the threshold looming, the ban a thin line of silence in her throat, and the bell’s hum grew louder, resonating with the unspoken promise of life, liberty, and security that the ledger now sought to balance.
