# Abschnitt 3: The Calibration of Chains

The maintenance shaft narrowed, then opened into the bell chamber where dust motes hung like a suspended sentence—Mira remembered that first ascent, the way the air had tasted of copper and old verdicts. Now the chamber stank of ozone, sharp and chemical, as the discharge lamps along the relay wall flickered at forty-seven beats each second, their stutter painting the bronze of the ninth bell in pulses of sickly green.

Corin had not moved from his kneel. His bound arm had begun to swell against its splint, the flesh above the wrappings turning the color of bruised plums. "The coefficient," he said, and stopped. His breath came in shallow measures, each exhale a small surrender.

Old Sable pressed her palm flat against the stone lintel. "The Article-4 parameter," she said, her voice the texture of worn rope, "was never zeroed. It was *inverted*."

The Keeper's ledger fell open in his hands as if of its own accord. There, in the margin beside a column of water-rations, a row of figures had been scraped and rewritten so many times the skin-page had grown translucent. The notation read: *Servitude Index — bound to Bell-9 calibration. Any passage granted below threshold requires offset. Offset requires—*

The line ended. The rest had been cut away with a blade fine enough to sever a hair.

"The council's revision," Mira said, "requires a body to balance the scale." She looked at Corin's arm, at the way his good hand still rested on the cold metal, and understood what the ledger could not say aloud: the ninth bell did not ring for passage. It rang for *payment*. And the payment was flesh, held in place, unable to leave—a servitude measured not in chains but in the arithmetic of who was permitted to walk away.

The lamps flickered again. Ozone burned her nostrils. Somewhere below, in the tiers where listeners crouched in the stone, a thousand ears pressed to the rock, recording every syllable that might tip the coefficient toward song.

Tobin stepped forward. His hands no longer trembled. "Then we do not cross," he said. "We *rewrite*."

The Keeper's head lifted. For the first time, his flat gaze held something like fear. "The ledger cannot be altered without a witness," he said. "And every witness is bound."

Old Sable smiled—a thin, terrible thing. "Then we become the witness," she said. "All five. The bell will not know whose weight to take."

The hum deepened. The dust on the relays shifted, forming a single glyph: a hand, open, releasing.

Corin lifted his broken arm from the bell. The metal screamed—a sound below hearing, a pressure in the teeth—and the Article-4 margin in the ledger began, slowly, to bleed.
