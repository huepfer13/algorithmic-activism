# Abschnitt 9: The Calibration of Unlearned Things

The relay clicked over. Condensation fell in a slow metronome from the false floor above, each drop striking cable sheathing with a sound like a needle finding a groove. Tobin remembered the whine in the Registry—the clenched hand glyph holding its pressure against the wall—and set his jaw against the new frequency climbing the speaking-tube.

Mira's palm burned where the ninth bell's hum met her skin. Not heat. *Information*. The bronze had begun to transmit in pulses that tasted of chalk dust and slate, of small hands tracing letters in the dark before the listeners came to count them. She understood then: the bell was not ringing. It was *reciting*.

"Elementary," Corin breathed, his violet fingers brushing the grille where the listener had breathed. "Fundamental. The ledger calls it Tier One Access. Compulsory until the twelfth year. Then the rationing begins."

Old Sable's staff struck the rung a third time. The sound did not echo. It *absorbed*.

"The ledger measures what is given," she said. "Not what is taken away. The difference between a lesson and a lock is whether the door opens from both sides."

Above them, the third bell tolled again. Two hours. The auditors' boots had paused at the Registry's heavy door—ink-stained fingers checking seals, consulting ledgers, calculating the arithmetic of denial.

Tobin's screwdriver bit into the next relay cabinet. The thermal drift had warped the contact plates; he shimmed them with a strip of copper from his belt, feeling the cold sweat of the shaft condense on his forearms. The speaking-tube's song dropped an octave, became a carrier wave for something older than protocol.

*Threshold. Trial. Ban.*

The ninth bell's bronze skin rippled again. In the green pulse-light, Mira saw new watermarks forming—pressure boundaries drawn not by the Keeper's hand but by the bell itself. A curriculum of mercy, written in frequencies the auditors' pens could not transcribe.

Her fingers found the speaking-tube's brass throat. The Keeper's song still vibrated there, a margin holding silence against the ledger's columns. Mira added her own note: a frequency she had learned from a child in the lower tiers, tracing letters on a slate the listeners had never numbered.

The relay cabinet sparked. Tobin swore, soft and precise.
