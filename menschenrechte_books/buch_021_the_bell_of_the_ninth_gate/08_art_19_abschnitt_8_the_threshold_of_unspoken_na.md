# Abschnitt 8: The Threshold of Unspoken Names

The shaft swallowed them in the order of their courage: Tobin first, his screwdriver bitten between teeth, then Mira with the hum warm against her palm, then Corin cradling his violet arm like a broken bird. Old Sable came last, her staff tapping a rhythm the ledger had never numbered. The Keeper did not follow. She remained at the speaking-tube, her fingers on the brass throat, feeding the song that was not a song but a *space*—a margin where the auditors' pens would find only silence.

Forty-seven beats each second. The discharge lamps along the relay wall stuttered at that frequency, painting the bronze of the ninth bell in pulses of sickly green. Mira tasted copper again, remembered the first ascent—how the air had held verdicts, how dust motes had hung like suspended sentences. Now the chamber stank of ozone, sharp and chemical, seeping from the switch cabinets where the auditors' new protocols sparked against old wire.

"They wrote the right to speak into the ledger as a column," Tobin whispered, his voice caught in the lamp-flicker. "A measurable. A ration."

"And the right to *listen*?" Corin asked. His good hand found the wall, fingers brushing dust from a grille where a listener had once breathed. "The right to hold a thought before it becomes a word?"

Old Sable's staff struck the rung. Once. Twice. "The right to carry a name across a threshold without the ledger weighing it. That is not a column. That is a *door*."

The ninth bell shuddered. Not a ring—a *recognition*. Its bronze skin rippled where the green pulses kissed it, and in that light Mira saw the watermark the Keeper had traced: a boundary drawn in pressure, not ink. The margin where harm could not follow.

Above them, the third bell tolled. One hour gone. The auditors' boots sounded in the Registry's corridors—heavy, measured, ink-stained.

Tobin's screwdriver found the next relay. The speaking-tube's song shifted key.
