# Abschnitt 1: The Ledger's Hollow

The paper dust lay on the relay contacts like frost on a grave.

Mira saw it first when she crept through the maintenance shaft beneath the second gate, her cracked jar empty and her breath held. The pneumatic tubes that carried the Keeper's orders had gone quiet three days past, and now she understood why: someone had ground paper fine as ash and fed it into the system, and the brass valves wore the grey coat like mourning. She did not touch them. She had learned in the lower tiers that touching what the council owned left marks on the fingers that listeners could read.

Above her, through the stone, she heard Corin's voice—low, urgent, speaking to the bronze itself.

"You have no tongue," he murmured to the ninth bell. "So how do you sing?"

Mira climbed toward him. The shaft narrowed, then opened into the bell chamber where dust motes hung like a suspended sentence. Corin knelt before the great bronze mouth, his broken arm strapped to his chest, his good hand pressed flat against the metal. His fingers trembled.

"It hums in a frequency the ledger cannot record," he said without turning. "I checked the calibration marks. Someone has altered them. The council's standard—" He stopped. Swallowed. "The standard assumes every citizen's voice weighs the same. But the marks have been filed down. Some voices now measure lighter than they are. Some measure heavier."

Mira thought of Tobin's shaking hands, of the names that appeared twice and the names that vanished.

"Which names?" she asked.

Corin finally looked at her. His eyes were the color of cooled iron.

"Yours," he said. "Yours measures heavier. And Old Sable's. And mine." He paused. "And the Keeper's own name has been struck from the margin entirely."

The bell hummed louder. The paper dust on the relays shifted, as if something below was breathing.

Then came the sound of footsteps on the stair—measured, unhurried, the gait of a man who had walked forty years of gates and never once stumbled. The Keeper's shadow fell across the threshold before his body did.

"You have found the hollow," he said quietly. "I wondered who would."

In his hand he held the ledger. Its cover was stained with something dark, and the pages—Mira saw—were not all paper. Some were skin.
