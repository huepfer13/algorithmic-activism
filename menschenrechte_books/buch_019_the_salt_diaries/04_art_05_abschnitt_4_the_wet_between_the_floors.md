# Abschnitt 4: The Wet Between the Floors

**April 10**
The drip started sometime before dawn. I counted it before I opened my eyes—one, two, three, then the pause that lasts too long, then again. Condensate from the crawlspace above the relay housing, running down the cable shafts in thin threads that pool at the junction boxes. Tomas says it's the thermal drift, the old insulation sweating through its last winter. I logged it as a maintenance note, nothing more. But the sound follows me. It has a rhythm like the 47 Hz stutter of the lamps, like something trying to speak in a language made of water and rust. I keep the plastic tag in my boot still. Rook has not come back. Four days now. Mira wrote nothing on the whiteboard this morning. The cistern reads 29.

**April 11**
Pavel's hands shook so badly he dropped the net weight into the sump. We fished it out with the long hook, but he stood there wet to the elbows, staring at the water like it had asked him a question he couldn't answer. I told him to sit. He sat. I logged his intake at 1,650 calories, 1.7 liters. The servitude parameter: zero. The life-integrity reading: 0.91, down from 0.94. I ran the calibration twice. The housing authority requires the integrity floor at 0.85 before the automatic ration reduction triggers. We have margin. Not much. The drip kept time behind the walls. Drip. Pause. Drip.

**April 12**
Mira pulled me into the lamp room before the morning log. She had the blue-inked paper—the same folded square Tomas rescued from the shredder relay, the one that still smells faintly of warm dust and ozone. She spread it flat on the workbench and pointed to a corner I hadn't seen before. A watermark. A serial number. She said the paper came from a supply batch that was decommissioned eight years ago, before the rationing protocols were even drafted. Before the island. Before us. She asked me what it means that the machine is printing on paper older than the crisis. I said I didn't know. She said the audit team will know. The drip had found a new cadence by then—faster, almost urgent. I checked the junction box under the east stair. The water there was warm.

**April 13**
The pneumatic tube hissed at 03:40. I was awake. I am always awake now. The capsule held a single sheet: a technical directive, unnumbered, unsigned. It instructs us to recalibrate the treatment parameter to art_05, threshold zero, no exceptions. The directive says the protection of life and physical integrity is the primary constraint and that all subordinate parameters must yield. I read it three times. The drip stopped. In the silence I heard Pavel coughing in his bunk, and Mira's pen scratching the log, and somewhere below us the tide eating the north marker another centimeter. I logged the directive. I did not wake the others. The white plastic tag sat on the table where I'd left it, and for the first time I noticed it has a number stamped on the edge. It matches the serial on the blue paper. I am writing this now with the lamp flickering at 47 Hz and the condensate beginning again, slower this time, as if waiting.
