# Abschnitt 1: Kalibrierung der Stille

**March 27**

The pipe is blocked again. Third time this week. I spent the morning with a length of wire, fishing for whatever's stuck in the pneumatic line that runs from the lamp room down to the kitchen. What came out was paper. Fine grit, like someone ground a page against the relay contacts until it became dust. I didn't tell Ines. I put the dust in my pocket and washed my hands.

**March 28**

Mira watched me at breakfast. She counts my chews now, I think. I counted hers back. We're even. Nobody wins.

The pipe coughed once around noon and spat out a scrap with three letters on it: R-E-S. The rest was pulp. I showed Rook. He said it was probably nothing. His shoulder twitched when he said it.

**March 29**

Old Pavel asked me what "equal" means. He said he heard the word in his head last night, like a song he couldn't place. I told him it's a mainland word. He nodded and went back to mending the net. I don't think he believed me.

**March 30**

Tomas fixed the pipe. He didn't say how. He just did it, and then he stood by the cistern for twenty minutes with his hand on the valve, like he was checking if it was still real. Ines logged it as maintenance. She didn't ask questions. That's two times now she hasn't punished him. I'm keeping count.

**March 31**

The calibrator on the relay is drifting. I noticed it this morning when the pressure gauge flickered between two numbers that shouldn't touch. The paper dust is back. I cleaned it. It came back within the hour. Something on the mainland is still sending, or something on the island is still grinding. I don't know which is worse.

Mira says we have thirty-four days of water. She said the number out loud this time. Nobody reacted. We all just kept eating.

**April 1**

Rook paced the perimeter and said the numbers. Eleven meters lost became twelve. He didn't sit down after. He walked straight into the sea up to his knees and stood there until Ines called him back. He came back. That's the only thing that happened today that I'm sure of.

I checked the relay again tonight. The dust was warm.
