# Abschnitt 2: Driftparameter

**April 2**

The audit notice came through the pneumatic tube at 0400. Red border. Central Registry. Three days. Ines read it twice, then burned the original and typed a copy with her fingers shaking. She doesn't shake. I've known her seven years. The calibrator drift from yesterday — the gauge flickering between 3.2 and 3.8 bar — she logged it as "resolved." Tomas's recalibration held. But the paper dust came back warm. Warm means friction. Friction means something is moving that shouldn't.

**April 3**

Mira recounted the water. Thirty-three days now. She didn't say it out loud. She wrote it on the whiteboard in the kitchen: 33. Rook erased the 4 and wrote 2. Ines made him rewrite it. The two-sentence rule started at breakfast. Pavel said "Good morning" and "The net needs mending." That was his allowance. I said "The relay holds" and "Pressure stable." Lie. Both. The gauge flicked again at 0917. 3.2 to 3.8. Back to 3.2. Like a heartbeat.

**April 4**

Tomas found the source. He didn't tell Ines. He told me. The paper isn't coming from the mainland. It's coming from the lamp room. The old logbooks. Someone is feeding them into the shredder relay, page by page. The pneumatic line runs both ways. He showed me the hatch. Fresh scratches on the metal. Recent. The dust in my pocket matches the ink — blue, the kind Ines uses for the official logs.

**April 5**

Rook walked the perimeter at midnight. I watched from the lamp room window. He stopped at the north marker. Twelve meters. He knelt. Put his hand in the water. Pulled up a piece of white plastic. A tag. He put it in his pocket and walked back. Didn't look up. The audit is in two days. Ines has us re-filing three years of consumption logs. Every calorie. Every word. She's sweating. I can smell it through the floorboards.

**April 6**

The parameter for life and physical integrity: calibrated at 2,100 calories, 2.3 liters, zero unauthorized speech. The audit will check the math. They'll check the bodies against the numbers. Pavel hasn't eaten in two days. He says the fish taste like metal. Mira says the cistern reads 32 days. Rook says the tide took another meter last night. Eleven. No, thirteen. The gauge just flicked again. 3.2. 3.8. 3.2. The paper dust in my pocket is warm.
