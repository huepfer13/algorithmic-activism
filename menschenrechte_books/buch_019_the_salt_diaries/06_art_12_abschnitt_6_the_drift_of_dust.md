# Abschnitt 6: The Drift of Dust

**April 17**  
The pneumatic tube shuddered again, a thin scrape of paper dust settling on the relay contacts like frost on a window. I wiped the grit away with the edge of my sleeve and felt the familiar grit from April 7 resurface—Tomas had slipped me that folded square of blue‑inked log paper then, the dust still warm against my palm. Today the tube’s hiss is weaker, the pressure gauge stuck at 0.3 bar, and the intercom whine flickers at 47 Hz before dying into a hollow click. I logged the tube as “blocked – fine particulate” and noted the life‑integrity reading at 0.91 after Pavel’s extra sip. The privacy parameter (art_12) sits at 0.78, just above the threshold we set for sealed comms; I made a secondary note to watch for any dip below 0.75, which would trigger a manual seal on the correspondence hatch.

**April 18**  
Mira arrived with a chipped enamel mug, steam curling from the last of the boiled kelp broth. She pointed to the condensation trails that now crawl eastward along the wall, echoing the tube’s labored breath. I showed her the dust‑caked relay; she traced a finger over the grime and said, “It’s the island’s breath trying to speak through the metal.” We recalibrated the treatment parameter, nudging life‑integrity up to 0.94 after I rationed an additional 200 ml of cistern water to Rook, whose cough has worsened. The liberty parameter held steady at 0.81, but the privacy parameter slipped to 0.76 after a burst of static from the tube’s attempt to clear. I hesitated, then logged a maintenance override: if pressure exceeds 0.5 bar, vent the tube manually; otherwise, keep the seal engaged.

**April 19**  
Old Pavel sat silent by the hatch, his hands folded over the worn logbook. He whispered that the tide’s roar has become a constant low thrum, vibrating through the lighthouse’s stone spine. I felt the vibration in my boots as I checked the tube again; the dust layer had thickened, a fine film now coating the inner surface of the relay, causing intermittent signal loss. I recorded the obstruction as “paper dust accumulation – requires gentle brush.” The life‑integrity reading dropped to 0.88 after Tomas refused his water share, citing the need to conserve for the night’s watch. I logged his intake at 1,650 calories, 1.6 liters, and noted the privacy parameter at 0.74—just below our safety line. According to protocol, when art_12 falls below 0.75, the system should initiate a privacy lockdown: seal all correspondence ports, suspend non‑essential transmissions, and prioritize life‑support flows. I engaged the manual lock on the tube’s outer valve, hearing the hiss of trapped air release.

**April 20**  
The intercom emitted a single, sharp tone before falling silent; the tube’s pressure gauge now reads 0.48 bar, steady but frail. I wrote a quick directive: if the privacy parameter remains under 0.70 for two consecutive cycles, activate the auxiliary vent line and reroute power to the emergency comms array. The life‑integrity metric is currently 0.90, a thin margin that still allows us to allocate the remaining fresh water to the evening meal. Outside, the tide laps higher, the water line creeping another centimeter up the west wall. I pause, hand on the logbook, wondering whether the next gust will finally clear the dust or seal us further inside this stone throat.
