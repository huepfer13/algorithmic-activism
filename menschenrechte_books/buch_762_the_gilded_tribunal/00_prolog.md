# The Gilded Tribunal

The elevator doors sealed with a sound like a gavel striking. Elias Ward watched the floor numbers climb—forty-one, forty-two—and felt the familiar constriction in his chest. Beside him, his client's hands trembled against her thighs, though Dr. Lena Moravec's face remained composed. She had the stillness of someone who had learned to survive by showing nothing.

"Forty-eight hours," she said quietly. "That's all they give us."

"Forty-eight hours is a lifetime in this building," Elias replied. "It's also no time at all."

The doors opened onto glass. Every wall of the tribunal chamber was transparent, revealing the grey sprawl of the corporate city-state below. Above, the ceiling held the dark eyes of surveillance nodes, each one tracking, recording, weighing. The remote jury would watch from their sealed rooms, their faces filtered through algorithmic assessment before the judges ever saw them.

Judge Alaric Voss sat at the elevated bench, his fingers steepled, his eyes never leaving the defense table. He had the reputation of a man who had never once hesitated before a verdict. His pen tapped a slow rhythm against the docket—tap, tap, tap—a metronome counting down.

Prosecutor Mira Chen stood at her table, already arranging her evidence tablets. She was young for her position, sharp-featured, and she wore her ambition like a blade. She did not look at Elias. She never did until the hearing began.

Warden Kade Rusk entered through the side door, his uniform crisp, his hands clasped behind his back. He was the keeper of the accused, the enforcer of the deadline, the man who would escort Dr. Moravec to her cell if the verdict fell against her. His jaw was set, his eyes flat. He had done this before. He would do it again.

"All parties present," the clerk announced. "Tribunal case four-seven-nine. The State versus Dr. Lena Moravec. Deadline: forty-eight hours from commencement."

Voss raised his pen. "Let the record show we begin."

Mira Chen stepped forward. "The State will demonstrate that Dr. Moravec's research directly endangered forty thousand citizens. We will show intent. We will show negligence. And we will show that she knew—" She paused, letting the word hang. "—exactly what she was doing."

Elias rose. "The defense will demonstrate that Dr. Moravec acted to protect lives, not endanger them. And that the State's own protocols—" He glanced at the surveillance nodes above. "—are the true threat here."

Voss's pen stopped tapping.

"Objection," Mira said, without looking up. "Counsel is arguing before testimony."

"Sustained," Voss said. "Confine yourself to procedure, Mr. Ward."

Elias nodded, but his eyes caught the warden's. Rusk's expression had shifted—just slightly. Something moved behind those flat eyes. Something that looked like fear.
