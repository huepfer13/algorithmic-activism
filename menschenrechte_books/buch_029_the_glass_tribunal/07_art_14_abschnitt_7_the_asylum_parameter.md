# Abschnitt 7: The Asylum Parameter

The clerk didn't walk. He stood frozen, fingers still curled around the seized pneumatic tube, while the registry bench buzzed with the particular silence that precedes a central audit. Three minutes earlier, a priority cipher had flashed across every console: CENTRAL REGISTRY REVISION TEAM EN ROUTE. ETA FORTY MINUTES. The woman in grey had gone pale beneath her composure, her tablet screen reflecting the red banner scrolling across the tribunal's closed circuit.

"Walk it," Okonkwo repeated, not unkindly. "The order stands. Recalibration audit. Independent instrumentation. 0600."

Halvorsen moved first, rising from counsel's bench with the economy of someone who had measured these bulkheads in strides. "Your Honor, if the consortium controls the instrumentation, they control the calibration baseline. Article fourteen's asylum parameter — the displacement threshold — was written into the array's firmware as a life-integrity override. Fifty-two decibels triggers it. The revision team knew this."

"Article fourteen," the woman in grey said, her voice thin. "That's a migration statute."

"It's a survival threshold," Mara said. She hadn't sat down. "The array treats any witness whose personal-channel variance exceeds fifty-two decibels as a displaced person. Their testimony routes to a protected partition. The consortium's own engineers coded it that way — life and physical integrity taking precedence over procedural continuity. The blackout cycles didn't just breach the privacy floor. They triggered asylum protocols for twelve witnesses. Their testimony is sealed in a partition the revision team cannot access without tripping the integrity flag."

Sister Adaeze's transcript binder snapped shut. The sound cracked like a rifle shot in the cramped deck. "And the central registry's audit team?"

"Will find clean instruments," Halvorsen said. "Because the partition hides in the calibration gap. Twelve decibels. Forty to fifty-two. The witnesses' lives are in that gap."

The clerk's hand trembled as he released the tube. He remembered the cylinder, the spark, the paper grit spitting into the silence. He took the order sheet and walked.
