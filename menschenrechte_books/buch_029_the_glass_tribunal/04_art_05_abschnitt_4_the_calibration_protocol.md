# Abschnitt 4: The Calibration Protocol

The darkness lasted four seconds. Then the emergency circuit caught, and the hearing deck returned in a wash of amber light that made every face look like something preserved. The clerk's hand was still on the seal. He had not moved.

"Nobody transmits," Okonkwo repeated. "The registry's audit is suspended pending ruling."

The woman with the tablet had not lowered it. "The charter does not permit suspension of registry functions."

"The charter does not permit registry officers to break a tribunal seal either." Okonkwo's voice carried no heat. "We will rule on the objection. Sit down or be removed."

She sat. The two men in grey remained standing until she gestured, and then they too folded into the gallery bench, their suits creasing in unison.

Mara had not moved from her place. The condensate was back — a slow, rhythmic drip from the ceiling panel above the cable shafts, each drop landing with a soft tick against the metal housing. She counted three before she spoke.

"Mr. Halvorsen. The array's calibration protocol. Article five of the technical annex."

Halvorsen's thumb had stopped pressing. "Thermal integrity threshold. If the sensor housing drifts beyond point-three degrees, the system flags a fault and the reading is void."

"And what happens to the void reading?"

"It's quarantined. Held for review. Not transmitted."

"And the maintenance log for a quarantined reading?"

"Generated automatically. Timestamped. Immutable."

The woman in grey leaned forward. "Then the log exists. The tribunal will produce it."

"The log exists," Mara said, "in the array's local memory. Which went offline for eleven days. Which the witness has already testified to." She turned to Okonkwo. "The registry is asking us to produce a record from a system that was not recording. The calibration protocol — article five, thermal integrity, the point-three threshold — that protocol exists to prevent exactly this. A reading taken outside threshold is not evidence. It is noise. And noise cannot be subpoenaed."

The drip ticked again. Four. Five.

"The registry's position," the woman said, "is that the witness deliberately disabled the thermal flag to avoid quarantine. That is the servitude. The instrument was kept in service to concealment."

"The instrument was kept in service to the river," Mara said. "The precedence clause. Protection of life and physical integrity. When the array failed, the witness chose to keep monitoring rather than shut down. The thermal drift was real. The readings were compromised. But the river was still breathing, and he kept listening."

Okonkwo's pen lifted. "The objection is sustained. The registry will not access the blank channel's maintenance log. The witness is not in contempt." He set the pen down. "The tribunal will rule on the precedence clause before the next session. Until then, the deck remains sealed."

The clerk reached again for the seal. This time the lamps held.

Above the clerk's station, the pneumatic tube coughed — that same dry, mechanical bark from the first morning — and fell silent. Mara looked up. The tube was empty. It had been empty for three days.

The drip ticked. Six.
