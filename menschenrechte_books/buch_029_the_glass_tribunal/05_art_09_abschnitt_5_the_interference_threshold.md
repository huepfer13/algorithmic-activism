# Abschnitt 5: The Interference Threshold

The high-frequency whine cut through the ventilation hum at 0400 hours, a needle of sound that made the fillings in Mara's jaw ache. She had been reviewing the thermal drift logs on a screen that flickered with the vessel's unstable power grid. The whine rose, fell, rose again — damping fields cycling in the bulkheads, the consortium's counter-surveillance suite reacting to something in the water column outside.

Halvorsen looked up from his notes. His fingers, still stained with sensor gel from the array retrieval, pressed against his left ear. "The interference threshold. Article nine of the operational annex."

"The array's isolation protocol," Mara said. "If external signal variance exceeds forty decibels, the telemetry locks out. Prevents contamination."

"Prevents transmission," Halvorsen corrected. "The readings still write to local memory. They just don't leave the hull."

The whine spiked. A monitor in the gallery sputtered, died. Sister Adaeze, seated three rows back, did not flinch. Her hands remained folded over the transcript binder she had brought from the morning session — the one with the redacted pages.

Okonkwo's pen stopped mid-stroke. Around the cramped hearing deck, three assistants began collating loose pages with a speed that had not been in them all morning. The judge's eyes tracked the movement, then returned to the witness stand.

"Mr. Halvorsen," Okonkwo said. "The interference threshold. Was it breached during the eleven-day blackout?"

Halvorsen's throat moved. "The damping fields were active. Consortium protocol. They cycled every ninety minutes. Each cycle pushed variance to fifty-two decibels. The array logged the breach. The array also logged that the thermal flag remained suppressed."

"Suppressed by whom?"

"The precedence clause. Protection of life and physical integrity. The witness made the call."

The whine dropped to a sub-audible throb. The monitor in the gallery rebooted, showing the consortium's seal pulsing in slow rotation.

"Then the record exists," the woman in grey said from the registry bench. "Local memory. Fifty-two decibels. Thermal flag suppressed. The tribunal will order extraction."

"The tribunal will not," Okonkwo said. "The extraction protocol requires physical access to the array housing. Which is currently under consortium seal. Which this tribunal has not authorized breaching."

"Then authorize it."

Okonkwo's pen hovered. The pneumatic tube above the clerk's station coughed again — once, sharply. A cylinder dropped into the receiving tray. The clerk did not reach for it.
