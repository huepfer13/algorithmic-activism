# Abschnitt 6: The Calibration Defect

The cylinder in the receiving tray gathered dust for eleven minutes before anyone touched it. Mara watched the clerk's hand hover, withdraw, hover again — the pneumatic tube's relay contacts were gummed with a fine grey powder, paper grit from a thousand sealed envelopes, and every attempt to cycle the tube now produced that same dry cough before the mechanism seized.

"The revision team's calibration certificate," Okonkwo said, nodding toward the tray. "Filed with the registry at the outset. Ms. Voss, you reviewed the annex."

Mara stood. The hearing deck's ceiling pressed low overhead, ventilation ducts sweating condensation onto the camera housings the consortium had bolted to every corner. "The certificate lists a privacy threshold of forty decibels, calibrated against Article twelve's interference standard. Arbitrary interference with correspondence — the annex treats it as a signal-to-noise floor. Anything above forty decibels in the personal-channel band counts as an attack on the record."

"An attack," the woman in grey repeated from the registry bench. "Or a calibration failure?"

"Both," Mara said. "The certificate was signed by the same revision team that arrived on this deck in chapter three — two men in grey, one woman holding her tablet like a shield. They certified the array's isolation protocol against a threshold they themselves could override."

Halvorsen's head came up. "Override how?"

"By cycling the damping fields during the blackout. Each cycle pushed variance to fifty-two decibels — twelve above the privacy floor. The system logged it as interference, not contamination. The thermal flag stayed suppressed because the precedence clause outranked the privacy threshold."

Okonkwo's pen moved. "So the blackout wasn't a failure of the array. It was a failure of the calibration."

"The calibration was never meant to hold," Mara said. "The revision team set the floor at forty and the override at fifty-two. A twelve-decibel gap. Wide enough to drive a suppression order through."

The clerk finally lifted the cylinder. The relay contacts sparked, spat paper grit, and the tube above the station went silent. He unscrewed the cap and slid the certificate across the bench. Okonkwo read it once, then again.

"Article twelve," he said slowly. "Protection against arbitrary interference. The consortium's own annex invokes it as a technical parameter — a floor, not a right. And the floor was breached by design."

"By design," Halvorsen echoed. "The array recorded every cycle. Local memory. Fifty-two decibels, twelve times over eleven days."

Sister Adaeze's hands tightened on her transcript binder. She had not spoken since the morning session, but her eyes moved between Okonkwo and the certificate with the patience of someone who had already read the ending.

"The tribunal will order a recalibration audit," Okonkwo said. "Independent instrumentation. The revision team's certificates are suspended pending review."

The woman in grey rose. "The consortium controls the instrumentation."

"Then the consortium will watch its own instruments being audited," Okonkwo said. "Under seal. Under camera. Starting at 0600."

The clerk reached for the pneumatic tube to dispatch the order. The relay contacts ground against the paper grit and seized entirely. He looked up, pale.

"The line is blocked, Your Honor."

Okonkwo did not blink. "Then walk it."
