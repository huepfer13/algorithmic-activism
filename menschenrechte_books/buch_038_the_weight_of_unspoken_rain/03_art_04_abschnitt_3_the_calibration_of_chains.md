# Abschnitt 3: The Calibration of Chains

The auditor's name was Ines Rook, and she carried the Ledger as if it were a sleeping animal. Mira watched her ascend the cliff path, each step precise, the fog parting before her like a congregation making way for the officiant.

The relay station's discharge lamps flickered at forty-seven hertz—a frequency Mira had measured often enough to feel in her teeth. The ozone smell sharpened as Rook approached, the air turning metallic at the switch cabinets. Cass had once told her the lamps were calibrated to that rhythm because it discouraged whispering. The body learned to hold its breath at the wrong moments.

"Stationkeeper," Rook said. Not a question.

Cass inclined her head. "Auditor."

Rook set the Ledger on the intake table. Its cover rippled, catching light the way the water carts did when they still ran full. "You've received the revised parameters."

"Life folded into one vector," Cass said. "We read the drone."

"Then you understand the calibration." Rook opened the Ledger. The pages were not paper but something thinner, translucent, veined like the inside of a wrist. "Article Four requires verification. Bondage indices. Servitude markers. The Ministry has determined that certain labor arrangements in the lower districts require reclassification."

Mira's pencil moved before she could stop it. *Reclassification. Not abolition. Renaming.*

"The water carriers," Cass said slowly. "The ones who signed indenture contracts after the drought."

"Voluntary servitude is a protected category," Rook said. "But the Ministry requires documentation. Proof that the arrangements remain—" she paused, her finger tracing a vein in the page "—consensual."

The lamps flickered. Ozone bit the air. Mira thought of the water cart's wheel, the too-fast squeak she had recorded at dawn, the way sound moved when no one was permitted to speak it aloud. The cart had passed the relay station three times that morning. Three times, the same wheel, the same rhythm. As if someone were counting.

"The carriers can't consent to what they can't speak," Mira said.

Rook looked at her for the first time. Her eyes were the color of the Ledger's pages—translucent, veined. "You're the recorder."

"I keep the calibration logs."

"You keep something." Rook turned a page. "The Ministry is aware of unregistered notations in Sector Seven. Whispers that never reached the Ledger. Shadows collected without proper filing." She closed the book. "The audit will proceed at dusk. Have your records ready."

She descended the cliff path without looking back. The fog closed behind her like water over a stone.

Cass exhaled. "She knows about Tomas."

"She knows about the tin box," Mira said. "She just doesn't know she knows."

The lamps flickered again—forty-seven hertz, steady as a heartbeat. Somewhere below, the water cart's wheel began to squeak.
