# Abschnitt 6: The Calibration of Walls

The pneumatic tube shuddered. A cylinder jammed halfway through the relay, its brass cap scored by fine paper dust that smelled of dried ink and old decisions. Mira tapped the contact plate. The dust lifted, suspended in the lamp-light like pollen in a breath held too long.

"Eleven, twelve, thirteen," she said. The drip had not stopped counting since she picked up her pencil. The rhythm lived in her fingers now, a second pulse beneath the skin.

Tomas flexed his hand. The frost-pattern had deepened, branching across his wrist in intervals that matched the condensation cycle. "Rook's broadcast rides the pressure differential. She's using the pipes as antennae. Every carrier's shadow thins because the fog reads their frequency as unauthorized transmission."

Cass unfolded a recording envelope. The whisper inside smelled of river-salt and rust. "The Listener says the integrity clause activates when physical protection exceeds verification thresholds. The carriers' bodies are the parameter. Their shadows are... collateral syntax."

Mira moved to the intake bench. Her calibration logs lay open to the unwritten column—numbers measured in teeth, recorded nowhere official. She touched the pencil to paper. The graphite left no mark. The fog had already claimed the page's willingness to receive.

"Article Twelve," she said. The words formed in the air between them, visible as frost-crystals. "Privacy parameter. Home parameter. Correspondence parameter. The Ministry's isolation chambers violate all three by design. The Ledger cannot document what it refuses to acknowledge."

The intercom whined. Forty-seven hertz. Rook's voice came through stripped of harmonics, pure carrier wave: "Verification proceeds at dusk. The carriers will submit to calibration or the fog will submit them."

Tomas laughed, a dry sound like paper tearing. "She thinks the fog takes orders."

The pneumatic tube coughed. The jammed cylinder ejected backward, clattering onto the relay shelf. Its cap had unscrewed. Inside, a single sheet of paper bore no Ministry seal—only a hand-drawn map of the lower districts' water mains, annotated in ink that shifted color when the light caught it.

Mira picked it up. The map showed her own relay station, marked with a red X. Beside it, someone had written: *The walls listen. The pipes remember. Calibrate accordingly.*

The drip counted fourteen, fifteen, sixteen.
