# Abschnitt 2: The Ledger of Breath

The revision notice arrived pinned to the pneumatic tube's mouth like a moth impaled on a collector's board. Mira found it at dawn, before the Hush had fully lifted, when the fog still clung to the cliff face in grey ribbons and the lower districts were nothing but a rumour of rooftops beneath the cloud-line.

**CENTRAL REGISTRY AUDIT — SECTOR 7 — IMMINENT.**

She read it three times. The paper was new stock, unrationed, the kind that held ink without bleeding. Someone in the capital had spent water on this warning.

Old Cass was already at the relay station, her fingers moving through the seized tubes with the precision of a woman who had once calibrated them herself. She did not look up.

"They're sending an auditor," Cass said. Her voice was a whisper, but the Hush was thin this close to the cliff's edge, and the words survived. "Forty years I've kept this station breathing. Now they want to see the books."

Mira slid the notice into her notebook, beside the calibration order Tomas had brought. The brotherhood checkbox remained unmarked. She had checked it twice in the night, as if the ink might have shifted.

"Where's Tomas?"

"Swallowed." Cass tapped her throat, then her chest. "The way he does. He's gone to retrieve something from the lower intakes. Said the drought's been climbing faster than the Ledger admits."

The lower districts. Where the water ration had dropped to a quarter-cup per household, where shadows were collected at random and never returned. Mira thought of the tin box against Tomas's sternum, eleven years of whispers compressed into metal. He had swallowed something again. He always did, when the Ministry's eyes grew too bright.

A drone passed overhead, its underbelly flickering with fresh decrees. The fog stirred, tasting the air.

"Life parameter," Cass said suddenly, reading the drone's glow. "They've reclassified it. Liberty and security of person—all folded under a single calibration vector. One checkbox now. Not three."

Mira's pencil moved across onion-skin. *The Ministry collapses life, liberty, and security into a single parameter. One box to mark. One box to leave empty.*

"And if the box stays empty?" she asked.

Cass did not answer. Her hands had stopped moving through the tubes.

The fog curled around the relay station's legs, patient as a collector. Somewhere below, in the parched streets, a water cart's wheel began to squeak—the same wheel, the same squeak Mira had recorded at dawn. But the sound was wrong now. It came too fast, too rhythmic, like a pulse.

Cass looked up. "That's not the cart."

Mira closed her notebook. The Hush was thickening, drinking the light.

"Audit's moved up," Cass whispered. "They're already here."

The fog parted at the cliff's edge. A figure stood there, silhouetted against the grey, holding a Ledger bound in something that caught the light like water.
