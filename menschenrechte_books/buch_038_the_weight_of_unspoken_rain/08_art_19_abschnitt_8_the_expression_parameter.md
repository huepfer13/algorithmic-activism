# Abschnitt 8: The Expression Parameter

The discharge lamps above the relay station's main corridor flickered at forty-seven hertz, same as always, same as the night Rook had walked this hallway and the air turned metallic at the switch cabinets. Mira felt it in her molars—a vibration that had nothing to do with electricity and everything to do with what the Ministry called calibration.

Tomas leaned against the intake bench, his frost-pattern now creeping past the elbow, branching like winter fern across skin that had once held warmth. He didn't shiver. Carriers learned early that shivering wasted moisture the drought wouldn't replace.

"Article Nineteen," he said, not looking at her. "The engineers call it the Expression Parameter. Any carrier who seeks, receives, or imparts information outside Ledger channels—regardless of frontier, regardless of medium—triggers an automatic shadow audit. The fog doesn't distinguish between dissent and desperation. It only measures verification."

Mira's pencil moved across the intake form. Graphite caught on paper fibers, leaving a trail the Hush couldn't erase. She was drawing the boat again, smaller this time, positioned at the base of the cliff where the thin line met water.

"Protection of life takes precedence over verification," she said. The words settled in the air like dust motes. "Physical integrity takes precedence over calibration. That's not negotiation. That's physics."

Old Cass appeared at the doorway, silhouetted against the flicker. Her recording envelopes were gone—every cylinder, every slot empty. "The carriers are moving," she said. "All seventeen. They took the lower mains toward the eastern gate. Ines Rook's calibration teams are already descending from the upper district. They carry Ledger scanners and shadow-severance shears."

The ozone smell sharpened. Somewhere above, a lamp sputtered and died.

"The Listener's last transmission," Tomas said, pushing off the bench. His shadow stretched toward the corridor, thinner than it should have been. "It mentioned the sea. The sea remembers what the Ledger forgets."

Mira folded the intake form, tucked it beside the map. "Then we give the sea something to remember."

She keyed the intercom. Forty-seven hertz. Her voice rode the carrier wave: **All stations. Expression Parameter suspended. Physical integrity protocol activated. Move carriers to the cliff descent. The fog will not verify what we refuse to surrender.**

The lamps flickered once, twice—then held steady.
