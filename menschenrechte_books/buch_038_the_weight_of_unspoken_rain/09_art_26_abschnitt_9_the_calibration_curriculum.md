# Abschnitt 9: The Calibration Curriculum

The condensate had found its rhythm by midnight—a slow drip from the thermal drift in the crawlspace above the relay station's lower corridor, each drop striking the cable shafts with the patience of a metronome counting down to something no Ledger would record.

Mira stood at the eastern gate with eleven carriers behind her, their shadows thin as rice paper where the Hush had already begun its arithmetic. The first carrier—the one who had crawled through the overflow channel three nights ago, his shadow trailing like torn flag—now leaned against the gate's rusted hinge, his breath shallow, his eyes fixed on the descent path where Rook's calibration teams had planted their scanners.

"The Ministry calls it Article Twenty-Six," Tomas said, his frost-pattern now mapping the hollow of his throat. "Educational access protocol. Every citizen receives foundational calibration. Elementary verification is compulsory. Technical training, professional certification, higher verification—all distributed according to merit, according to need, according to the Ledger's assessment of compliance."

Old Cass emerged from the relay station's shadow, her empty recording envelopes clutched against her chest like dried leaves. "They're using the schoolhouses," she said. "Rook's teams. They've converted the lower district's elementary halls into verification chambers. Children who can't recite their Ledger entries—" She stopped. The drip filled the silence.

Mira felt the map's red X still burning against her retina, the memory of that first carrier's arrival sharp as graphite on paper fibers. "Article Twenty-Six isn't about education," she said. "It's about who gets to learn what the fog permits. Who gets calibrated into silence before they're old enough to whisper."

The ozone smell sharpened. Above them, a scanner pulsed—once, twice—its beam cutting through the Hush like a blade through water.

"The Listener's transmission," Tomas said, pressing his palm against the gate's cold iron. "It mentioned the tide tables. The sea keeps records the Ministry can't confiscate."

Mira unfolded the intake form from her pocket, the boat she'd drawn now smudged but visible, its hull pointed toward the cliff's edge where the water met stone. "Then we teach the sea to read."

She keyed her intercom. Forty-seven hertz. The carrier wave hummed beneath her voice: **All stations. Educational access protocol suspended. Physical integrity takes precedence over verification. Move carriers to the tide caves. The fog will not calibrate what we refuse to surrender.**

The gate groaned open. Beyond it, the descent path glistened with condensate, each drop catching the scanner's fading pulse.

Rook's voice cut through the fog from somewhere above: "Mira Solano. Your shadow audit has been scheduled. Report to the upper district calibration chamber at dawn."

Mira stepped onto the path. Her shadow stretched behind her, thin but intact.

"Tell the Ministry," she said, "that dawn is already booked."
