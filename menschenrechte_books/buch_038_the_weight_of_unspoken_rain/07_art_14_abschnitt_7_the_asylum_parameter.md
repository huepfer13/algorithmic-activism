# Abschnitt 7: The Asylum Parameter

The map's red X still burned against Mira's retina when the first carrier arrived at the relay station's lower grate. He came crawling through the overflow channel, his shadow trailing behind him like a torn flag—thin at the edges, translucent where the fog had already begun its quiet arithmetic.

"Tomas Vey," he said, though she knew him. The tin box was still warm against his sternum. Eleven years of whispers compressed into metal, and now his shadow was paying the tariff.

Mira pulled him through. The drip counted twenty-three, twenty-four.

"The calibration order changed at midnight," Tomas said. His frost-pattern had spread past his wrist, climbing toward the elbow in branching intervals. "Rook's engineers are calling it the Asylum Parameter. Any carrier who crosses the lower district boundary without Ledger registration gets processed as an undocumented transmission. Their shadows are confiscated as contraband."

"That's Article Fourteen territory," Mira said. The pencil in her hand left no mark on the intake form. "Physical protection takes precedence over verification. They cannot calibrate what they refuse to shelter."

Old Cass emerged from the back room, her arms full of recording envelopes. She was filing them with unusual speed—shoving cylinders into slots, misaligning labels, her hands moving like startled birds. "Central Registry just announced an audit. Unspecified date. Unspecified scope." She dropped an envelope. It rolled under the bench. She didn't retrieve it. "They're coming to count what we haven't recorded."

The intercom hissed. Forty-seven hertz. Rook's voice, stripped and cold: "All carriers must present their shadows for calibration by dusk. Those who cannot produce documentation will be processed through the eastern gate. The fog will determine their status."

Tomas laughed—that dry, tearing sound. "The eastern gate leads to the cliff. There's nothing beyond it but the drop."

"Then the fog will determine their status," Mira said.

She crossed to the intake bench. The map still lay there, its ink shifting color. She traced the red X over her own station, then followed the water mains outward—lower districts, the grate where Tomas had crawled through, the eastern gate, and beyond it, a thin line that descended the cliff face toward the sea. Someone had drawn a boat.

The Listener's voice came through the recording envelope Cass had dropped. It smelled of river-salt and something older—wet stone, perhaps, or the memory of rain. "The Asylum Parameter activates when the fog cannot verify a shadow's origin. Unregistered carriers become unverified transmissions. Unverified transmissions become..." The whisper paused. "The sea remembers what the Ledger forgets."

Mira picked up her pencil. This time, the graphite left a mark—a single line, descending from the eastern gate toward the water.

The drip counted thirty-one, thirty-two.

"Get the carriers to the lower mains," she said. "All of them. Before dusk."

Tomas touched his frost-pattern. "And their shadows?"

"Let the fog take them." She folded the map into her pocket. "We'll find new ones on the other side."
