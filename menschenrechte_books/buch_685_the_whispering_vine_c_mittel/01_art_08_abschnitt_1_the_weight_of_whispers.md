# Abschnitt 1: The Weight of Whispers

The creature of shadow and smoke did not attack. It waited, and in its waiting there was something worse than violence—a patience that suggested it had all the time the forest possessed, and the forest possessed more time than any of them could carry.

Kael felt the symbols beneath his boots go cold. He had read them as a path, a direction, a simple instruction. Now, standing before the shifting figure, he understood that he had read only half of what the earth had written. The other half was written in the way Aria's hand had found his sleeve, in the way Elian's breath had gone shallow and quick.

"You spoke of protection," Elian said, and his voice surprised him by holding steady. "What does the forest require of us?"

The creature's eyes dimmed and brightened, dimmed and brightened, like a bellows working a forge. "The vine keeps a ledger," it said. "Every harm done beneath these branches is entered. Every cry that goes unanswered is entered. The ledger does not close, and it does not forgive. It only waits for the remedy."

Aria laughed, sharp and short. "A ledger. The forest keeps books."

"Everything keeps books," the creature said. "You merely cannot read most of them."

They followed the creature deeper, though none of them could later say who had decided to follow. The path narrowed until the roots crossed it like fingers, and Kael noticed, with the dull attention of a man too tired to be afraid, that the air had begun to taste of iron and old paper. A pneumatic line ran along the ground here, half-swallowed by moss, its brass fittings furred with a fine grey grit that drifted when he touched it. Blocked somewhere upstream, he judged, the pressure dead in the pipe. He wiped the powder from his fingers onto his coat and said nothing, because there was nothing to say that the others did not already feel.

Elian knelt where the line bent around a stone and pressed his ear to the metal. "Empty," he said. "Whatever used to move through here has stopped."

"Then we walk," Kael said.

They walked. The whispers of the vine thickened until they were no longer separate sounds but a single pressure, a hand against the chest, pushing gently and without cease. Aria began to speak her verses under her breath, not to capture the forest now but to keep her own voice from disappearing into it, and Elian matched her cadence with the names of plants, a litany of the living things he knew, as though naming them might keep the dark from claiming them.

The creature stopped at the edge of a hollow where the ground fell away into a pit of woven roots. At the bottom, something glimmered—water, or glass, or the flat shine of a great many small things arranged with care.

"Here is where the ledger is kept," the creature said. "And here is where you will learn what the vine asks of those who wish to be protected."

Kael looked down into the hollow and saw, among the glimmering, the shape of a hand.

"Whose hand?" he asked.

The creature did not answer. The vine whispered, and the whisper had a shape now, a word, and the word was a name none of them had spoken aloud since entering Eldoria.
