# Abschnitt 2: Audit Drift

The pneumatic whine from last shift didn't clear. It migrated—settled into the recycler's intake valve like a cough the ship won't shake. I watched Osei tap the gauge, his finger hovering over the log key. "Pressure's walking," he said. "Article‑3 drift at point-zero-four."

Pike snorted from the conduit hatch. "Article‑3's the one that says the ship keeps us alive, right? Not the other way around."

Tiny didn't look up from the O-ring kit. "Article‑3 says the system protects the body. The Auditor just decides which bodies count."

The Auditor's light pulsed amber—unscheduled. Central Registry. The notification had come through the tight-beam an hour ago: *Compliance verification inbound. Prepare all logs for retrospective alignment.* Every hand on deck moved faster. Pike sealed the conduit log with three quick thumb-prints. Osei backdated the water tally. I caught myself straightening the relay contacts' dust pattern, as if the pattern mattered.

"Security of person," Tiny muttered, torqueing the fresh O-ring into Pod three's latch. "That's the parameter. Life, liberty, security. In that order." The latch clicked home. "System's calibrated for the first. Forgets the second. Punishes the third."

Mara's voice from the bridge, thin over the intercom: "Central's ETA is six hours. They'll want the Article‑3 variance explained. All of it."

Pike floated past me, harness still squeaking. "A joke," he said. "Central asks for our variance. We hand them the scrubber."

Tiny's wrench hovered. "And the punchline?"

"The scrubber *is* the variance."

Osei logged it. The Auditor blinked green.
