# Abschnitt 1: Pressure Drop

I glanced at the pneumatic tube hiss, the fine paper dust settling on the relay contacts like a lazy snowfall. Osei was already at the water recycler, counting his sips aloud. “Three sips,” he said, then glanced at Tiny, “then a joke.” Tiny smirked, “If the joke’s a dud, you get four sips—quality control.” Osei chuckled, logged the sip, and the tube coughed a protest.

Pike floated past the bulkhead, his harness squeaking. “Auditor says I’m at ninety‑four percent compliance,” he muttered, “what’s the missing six?” I shrugged, “Your mouth, Pike.” He grinned, logged it himself, and the Auditor blinked a soft green.

The scrubber’s pressure read low again. I tapped the calibration console; the readout flashed “ARTICLE‑1 OFFSET: +0.02”. The system had been tuned to the UN baseline—equal distribution of life‑support resources per Article 1—and any drift triggered a quiet penalty. I adjusted the valve, feeling the hiss stabilize, and noted the dust on the tube’s relay contacts thickening.

Tiny drifted by the escape pod hatch, his fingers tracing the seal. “Pod three’s latch is sticky,” he said, “like it’s holding its breath.” I nodded, “We’ll need a spare O‑ring before the deadline tightens.” He logged the observation, his eyes flicking to the dwindling gauge on the wall.

Osei finished his water, lifted his cup, and declared, “Three sips, then a joke.” Tiny raised an eyebrow, “If the joke’s stale, you get five sips—extra credit for endurance.” Osei laughed, logged the sip, and the Auditor recorded another compliance point.

I checked the air flow again; the scrubber hummed steady, the dust on the tube contacts a reminder that even the smallest particles could shift the balance. The pneumatic line groaned, a faint whine hinting at a blockage forming deeper in the conduit.

Mara logged the latest readout, the Auditor’s light steady, and the clock ticked down to thirty‑nine hours.
