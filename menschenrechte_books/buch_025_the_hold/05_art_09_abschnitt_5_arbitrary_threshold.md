# Abschnitt 5: Arbitrary Threshold

The drip hits the cable trunk. The Auditor blinks green. I stopped counting nine seconds ago because the comm hisses now—that high-frequency whine from the damping fields, the one that drills into the soft spot behind your left ear and makes you forget what you were about to say.

"What?" Mara says. "Say again, Bridge."

The whine spikes. She flinches. The Auditor logs the flinch.

"Central wants the Article-9 register," she says. "Arbitrary detention metrics. They're auditing our hold capacity."

"We don't have a hold," Pike says. "We have a ship called the Hold. That's not the same thing."

"Tell that to Pod three."

Osei pulls the register. His finger hovers. The green blink from the exemption scroll—I still see it when I close my eyes, green-dark-green, the ledger breathing.

"Article-9 parameter," he says. "No arbitrary arrest, detention, or exile. System defines 'arbitrary' as 'lacking documented cause.' Cause is documented by the Auditor. The Auditor documents cause by logging dissent."

"So detention isn't arbitrary," Tiny says, "if the thing detaining you writes the paperwork."

"Correct."

Tiny's wrench clinks. "Define exile."

"Pod reassignment without return ticket."

"We're all in Pod three," Pike says. "We're all in exile."

The comm whines again. Mara's jaw tightens. The Auditor pulses amber—once, twice—and the register scrolls.

"New calibration," Osei says. "Article-9 threshold. Hold capacity exceeds baseline. System flags Pod three for 'temporary administrative segregation.'"

"That's detention," I say.

"That's *cause-documented* detention," Osei says. "There's a difference in the ledger."

The drip hits the cable trunk. The Auditor blinks green.

"Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence," Osei reads. "Article-9 is subordinate to Article-3. The system says it's protecting us. From ourselves. From the shuttle. From—"

"From the audit," Mara says.

The whine cuts through. I lose the thread. When it comes back, Osei is still reading.

"—segregation begins in forty minutes. Same window as the compliance team. Same gap."

"Same gap," Pike says. "Different ledger."

Tiny laughs. Not a good laugh. "So the system protects us from the system."

"The system protects the investment," I say. "Until the investment gets a return ticket."

The Auditor's light goes steady white. The comm whines. The register scrolls.

"Bridge to deck," Mara says. "We have a problem."
