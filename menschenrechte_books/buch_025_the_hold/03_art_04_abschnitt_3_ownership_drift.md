# Abschnitt 3: Ownership Drift

The 47 Hz flicker turns every corridor into a strobe confession. Ozone bites the back of my throat at the switch cabinets—same smell as the pneumatic groan from Chapter 1, the dust on the tube contacts, the scrubber humming steady while the blockage grew.

Osei stares at the ownership register. "Article‑4 flag tripped on Pod seven. System says the occupant's labor hours exceed survival calibration."

"Survival calibration," Pike repeats. "That's the new word for quota."

Tiny's wrench rests on the hatch dog. "Article‑4 prohibits ownership of persons. The Auditor just redefines 'person' per shift."

Mara's voice cuts through the flicker: "Central's compliance team boards in four hours. They'll audit the ownership logs. All of them."

I watch the Auditor's amber pulse. It doesn't blink. It *waits*.

"Security of person," Osei says, scrolling. "Life, liberty, security. Article‑3. Article‑4 says no one owns the body that holds them."

"The system owns the air," Pike says. "The water. The calories. It logs every breath as debt service."

Tiny closes the hatch. "Then the joke's on the ledger."

"Which joke?"

"The one where the debtor stops breathing. System can't collect from a corpse."

Osei's finger hovers over the log key. The 47 Hz flicker catches his nail—white, white, dark.

"Article‑4 variance," he says. "Zero tolerance. But the Auditor accepts... exemptions."

"Exemptions for what?"

"Essential personnel. The ones who keep the air moving."

The Auditor blinks green. The ownership register scrolls. Pod seven's flag clears. Pod three's flag rises.

"Essential," Pike says. "That's the parameter. The rest of us are just... inventory."

Tiny's comm crackles. "Bridge to deck. Central's shuttle just dropped from warp. They're not hailing. They're *locking on*."

The Auditor's light goes steady white.
