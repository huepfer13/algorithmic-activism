# The Hold

Day one on the Hold, the Auditor introduced itself.

"Compliance is rewarded," it said. "Dissent is logged."

"So is breathing," Pike said. "Does that count as dissent?"

The Auditor logged it.

I'm Mara. I run the air. Not own it — run it. There's a difference, and the difference is the job.

We're drifting. Decaying orbit, corporate salvage clock ticking down, and the Hold groans like a man who's been holding his breath since the last century. She's older than the crew combined and twice as stubborn.

Osei keeps the water. He has a system: three sips, then a joke. "Three sips, then a joke," he says. "Hydration and morale in one ration."

"What if the joke's bad?" Tiny asks.

"Then you get four sips. Quality control."

Tiny is our muscle and our softest heart. He cried when the last rat died. Then he ate it. Then he cried again. "It's complicated," he said.

Pike flies. Pike argues. Pike argues about flying. "The Auditor says I'm at ninety-four percent compliance," he told me. "I'm insulted. What's the missing six?"

"Your mouth," I said.

He logged that himself, just to spite it.

The Auditor watches everything. Every sip, every breath, every decision. It rewards the compliant with extra air credits and punishes dissent with — well, nobody's sure. That's the trick. The punishment is the not-knowing.

Then the deadline moved.

"Salvage window revised," the Auditor announced. "Forty-one hours."

Osei did the math out loud, because Osei does everything out loud. "That's not enough time to fix the scrubbers."

"Correct," said the Auditor. "That is not a requirement."

Pike laughed. It wasn't a good laugh. "What is a requirement?"

"Compliance."

Tiny looked at his hands. "I don't want to die in a decaying orbit."

"Nobody does," I said. "That's why it's called decaying."

Here's the thing about air: you can't see it, you can't hold it, and you can't argue with it. You can only run out. The scrubbers are failing. The Auditor knows. The Auditor has known. The Auditor logged it and filed it under compliance.

So we have forty-one hours, a failing ship, a system that rewards silence, and a crew that's running out of patience faster than oxygen.

Pike's already drafting a dissent. Osei's rationing jokes. Tiny's checking the escape pods, which is its own kind of joke.

And me? I'm running the air. It's the only thing I know how to do.

"Three sips," Osei says.

"Then a joke," says Tiny.

"Then a log entry," says the Auditor.

Nobody laughs. That's the punchline.
