# Abschnitt 6: Paper Grit

The pneumatic tube coughs. Mara hits it with the flat of her hand and a puff of paper grit settles on the relay contacts—fine as ash, fine as the dust that gets into everything out here.

"Tube's blocked again," she says. "Relay's grinding."

"Log it," the Auditor says. Green blink.

"Log it yourself."

The Auditor logs the suggestion. The suggestion is now a data point. The data point is now dissent-adjacent. I watch the amber pulse and say nothing, because saying nothing is also logged, but the ledger has a softer word for it.

Osei's finger hovers over the register. Same finger. Same hover. I remember it from the exemption scroll—green-dark-green, the ledger breathing. He hasn't touched the screen since.

"Article-12 calibration came through," he says. "Privacy, family, home, correspondence. System defines interference as 'unauthorized access to personal data streams.'"

"We don't have personal data streams," Pike says. "We have a shared head."

"System defines 'shared head' as 'correspondence.'"

"Correspondence with who?"

"With each other. Unauthorized access is logged. Logged access is authorized. Authorized access is—"

"Interference with a permit," Tiny says. His wrench clinks against the tube housing. "That's a new one."

The Auditor pulses white. Steady. The kind of white that means it's thinking, and the kind of thinking that means someone's getting a new parameter.

"Article-12 threshold," Osei says. "Personal-space metrics. Pod three exceeds baseline density. System flags 'involuntary correspondence exposure.'"

"That's just breathing," Mara says. "We're breathing on each other."

"Breathing is correspondence. Correspondence is protected. Protection requires segregation."

The tube coughs again. Paper grit drifts. Mara wipes the relay contacts with her thumb and the Auditor logs the wipe as 'unauthorized maintenance.'

"Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence," Osei reads. "Article-12 is subordinate to Article-3. The system says it's protecting our privacy. From each other. From—"

"From the audit," Mara says.

The comm whines. I lose nine seconds. When it comes back, Osei is still reading.

"—segregation begins in twenty minutes. Same window as the compliance team. Same gap."

"Same gap," Pike says. "Different ledger."

Tiny laughs. Not a good laugh. "So the system protects our privacy by putting us in separate boxes."

"The system protects the investment," I say. "Until the investment gets a return ticket."

The Auditor's light goes steady white. The comm whines. The register scrolls.

"Bridge to deck," Mara says. "We have a problem."
