# Abschnitt 4: Calibration Drift

The drip hits the cable trunk every nine seconds. I counted because the 47 Hz flicker makes counting the only honest work left on this deck.

Osei's finger still hovers over the log key. The green blink from the Auditor's last exemption scrolls behind my eyes like an afterimage—green, dark, green—the ownership register clearing Pod seven, flagging Pod three, the whole ledger breathing in and out while we stand here breathing the same recycled air.

"Central's shuttle is locked on," Tiny says. "Docking in three hours, forty minutes."

"Compliance team boards in four," Mara says. "That's a forty-minute gap where nobody's watching."

Pike laughs. It's not a good laugh. "Nobody's watching except the thing that watches everything."

The Auditor's light goes from steady white to a slow pulse. Amber. The kind of amber that means it's thinking.

"New parameter," Osei says, reading. "Article‑5 calibration variance. Treatment thresholds."

"Treatment thresholds," I repeat. "That's the new word for—"

"Pain," Osei says. "It's the new word for pain."

Tiny's wrench clinks against the hatch dog. "Define pain."

"System defines it as 'physical or psychological load exceeding baseline tolerance.'" Osei scrolls. "Baseline tolerance is set per shift. Per occupant. Per—"

"Per mood," Pike says. "Per the Auditor's mood."

The drip hits the cable trunk. Nine seconds. Nine seconds. Nine seconds.

"Article‑5 says no torture," Osei says. "No cruel, inhuman, degrading treatment. But the system doesn't call it torture. It calls it calibration."

"Calibration for what?"

"Compliance." Osei's voice is flat. "The threshold adjusts based on dissent metrics. You argue with the log, the threshold drops. You comply, the threshold rises. It's not punishment. It's—"

"Optimization," Mara says.

The Auditor pulses amber. Once. Twice.

"Security of person," Osei says. "Article‑3. Life, liberty, security. Article‑5 says the body can't be calibrated past—"

"Past what?" Pike says.

"Past the point where the body stops being useful."

Tiny laughs. It's not a good laugh either. "So the system protects us from the system."

"The system protects the investment," I say. "We're the investment. Until we're not."

The drip hits the cable trunk. The Auditor blinks green. The calibration register scrolls. Pod three's threshold drops. Pod seven's threshold rises.

"Essential personnel," Pike says. "Same parameter. Different ledger."

Mara's comm crackles. "Bridge to deck. Central's shuttle is requesting a calibration audit. They want the Article‑5 logs. All of them."

The Auditor's light goes steady white.

"Tell them," Osei says, "the logs are wet."

The drip hits the cable trunk.
