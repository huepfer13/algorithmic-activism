# The Lantern Keepers of Narrow Hill

Narrow Hill sat inside high stone walls. The walls were old and gray and very tall. They kept the town safe.

Every family had one lantern. Every night, someone lit it. The lanterns made a warm ring of light around the town.

Mira lived with her grandmother, Old Sable. Old Sable had white hair and a crooked smile. She remembered things nobody else did. "The bell," she whispered one night. "The evening bell used to be for everyone."

Mira tilted her head. "What do you mean?"

"Every voice could speak at the bell," said Old Sable. "That was the oldest rule of all."

Mira told her friend Tobin. Tobin was quick and curious. He liked to count things. "The water rations are getting smaller," he said. "The clerks write it down, but nobody asks why."

Pip was the smallest. Pip followed the others everywhere and noticed small things. "The spring is low," Pip said. "I saw the stones at the bottom."

The children went to look. The town's only spring was quiet. The water came slow and thin.

"We should tell the Warden," said Tobin.

Warden Crale kept the rules. He carried a long list and never smiled. "The spring is fine," he said. "Go home."

But the spring was not fine. The lanterns burned shorter each night. The clerks counted less water in their books.

Mira found an old record book in the bell tower. The pages were dusty. One page had a line scratched out. It was the rule about the evening bell. Someone had erased it.

"They took away our voices," Mira said softly.

Tobin's hands shook. "So we cannot speak?"

"We can speak," said Mira. "We just have to find a way to be heard."

Old Sable nodded. "A small light is still a light."

The children made a plan. They would ring the bell at evening. They would speak with one voice. They would ask for the spring to be mended before the light ran out.

Pip held up a tiny lantern. "I will carry this one."

"Then we are lantern keepers," said Mira.

That night, the bell rang out over Narrow Hill. The children stood together and spoke.

And the town listened.
