# Abschnitt 1: The Calibration of Whispers

The creature of shadow and smoke did not attack. It tilted its head, as though listening to something behind the three wanderers, and then it dissolved into the underbrush the way mist gives way to morning. Kael let out a breath he had not known he was holding, and Aria's hand found the hilt of her belt knife, though she did not draw it.

"Look," Elian said, and pointed at the ground where the creature had stood.

The symbols had changed. Where before they had been a single winding path, now they branched into three, each one etched deeper than the last, and at the fork a thin iron pipe emerged from the soil, half-swallowed by roots. It was a pneumatical line, old as the forest itself, and it was blocked. Elian crouched and brushed his fingers along the seam; a fine grey powder came away on his skin, paper-fine, the kind of grit that settles into relay contacts and silences them. Somewhere far down the pipe, a message had stopped moving.

"The forest speaks through this," he murmured. "And something has clogged its throat."

Aria laughed, short and sharp. "Then let it choke. We have a shadow to outrun."

But Kael was already kneeling, his broad hands working at the joint of the pipe, and he did not look up when he spoke. "My village has a saying. A blocked pipe is not a dead pipe. It is a pipe waiting for someone patient enough to clear it."

The three of them worked in silence for a while, and the whispering of the vine changed its register, no longer urgent but expectant, as though it had been waiting a very long time for hands to touch this iron throat. Elian scraped the powder from the relay with the edge of his trowel and felt the contact hum faintly under his thumb, a pulse like a heartbeat learning to beat again. Aria, who had sworn she would not help, found herself holding the pipe steady while Kael twisted the coupling loose.

When the line opened, no words came through. Only a breath of warm air, and then a single sound, low and rhythmic, that might have been a voice reciting something very old, or might have been the forest itself exhaling.

Kael sat back on his heels. "It was never about the shadow," he said. "The shadow was only the noise of a stopped thing trying to be heard."

Aria opened her mouth to answer, but the underbrush stirred again, and this time it was not smoke that moved between the trees but something with weight and footsteps, many of them, and they were coming closer.

Elian pressed his ear to the open pipe, and his face went pale. "There is a second line," he said. "And it is not blocked. It is counting."
