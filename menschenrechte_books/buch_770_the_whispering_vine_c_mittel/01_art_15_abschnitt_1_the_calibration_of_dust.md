# Abschnitt 1: The Calibration of Dust

The creature of shadow and smoke did not attack. It simply waited, its coal-bright eyes tracking each of them with the patience of stone, and Elian understood that the thing had asked its question and would now watch to see what they would do with it.

Kael moved first. He stepped between Aria and the creature, not with drama but with the plain economy of a farmer who had spent his life placing his body between livestock and storm. Aria opened her mouth, likely to object, and Elian caught her sleeve and held it. The whispers of the vine had changed their rhythm, quickening like a pulse, and in that quickening he heard something he had not heard before: a clicking, a grinding, as though some vast mechanism beneath the glade had seized.

He looked down. The symbols on the ground were not only symbols. Fine paper-dust lay in them, pale and gritty, drifted into the grooves like snow into furrows, and where the dust had settled thickest the lines had stopped humming. A blockage, he thought, though he could not have said what he meant by it. The forest's old relays, whatever they were, had choked on their own slow decay.

Kael did not turn. "Whatever it is," he said, "it wants us to choose."

"It wants us to fail," Aria said, and her voice was sharp, but her hand had found Kael's shoulder and stayed there.

The creature's form shifted, and for a moment Elian saw in it a face he almost knew, a face from his homeland, from the market square of his childhood, and then it was smoke again. He understood then what it was offering, and the understanding was cold. It would let them pass, one at a time, each of them named and claimed and bound to this place, and the binding would feel like belonging and would be a kind of theft. No one would be deprived by force. They would simply be denied the right to leave, and the denial would wear the mask of welcome.

"Together," Elian said. "We go together or not at all."

The dust in the grooves stirred, lifted, and the grinding beneath the glade fell silent. The creature tilted its head, and its coal-eyes dimmed, and it stepped aside, and the path beyond it opened into a darkness that smelled of rain and iron.

Aria drew breath to speak, and the vine whispered over her, and she closed her mouth and walked.
