# Abschnitt 1: The Clerk of the Pneumatic Line

The clerk in the basement of State,
Where the tubes and the relays gyrate,
Found a drift in the line
Where the paper-dust fine
Had silted the brass-bearing plate.

He had written a codex of rules,
With its margins and cross-indexed schools,
But the tube would not pass
Through the clogged-up morass
Of the dust that had settled like fools.

Now the parameter, marked as art_01,
Was a gauge that must never be done
With a drift or a bend,
For the life at the end
Of the line is the first of the run.

He tapped at the brass with his pen,
And the dust puffed and settled again;
"Born free and equal," he read
From a slip by his head,
"Yet the pressure is one-point-oh-ten."

The relay gave out a small cough,
And the paper-slips fluttered and dropped off;
He adjusted the screw,
For the life must come through,
And the dignity-gauge must not stop.

But a rattle came up from the floor,
Where the tube met the old iron door;
And the clerk saw the drift
Was a slow, silent shift
In the gauge he had trusted before.

He reached for the clamp and the seal,
For the physical must be the real;
But the dust in the line
Gave a whisper, a sign,
And the pressure began to reveal—

A fluctuation, faint and unplanned,
As the slip in his palm and his hand
Said the brotherhood clause
Had a technical pause,
And the metre alone could not stand.
