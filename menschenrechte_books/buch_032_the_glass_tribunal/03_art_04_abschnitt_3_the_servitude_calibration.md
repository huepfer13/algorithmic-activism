# Abschnitt 3: The Servitude Calibration

The overhead discharge lamps stuttered at forty-seven hertz, casting the witness rail in a flicker that made Sister Odile's veil appear to breathe. Petrov caught the ozone stink at the switchgear cabinet behind him—sharp, chemical, the smell of a relay burning through its insulation—and pressed his thumb against his temple.

"The tribunal will now calibrate Article Four," Kaine said. He had not replaced the folded glasses. The audit notice from the pneumatic tube lay open beside them, its crimson sleeve slit down the middle. "Servitude threshold. The Registry wants a measurable definition of 'held in slavery or servitude' as applied to the platform's detention protocol."

Mercer did not sit. He stood at the witness rail with one hand flat against the surface, the same posture he had held since the morning session opened. "Sister Odile. The intake interview. Did the child's movement within the facility require authorization?"

"It did."

"Was that authorization ever refused?"

"Twice. Once for the medical bay. Once for the exercise deck."

"And when authorization was refused, what was the child's status?"

Petrov stood. "Objection. The witness is being asked to characterize a lawful detention as servitude. The tribunal has already established the detention order's validity under the security-of-person metric."

"Overruled," Kaine said. "The calibration requires the characterization. Sister Odile."

The lamps flickered again. Ozone thickened. Sister Odile's hands emerged from her sleeves and folded on the rail.

"The child was not permitted to leave her cell block for forty-one hours," she said. "She was not permitted to communicate with her brother in Sector Two. She was not permitted to refuse the sedative administered at hour thirty-six. When she asked why, she was told the platform's life-support contract required her compliance."

Mercer turned a page. "Required her compliance. Those were the words used?"

"Those were the words used."

Petrov's jaw worked. "The life-support contract is a safety protocol. The tribunal cannot calibrate Article Four against a facility's operational constraints."

"Then what does the tribunal calibrate it against?" Voss asked. She had not risen. "The Registry's audit notice arrived at 04:47 this morning. It required the calibration methodology within the hour. That hour has passed. The engine is waiting."

Kaine's hand moved to the audit notice, then stopped. The tube system had pressurized again—the bench vibrated once, a low mechanical shudder, before settling. He did not open the new delivery.

"Article Four calibration," he said. "The threshold for servitude is defined as any restriction of movement, communication, or bodily autonomy imposed without the subject's consent and enforced by the facility's operational necessity. The engine will now process the witness testimony."

The lamps stuttered. The ozone sharpened. Sister Odile did not blink.

"And if the engine flags the platform itself?" she asked.

Kaine looked at the crimson sleeve on the bench. He did not answer.
