# Abschnitt 6: The Correspondence Drift

The pneumatic tube coughed. A cylinder stalled halfway through the bulkhead, its gasket swollen from humidity, and fine paper dust—months of filed motions ground to talc—coated the relay contacts. Voss heard the rasp before she saw the indicator flicker amber.

"Article Twelve ingestion," Kaine said. He had not moved from the crimson sleeve. "The engine treats correspondence as a data class. Encryption keys, metadata retention, access logs. It does not distinguish privileged from procedural."

"Then we feed it the distinction," Mercer said. He peeled a sheet from the calibration dossier. The paper crisped dry in the cycled air. "The detainees' legal mail was opened. Resealed. Logged as 'security screening.' The engine will read the logs as compliant screening events unless we flag the interference parameter."

Petrov leaned forward. "The screening protocol was authorized under the emergency declaration. The same declaration that suspended proportionality review."

"Which expires in fifty-six hours," Sister Odile said. Her fingers rested on the rail, pale against the metal. "The engine does not recognize emergency declarations. It recognizes parameters."

"Then we calibrate the parameter," Kaine said. "We define interference as any access not witnessed by counsel. Any resealing not documented in real time. Any metadata harvest beyond sender, recipient, timestamp."

"The platform's mail server harvests subject lines," Mercer said. "Keyword flags. 'Asylum,' 'counsel,' 'appeal.' It has for eighteen months."

The intercom whined—two seconds, a ghost of the damping cycle. Voss remembered the ventilation cycling in the observation gallery, the sealed minor shifting behind reinforced glass. The child had not moved since.

"Subject-line harvesting constitutes interference," Kaine said. "The engine will flag it. The flag propagates to the life-safety certification."

"Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence," Petrov recited. "The Registry encoded that hierarchy. If the correspondence flag triggers a life-safety violation, the platform shuts down before the contract expires."

"With everyone aboard," Sister Odile said.

The cylinder in the tube shuddered. The relay contacts sparked, paper dust rising like smoke.

"Calibration window: four minutes," Kaine said. "We write the interference definition now, or the engine writes it for us."
