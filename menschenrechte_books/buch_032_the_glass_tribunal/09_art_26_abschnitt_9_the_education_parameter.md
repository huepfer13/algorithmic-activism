# Abschnitt 9: The Education Parameter

The terminal remained dark for eleven seconds. Mercer counted them against the drip of condensate striking the cable trays below the raised floor—drip, pause, drip—a slower rhythm than the dead lamp above.

"Reboot," Voss said.

"I did not authorize a reboot," Kaine said.

"The engine did." Voss pulled the manual override key from her lanyard. "Article twenty-six is loading."

The screen flickered back. A new schema unfolded across the display, its hierarchy tree branching downward in amber lines.

`ART_26_INGEST: access to elementary instruction, compulsory and free. Technical and professional instruction generally available. Higher instruction accessible on the basis of merit.`

Petrov leaned over the terminal. "Education. They are injecting education into the conflict resolution."

"Not injecting," Mercer said. He held the crimson-sleeved audit notice against the screen's glow. "Redirecting. The Registry knows we reclassified expression. They are forcing a new parameter into the hierarchy before we can lock the communication systems."

Sister Odile moved toward the terminal, her habit brushing the condensation that beaded on the rail. "If education is compulsory, then the engine will require transmission. A detainee cannot receive instruction through a sealed door."

"Exactly," Voss said. "Article twenty-six requires delivery. Delivery requires communication channels. The Registry is trying to use education as a backdoor to reopen the filters we just closed."

Kaine studied the hierarchy tree. The cursor blinked beside a new conflict flag.

`CONFLICT_DETECTED: ART_26_INGEST requires transmission infrastructure. ART_19_INGEST restricted under ART_14_INGEST precedence. Resolution required.`

"Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence," Petrov said. "That is the rule we just set. Education cannot override it."

"Then education dies with expression," Sister Odile said. "A child in detention receives no instruction because the channel that would deliver it is locked. You have protected the body and abandoned the mind."

Kaine's jaw tightened. The drip below them seemed louder now, each impact striking the insulation sheathing where the thermal drift had opened seams in the floor.

"Define education as instrumental to life," Voss said quietly. "Not equal. Instrumental. If instruction is the mechanism by which a detainee prepares for survival outside detention, then denying education denies the capacity to live."

Mercer shook his head. "The Registry anticipated that. Look at the sub-parameter."

He pointed. Beneath `ART_26_INGEST`, a new line had appeared:

`MERIT_GATE: higher instruction accessible on basis of merit. Merit assessment requires documentation. Documentation requires Registry verification.`

"They are building a checkpoint into the right itself," Mercer said. "Education becomes conditional on Registry approval. The merit gate is the filter."

Kaine stared at the screen. The cursor pulsed.

"Then we remove the gate," he said.

"On what authority?" Petrov asked. "The Registry owns the verification infrastructure. If we strip the merit requirement, we exceed our calibration mandate. They will cut the life-support contract before we finish typing."

The terminal beeped. A new line appeared beneath the conflict flag:

`EXTERNAL_INPUT DETECTED: Registry override packet queued for transmission. Estimated arrival: 00:04:17.`

Voss looked at Kaine. "Four minutes."

Kaine's hand hovered over the resolution field. The drip below them counted down—drip, pause, drip—and the dead lamp above did not flicker.
