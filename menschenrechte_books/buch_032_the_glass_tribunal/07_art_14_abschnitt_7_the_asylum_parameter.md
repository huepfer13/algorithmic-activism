# Abschnitt 7: The Asylum Parameter

The relay contacts had stopped sparking eleven minutes ago. Voss still smelled the scorched paper dust. It clung to the ventilation intake, a fine grey film that the filters could not cycle out.

"Registry revision," Mercer said. He held the printout by its corner, as if the ink might transfer. "Central Registry announces an unscheduled audit. Effective immediately. All protocol filings must be submitted in duplicate, witnessed, and time-stamped within the hour."

Kaine's jaw tightened. "They are auditing a calibration window that has not closed."

"They are auditing the window because it has not closed." Mercer laid the sheet on the rail. "Someone upstream is nervous. The correspondence flag propagated faster than the mail server logs."

Petrov had not sat down since the adjournment. He stood at the observation glass, hands clasped behind his back. "The engine has already ingested the interference definition. If the Registry audit arrives before we finalize the asylum parameter, the engine will default to the pre-declaration hierarchy."

"Which means?" Sister Odile asked.

"Which means the engine will treat every pending asylum claim as a procedural irregularity. Not a right. An irregularity."

Voss pulled the calibration dossier toward her. The asylum parameter sat at the bottom of the schema, a blank field labeled `ART_14_INGEST` with three sub-parameters: `SEEK`, `ENJOY`, `PERSECUTION_DEF`. None were populated.

"The engine needs a threshold," she said. "At what point does a claim of persecution register as valid before the platform's jurisdiction?"

"Persecution is not a threshold," Petrov said. "It is a finding of fact. The engine cannot find facts."

"The engine can weigh corroboration," Kaine said. "Witness statements, country-of-origin data, detention records. Define persecution as any documented harm meeting the Registry's severity index."

"And if the documentation is incomplete?" Sister Odile asked. "If the harm is verbal, psychological, structural? The severity index was written for physical torture."

Kaine was silent for two seconds. "Then we calibrate the index."

"The audit will flag any mid-window recalibration," Mercer said. "They will see the change and ask why."

"Let them ask." Kaine turned to the terminal. "Populate `PERSECUTION_DEF` with the expanded index. Flag the audit as a procedural objection. The engine will hold the flag until the Registry responds."

Petrov shook his head. "The engine will not hold. Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence. If the audit triggers a life-safety review, the platform shuts down before the Registry responds."

"Then we do not trigger the review." Voss typed the first sub-parameter. `SEEK: any expressed intent to remain, logged by counsel or witness.` "We define seeking as expression. Not documentation. Not proof. Expression."

The terminal blinked. A new line appeared beneath the field: `AUDIT_FLAG: CENTRAL_REGISTRY_REVISION_PENDING.`

"Seventeen minutes," Mercer said. "Before the audit window closes."

Sister Odile touched the rail. "And the child in the gallery? Her claim is not logged. She has not spoken."

"Then she is not seeking," Petrov said.

"She is seven years old," Voss said. "She does not know the word."

Kaine looked at the terminal. The cursor pulsed beside `ENJOY`. "Populate it. Any sustained presence under tribunal protection. The child qualifies."

The terminal accepted the input. A second later, the screen went dark.
