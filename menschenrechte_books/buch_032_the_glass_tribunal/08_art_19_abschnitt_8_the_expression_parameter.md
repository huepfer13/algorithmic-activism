# Abschnitt 8: The Expression Parameter

The relay bay smelled of ozone and scorched dust. One of the discharge lamps above the calibration terminal stuttered at forty-seven hertz, a flicker that turned Kaine's face into a sequence of still frames.

"Article nineteen," Voss said. She pulled the calibration dossier from beneath the terminal's cooling vent. "The Registry audit flagged the asylum parameter. It did not flag expression. That means we have room."

"Room for what?" Petrov asked. He had finally sat, but his hands remained flat on the rail, as if ready to push away.

"Expression is already in the engine's hierarchy. But it is filed under communications protocol, not under protection of life." Voss tapped the schema. "If we reclassify expression as a life-safety parameter, the engine cannot shut it down during a review."

Kaine leaned forward. "You want to elevate speech to the same tier as physical integrity."

"I want to define what happens when speech is the only thing keeping someone alive." Voss typed. `ART_19_INGEST: any transmission, reception, or retention of information by a protected person.`

The lamp above her flickered again. The ozone sharpened.

Mercer unfolded a second printout. "The audit notice arrived this morning. Crimson sleeve instead of gray. Central Registry does not use crimson for routine revisions. That color means a priority override."

"Then they know we are recalibrating," Sister Odile said.

"They suspect." Mercer set the sheet down. "The pneumatic tube logged the sleeve at 04:47. If they are tracking our terminal inputs, they will see the expression parameter within minutes."

Petrov stood. "If expression becomes a life-safety parameter, the engine will hold every censorship request for review. Every border filter. Every content restriction. The platform's communication systems will lock."

"Then they lock," Kaine said.

"And if the Registry responds by cutting the life-support contract?"

Kaine did not answer. The lamp buzzed.

Voss typed the final sub-parameter: `IMPART: any act of transmitting information to a person outside the detention perimeter, including legal filings, testimony, and personal correspondence.`

The terminal accepted the input. A new line appeared: `CONFLICT_DETECTED: ART_19_INGEST exceeds ART_14_INGEST severity threshold. Resolution required.`

"Resolution required," Mercer read. "The engine is asking which right takes precedence."

"Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence," Petrov said. "That is the hierarchy. That is always the hierarchy."

"Then expression dies," Sister Odile said. "If a detainee cannot speak, cannot file, cannot send word to anyone outside this platform, then the protection of life is a locked room with no windows."

Kaine looked at the terminal. The cursor pulsed beside the resolution field. "Define expression as instrumental to life. Not equal. Instrumental. If speech is the mechanism by which a person seeks protection, then restricting speech restricts the right to seek protection."

Voss typed: `RESOLUTION: ART_19_INGEST subordinate to ART_14_INGEST when expression is the sole available mechanism for seeking protection.`

The terminal paused. The lamp flickered. Then the screen went dark.
