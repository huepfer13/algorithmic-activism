# Abschnitt 4: The Degradation Threshold

Condensate struck the cable trays in a metronome that had nothing to do with the discharge lamps. Forty-seven hertz above, a slower rhythm below—drip, pause, drip—each impact darkening the insulation sheathing where the thermal drift had opened the raised floor's seams. The damp cold climbed the witness rail's bolts.

Mercer had not moved from the rail. Voss remembered him there when she entered the chamber three sessions past, already standing while she chose her bench, the same flat-handed stillness. He turned a page in the calibration dossier. The paper did not crisp; the air had thickened past paper's tolerance.

"Article Five calibration," Kaine said. The crimson sleeve remained unopened beside his hand. "The Registry defines the degradation threshold as any procedure whose cumulative physiological impact exceeds the platform's baseline metabolic allowance for a detained subject. The engine will now ingest Sister Odile's testimony."

Petrov's chair scraped. "The baseline metabolic allowance was calibrated for crew rotation cycles. Not for children held in Sector Two."

"The engine does not distinguish categories," Voss said. She had not risen then either. "It processes parameters. The sedative at hour thirty-six. The forty-one-hour confinement. The communication denial. Each parameter carries a metabolic cost. The engine sums them."

"Then the engine indicts the platform," Sister Odile said. Her veil did not flicker this time. The lamps had steadied. "The life-support contract is the platform. If compliance with the contract requires the very procedures the engine flags as degrading, the platform flags itself."

Silence, save the dripping.

"The protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence," Kaine said. "That is the hierarchy the Registry encoded. The engine will apply it."

"And if precedence requires the platform's shutdown?" Mercer asked. His voice did not rise. "The contract expires in fifty-eight hours. The engine's verdict arrives in twelve."

Kaine's fingers closed on the crimson sleeve. The tube system pressurized—a shudder through the bench, the rail, the floor plates beneath their feet. The dripping stopped.

The new delivery was not an audit notice.
