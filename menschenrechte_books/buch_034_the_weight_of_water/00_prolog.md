# The Weight of Water

The Cistern clung to the cliff like a swallow's nest, and like a swallow's nest it was built of what the wind had not taken. Its people measured their days in cupfuls. The well lay at the settlement's heart, a throat of stone that swallowed rope and returned water, and beside it stood the Counter's table, where every drop was tallied before it passed a threshold.

Mara kept the ledger. She had the hands of a woman who had once held softer things—a brush, perhaps, or a child's fingers—and now held only numbers. Each morning she drew the rope, and each evening she wrote what remained, and the writing was the truest prayer she knew.

Teo carried the buckets. He was young and quick and believed, in the way of the young, that speed could outrun scarcity. He had a habit of whistling at the well's mouth, a small defiance, and Old Sable, who had seen three droughts and buried a brother in the second, would say, "The well does not like music. It gives less when it is sung to."

Juniper sat on the council and rotated with the seasons, as was the custom. She had a voice like a closing door and a mind that could hold two truths without letting them touch. She counted the cistern's people each dawn, and each dawn the number was the same, and each dawn she feared it would not be.

The Counter was not a person but a rule made flesh—whoever held the tally-stick held the settlement's judgment, and the stick passed from hand to hand, and no hand was permitted to keep it long.

Then the drought came, not as a thief but as a guest who stays. The well's breath grew shallow. Mara's ledger showed a line that fell like a bird struck mid-flight. Teo whistled less. Old Sable sat by the well's mouth and spoke to it in a voice too low to hear, and Juniper counted the people twice each dawn and told no one.

And on the seventh day of the failing water, a stranger climbed the cliff path with empty hands and a dry tongue and stood before the Counter's table and said, "I will drink, but I will not be counted."

The Counter's stick trembled in the hand that held it.

The well waited.

*And the settlement learned that a cup withheld is also a cup poured, and that the hand which measures must first decide what it is measuring.*
