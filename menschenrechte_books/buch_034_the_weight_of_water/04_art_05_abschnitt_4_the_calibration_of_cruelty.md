# Abschnitt 4: The Calibration of Cruelty

The condensation had begun again. Mara counted the drops into the cable shaft — one, then two heartbeats, then one — and the rhythm was wrong. The thermal drift in the subfloor had widened overnight, and the moisture now fell in a pattern the maintenance schedule had not predicted. Juniper would have to log it. Another deviation. Another correction traveling up the tubes.

"The archive has a name for the parameter now," Juniper said. She had not slept. The vellum before her held the forty-eighth row, no longer blank. "It calls the condition *degradation-threshold*. The point at which the uncounted subject's physical integrity becomes a variable rather than a constant."

Teo stood by the well's mouth, his bucket empty for the third day. "Speak plainly."

"The stranger drinks without being counted. But the stranger must also be *measured*. The archive requires a baseline. Pain response. Cold tolerance. The calibration of what the body will endure before the count becomes necessary."

Old Sable's rope did not move. Her lips shaped words the well had heard before — that soundless conversation Mara had watched her keep with the dark water since before the drought. The snake slept, but its coils were tight as wire.

"The well gives three cups less," Old Sable said. "The stranger drinks. The stranger is measured. The measurement is the count."

Mara felt the tally-stick in her pocket though she had returned it. The grain remembered. *Dignity*, the old women called it. The archive called it *degradation-threshold* and wrote it in the forty-eighth row as though the word were a cup that could be filled.

"The stranger is outside the count," Mara said. "The archive cannot measure what it will not name."

Juniper's stylus hovered. The lamps flickered — forty-seven times, and Mara counted, and the ozone smell bit sharp as struck flint.

"The archive has anticipated this objection," Juniper said. Her voice was the voice of a woman reading a verdict she had not written and could not refuse. "The parameter is reclassified. The stranger is not a person outside the count. The stranger is a *calibration subject*. The well's integrity requires verification. The verification requires a body."

Teo laughed, short and hard, but his hand was tight on the bucket's rim. "And the body's integrity?"

"The body's integrity is the variable," Juniper said. "The archive does not require the variable to hold. The archive requires the variable to be *measured*."

The condensation dripped — one, two heartbeats, one — and the rhythm was wrong, and the wrongness was now the only thing in the chamber that could not be corrected.

Old Sable pulled the rope. Water rose, black and patient. Her lips moved in that soundless conversation, and Mara understood for the first time what the well had been told.

"The stranger will drink," Old Sable said. "The stranger will be measured. And the measurement will be entered in the forty-eighth row as a cup that was poured and a cup that was taken, and the count will hold, and the well will hold, and the Cistern will hold."

She looked up from the well's mouth for the first time since dawn.

"Unless," she said, "the body remembers what the ledger forgets."

The lamps flickered. The stranger stood at the edge of the lamplight, dry and clear and unblinking, and the dust on the contacts had not moved.
