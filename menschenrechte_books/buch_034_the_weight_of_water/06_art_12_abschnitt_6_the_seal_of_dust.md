# Abschnitt 6: The Seal of Dust

The pneumatic tube shuddered. A cylinder jammed halfway, its gasket swollen from seasons of damp breath. Mara knelt beside the relay bank, fingers finding the familiar grit — paper dust, fine as bone ash, coating the contacts. She brushed it clear. The cylinder sighed forward, clicked home.

Inside: a single strip of vellum. Not a tally. A name.

*Elias Venn. Row forty-eight.*

Juniper's stylus lay snapped beside the ledger. The forty-seventh row ended mid-sentence, ink feathering into the fibers where her hand had jerked. The forty-eighth waited. A white wound, as the archive called it. Now filled.

Teo read over her shoulder. His bucket still hung empty. "Venn. The merchant caravan. Three winters gone."

"Four," Old Sable said. Her voice came from the shadows where the snake watched. "He drank at the well's edge. Uncounted. The Counter of that year wrote *transit* and left the column blank."

The stranger — Venn — stood motionless in the lamplight. The dust on his shoulders did not move. But his throat worked again. Another swallow. The sound of a body that remembered water, and the law that protected the remembering.

Mara turned the vellum strip. On the reverse, a single line in the Counter's cramped hand:

*Parameter twelve: the interior life is not a column. The name enters. The measure follows. The remainder belongs to the well.*

The tube whined. Thirty-two hertz. The same frequency as the stranger's first swallow.

Juniper's fingers closed on the broken stylus. "The archive distinguishes exile from detention by duration. It does not distinguish them by cause. But this —" She tapped the vellum. "This distinguishes the *person* from the *parameter*. The ledger cannot hold both."

Old Sable's rope creaked. The snake's head lifted, tongue tasting air thick with dust and deferred thirst.

Venn took another step. The contacts stirred. The cylinder in the tube began to rise, pulled by suction toward the surface council, carrying the name that had no row, the life that preceded the count.
