# Abschnitt 3: The Parameter of Servitude

The calibration slip lay on the stone table like a splinter of ice that would not melt. Juniper read it twice, then a third time, her stylus hovering above the forty-eighth row. The archive's language was not the language of the Cistern. It spoke of precedence and parameters, of physical integrity and uncounted subjects. It did not speak of cups.

The lamps above the council alcove flickered — forty-seven times in a breath, Mara had learned to count it — and the ozone smell bit at the back of her throat, sharp as a struck flint. Teo's bucket clanged against the wall as he set it down. Empty. The well had given three cups less at dawn, and the audit would notice, and the notice would travel up the pneumatic tubes to the Central Registry where names became numbers and numbers became rations.

"The parameter is active," Juniper said. Her voice was flat, the voice of a woman reading a verdict she had not written. "Life-precedence. The stranger drinks without being counted until the physical integrity is confirmed."

Old Sable's rope went still. The snake slept, but its coils were tight.

"And if the integrity is never confirmed?" Teo asked.

Juniper did not answer. The forty-eighth row remained white.

Mara felt the tally-stick in her pocket, though she had returned it to the tube. The grain had carried something the ledger had no column for — she had felt it in the stranger's grip, three heartbeats, no more, and the wood had remembered. Now the archive had named it. *Uncounted.* The eighth notch, worn smooth. Dignity, the old women called it. But the Founders' script was older than the virtues, and it had a different word for the same thing: *servitude*. Not the state of being owned, but the state of being *outside the count* — the one whose breath could not be verified, whose cup could not be tallied, whose body was a parameter rather than a person.

The stranger stood at the edge of the lamplight. Dry. Clear. Unblinking. The dust on the contacts had not moved.

"The audit requires a name for the uncounted," Juniper said. "A designation. The archive will not accept a blank."

Teo laughed, short and hard. "Then give it one. Give it the stranger's name and be done."

"The stranger has no name in the ledger," Mara said. "The stranger has no row."

The lamps flickered again. Ozone. The smell of something burning that should not burn.

Old Sable spoke for the first time since dawn. "The well gives three cups less," she said. "The audit will notice. The parameter will hold. And the stranger will drink, and the stranger will not be counted, and the count will fail, and the failure will travel up the tubes to the Registry, and the Registry will send a correction."

She pulled the rope. Water rose, black and patient.

"Unless," she said, "the parameter is reclassified."

Juniper's stylus touched the vellum. The forty-eighth row waited.
