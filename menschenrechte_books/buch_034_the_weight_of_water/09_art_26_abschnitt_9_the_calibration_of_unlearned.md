# Abschnitt 9: The Calibration of Unlearned Hands

The vellum took the stylus like a vein takes a needle. Juniper wrote: *Parameter Twenty-Six — active. Transmission cycle: continuous. Recipients: all hands below the age of majority.* The ledger's margin already held the Registry's amendment, stamped in red ink that smelled of iron: *Compulsory. Universal. Merit-blind.*

Drip. Drip. Drip.

The condensation fell from the subfloor's thermal drift, each drop striking the cable sheathing with a sound like a stylus hitting vellum. Rhythmically. Forty-seven drops per minute. Mara had counted once, the night the intercoms sang at forty-seven hertz for three hours straight — the damping field's complaint about something moving in deep rock. She did not count now.

"Compulsory," Teo said. He stood at the well's lower tap, bucket empty, watching the water rise slow as breath. "Means the Council chooses who learns the pump. Who learns the ledger. Who learns the gate."

"Means the Registry chooses," Old Sable corrected. Her fingers traced the armrest's worn grooves. "We only implement. The same way we implemented the stranger's uncounted drink."

Venn moved then — first movement since the cylinder rose. He crossed to the relay bank, dust falling from his shoulders like ash. "Parameter Twenty-Six has a sub-clause. Technical and professional education made generally available." He tapped the switchgear. "The well is technical. The ledger is professional. The gate is both."

Juniper's stylus paused. "The stranger has no apprenticeship. No calibration. No merit the Registry would recognize."

"The Registry is not here," Mara said. She touched the relay contacts, feeling pressure build behind clean metal. "The stranger is. He drank. He lives. His hands are unlearned."

Drip. Drip. Drip.

The feet on the walkways above had stopped. Silence pressed against the chamber walls, heavier than the ozone.

"Protection of life," Venn said quietly. "Takes precedence."

He threw the master switch.
