# Abschnitt 1: The Calibration of Thirst

Mara's fingers found the tally-stick before the Council could breathe. The wood was warm from the stranger's grip — three heartbeats, no more — and the grain carried something the ledger had no column for. She carried it to the table where the pneumatic tube waited, its brass mouth yawning toward the deep archive. The tube had not accepted a message in four days. Fine paper dust coated the relay contacts, a gray fuzz that caught the lamplight when she tilted the stick. She blew across the contacts once, twice. The dust did not move. It had the patience of stone.

Teo stood at her shoulder, bucket empty, whistle silenced. "The well gave three cups less today," he said. "Three."

Old Sable did not look up from the well's mouth. His lips moved in that soundless conversation he kept with the dark water. The rope lay coiled beside him like a sleeping snake.

Juniper entered the circle last, her count complete. Forty-seven souls. Forty-seven cups. The stranger made forty-eight, but the ledger had no row for the uncounted. The tally-stick in Mara's hand bore the calibration marks of the Founders — seven notches for the seven virtues, they said, but the eighth notch was worn smooth. That notch had always been called *dignity*. The Counter's rule held that when the stick passed to a new hand, the calibration must be verified. The pneumatic tube would carry the verification to the archive. The tube was blocked.

"The stranger drinks," Juniper said. Her voice was the closing door. "Or the stranger leaves. The calibration does not bend."

Mara looked at the dust on the contacts. She looked at the stranger's throat working without water. The tally-stick pulsed in her palm — not wood, not quite. Alive with the weight of what it measured.

She raised the stick to the tube's mouth. The relay contacts waited, gray and patient.

The stranger's eyes found hers. Dry. Clear. Unblinking.
