# Abschnitt 7: The Calibration of Strangers

The cylinder rose through the tube with a sound like a held breath released. Mara watched it go — the vellum strip curling inside, the name of Elias Venn traveling upward toward the council chamber where the Counter waited.

"Revision," Juniper said. She had not moved from the ledger. Her broken stylus lay where it had snapped, but her hand now held a fresh one, and she was writing fast, her letters cramped and leaning. "Central Registry. They've announced an audit. Four days."

The relay bank clicked — a rhythm Mara knew, the sound of protocols being filed too quickly, gaskets forced past their tolerance. Somewhere above, feet moved across the cliffside walkways with a haste that had nothing to do with thirst.

"The lamps," Old Sable said. "Forty-seven flickers. You counted them, Mara. The night the tube first spoke."

Mara nodded. The ozone smell returned to her memory sharp as struck flint. "The archive knew before we did."

Teo set his bucket down. The empty sound of it against stone was a small verdict. "What does the Registry want?"

"Calibration," Juniper said. She turned the ledger toward the light. "Parameter fourteen. It's been dormant for six years — since the Venn transit. The Registry flags dormant parameters. They'll want the column reconciled."

Venn stood at the edge of the lamplight. His dust-covered shoulders had begun to tremble, not with cold. "I did not ask to be counted," he said. "I asked to drink."

"And the well provided," Old Sable said. "The well always provides. That is the problem."

Mara crossed to the relay bank. The contacts were clean now, but she could feel the pressure building in the tube — more cylinders coming, more protocols, the Registry's appetite for order arriving in pneumatic bursts. She thought of the forty-eighth row. The white wound. The name that had filled it.

"The audit will ask what we did with him," she said. "Not what the well did. What *we* did."

Juniper's stylus stopped. "Then we tell them the truth. He entered. The measure followed. The remainder belongs to the well."

"The Registry does not recognize remainders," Old Sable said. "The Registry recognizes columns. And parameter fourteen has no column."

The tube whined. Thirty-two hertz. Venn's throat worked — that remembered swallow, the body's argument with the law that protected it.

Teo picked up his bucket. "Then we make one," he said. "Before they arrive."

Juniper looked at the ledger, at the forty-eighth row where Venn's name now sat, at the broken stylus beside it. "The archive distinguishes exile from detention by duration," she said slowly. "It does not distinguish them by cause. But the Registry will."

She reached for a fresh vellum strip. Her hand did not shake.
