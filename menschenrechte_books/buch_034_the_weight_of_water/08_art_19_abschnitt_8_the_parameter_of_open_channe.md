# Abschnitt 8: The Parameter of Open Channels

The vellum strip fed into the tube, and the tube swallowed it with the sound of a door closing in another room. Juniper's fresh stylus moved across the ledger's margin, marking the time, the pressure, the name of the protocol she had just filed: *Parameter Nineteen — dormant since the second drought.*

"Open channels," she said, reading from the calibration sheet the Registry had sent ahead. "Information to be sought, received, imparted. Through any medium. Across any frontier." She looked up. "The Registry flags it dormant. They will want to know why we stopped listening."

Mara stood at the relay bank. The lamps above the switchgear flickered — forty-seven times, then held steady — and the ozone smell bit at her nostrils, sharp as a struck match. She had counted the flickers once before, the night the tube first spoke. She did not count them now.

"We never stopped listening," Teo said. He had filled his bucket from the well's lower tap, the one the council kept for the sick and the very young. "We stopped *transmitting*. There is a difference."

"The Registry does not recognize the difference," Old Sable said. She sat in her chair by the dead lamp, her hands folded. "The Registry recognizes columns. Parameter nineteen has no column either. It has a *gate*. And the gate has been shut since—" She paused. "Since before Juniper entered the circle. Forty-seven souls, she counted, and the gate closed behind the forty-seventh."

Juniper's stylus did not stop. "I remember," she said. "I counted twice. The second count was for the gate."

Venn stood apart from them, his dust-covered shoulders still. He had not spoken since the cylinder rose. Now he said: "The right to seek and receive. Does it extend to a stranger who asks for water?"

"The stranger asked to drink," Old Sable said. "The stranger did not ask to know."

"He asked to be uncounted," Mara said. "That is a kind of asking."

The tube whined. Thirty-two hertz. Then a second sound beneath it — lower, slower, the sound of a gasket being forced past tolerance somewhere deep in the cliff. Juniper's hand paused over the ledger.

"That is not a protocol," she said. "That is a *recall*."

Teo set his bucket down. "Recall of what?"

"Of the gate," Juniper said. She turned the ledger toward the light. "Parameter nineteen is not dormant because we stopped listening. It is dormant because the Registry closed it. And the Registry is reopening it now — not to hear us. To hear *through* us."

Mara crossed to the relay bank. The contacts were clean, but she could feel the pressure building behind them, the Registry's appetite for order arriving in pneumatic bursts. She thought of the forty-eighth row. The white wound. The name that had filled it.

"The audit will ask what we did with him," she said. "Not what the well did. What *we* did."

"And what did we do?" Venn asked.

No one answered. The lamps flickered — forty-seven times — and the ozone smell sharpened, and somewhere above, feet moved across the cliffside walkways with a haste that had nothing to do with thirst.

Juniper reached for a fresh vellum strip. Her hand did not shake.
