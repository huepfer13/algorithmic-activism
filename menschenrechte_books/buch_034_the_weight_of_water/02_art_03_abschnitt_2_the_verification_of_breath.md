# Abschnitt 2: The Verification of Breath

The tube swallowed the tally-stick with a sound like a held breath released. Brass teeth gripped wood. The pneumatic pulse shuddered through the wall, carrying the calibration toward the deep archive where the Founders' ledgers slept in nitrogen chill. Mara's fingers tingled where the stranger's warmth had lingered — three heartbeats, the rule said, but the grain remembered longer.

Teo's whistle stayed silent. His bucket hung crooked from the yoke, empty as the tube's promise. "Central Registry," he muttered, not to her. "Audit notice came with the dawn watch. They'll count the cups against the names. Every name."

Old Sable's rope twitched. The snake woke.

Juniper's ledger lay open on the stone table, forty-seven rows inked in the careful hand of the previous Counter. The forty-eighth row waited, a white wound in the vellum. She did not pick up her stylus. The audit accelerated everything — protocols filed in half the time, signatures witnessed by three instead of two, the verification cycle compressed from moon to day. The stranger's throat worked without water. Mara had seen it. The dust on the contacts had not moved. She had seen that too.

The tube coughed. A cylinder returned, hot from the archive's depth. Inside: a single slip of vellum, the Founders' script sharp as flint.

*CALIBRATION ACCEPTED. PARAMETER LIFE-PRECEDENCE ACTIVE. SUBJECT UNCOUNTED. VERIFICATION PENDING PHYSICAL INTEGRITY CONFIRMATION.*

The words were not in the seven virtues. They were older. Deeper. The eighth notch, worn smooth, had always been called dignity — but the archive named it something else. The stranger's eyes found hers again. Dry. Clear. Unblinking. The well gave three cups less today. The audit would notice.

Old Sable pulled the rope. Water rose, black and patient.
