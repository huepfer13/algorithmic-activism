# Abschnitt 1: The Calibration of Whispers

The creature of shadow and smoke did not attack. It waited, and waiting was worse.

Elian felt the pressure first—a faint grit beneath his fingertips where he had braced his hand against a fallen trunk. He looked down and saw it: a pale dust, fine as milled bone, clinging to the bark in a line that ran upward toward a rusted iron bracket. A pipe, no wider than his thumb, had been bolted to the tree long ago, and its seam had split. Something had jammed inside it—a wad of paper, swollen and grey—and the dust had sifted out through the crack and settled onto the relays beneath, a small brass box with contacts that no longer clicked cleanly but dragged, sluggish and choked.

"The old post-line," Aria said, following his gaze. "Someone stopped it up."

Kael pressed his ear to the pipe and listened, then drew back with a frown. "There is a message in there. It has been trying to arrive for a very long time."

The shadow-creature tilted its coal-bright eyes toward the pipe, and for a moment its shifting form held still. "The forest keeps its elections in strange ballots," it said. "Every voice that travels these lines must pass through the same narrow throat. When one throat is blocked, all voices wait behind it. No voice is counted until the throat is cleared."

Elian drew his knife and worked the blade gently under the swollen paper, and the wad came free with a wet sucking sound. The dust puffed into the air between them, and the brass contacts beneath it shivered, then snapped shut with a clean, hard click that echoed up the pipe and away into the dark. Far off, deep in the forest, something answered—a single note, low and resonant, like a bell struck once under water.

"There," Elian said. "It moves again."

But the creature had not moved, and its voice had changed. "You have cleared one throat," it said. "Now clear the other. The vine has been whispering to the three of you since the glade, and each of you has heard a different instruction. Speak them aloud, all at once, and see whether the forest counts three voices or only one."

Aria's jaw tightened. "And if we speak and the vine counts only the loudest?"

"Then the loudest will govern," the creature said, "and the other two will be governed. That is how the forest has always chosen—by whoever is heard first and clearest. It has never once asked whether the silent ones consented."

Kael looked at the pipe, then at his companions, and his hand found the rusted bracket and gripped it. "Then we do not speak at once," he said slowly. "We speak in turn, and each of us waits to be heard before the next begins. Let the vine learn a new procedure."

The creature's eyes dimmed, and the shadows around it drew in close, as if listening for a rule it had never been given. The whispers of the vine rose, tangled and insistent, and beneath them the pipe hummed with the message it had carried too long.

Elian opened his mouth to speak first, and the forest leaned in to count him.
