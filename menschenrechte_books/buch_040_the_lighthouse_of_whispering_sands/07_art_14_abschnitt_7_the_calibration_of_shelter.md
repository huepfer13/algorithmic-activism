# Abschnitt 7: The Calibration of Shelter

Mira descended the spiral stair. Her hand brushed the cool stone. Pip walked beside her. His lantern painted circles on the winding wall.

The great ledger room glowed with lamplight. Old Sela sat at the long table. Her fingers moved fast over the pages. Ink stained her fingertips blue. She did not look up.

"Central Registry comes tomorrow," she said. Her voice was steady. "Every file must be perfect."

Tobin stood by the window. He watched the tide kiss the sea wall. The water rose higher than last moon. His shoulders were tight. "The audit checks Parameter Fourteen," he said. "Shelter protocols. Entry codes. Safe harbor logs."

Mira felt a small knot in her stomach. Worry. Quiet and cold. She remembered the blue ink. *The water tastes sweet today.* A child's hand. A mother far away.

"Parameter Fourteen guards the door," Pip said. He had memorized the code. "It opens for those who flee danger. It closes for none who need."

Sela's pen scratched. Scratch. Scratch. "The ledger records every name. Every date. Every reason." She turned a page. "Brann's bucket still catches the drip in the lantern room. Three drops per minute. He measured it last week."

Mira nodded. She remembered the Captain on the ladder. The small tin bucket. The steady plink of water. Care in small things.

"The audit fears gaps," Tobin said. He turned from the window. His face was pale. "Gaps mean lost rights. Lost rights mean turned backs."

Pip lifted his chin. "We do not turn backs," he said. "We calibrate."

Mira approached the ledger. She saw columns for names. Columns for origins. Columns for the danger fled. A blank row waited at the bottom. Fresh paper. Ready ink.

"Parameter Fourteen active," she whispered. She touched the blank line. "Shelter priority: Life. Integrity. First."

The bell rope shuddered in its housing. A low hum vibrated through the floorboards. The lighthouse light swept the dark water. Once. Twice. Three times.

A knock sounded at the heavy oak door. Three sharp raps. Pause. Two more.

Sela froze. Her pen hovered. The ink held its breath.
