# Abschnitt 1: The Bell and the Dust

The lighthouse bell hung still. Mira climbed the stairs to look at it.

Fine white dust covered the bell's relay contacts. It looked like sand, but softer. It came from the old paper tubes that ran through the walls.

"The tube is stuck," said Tobin behind her. He poked the brass fitting. "The message from the north lane never arrived."

Mira wiped the contacts with her sleeve. The bell stayed quiet. "So the bell cannot watch today."

"Then nobody knows the rules," said Tobin.

Old Sela came up the stairs, slow and steady. She carried the ledger under one arm. "The bell is not the rule," she said. "The bell only reminds us."

Pip squeezed past her legs. "The old stories are in the tower room. I saw them once."

They found the stories in a wooden chest. The pages smelled of salt. Sela read one aloud. It told of a time when the islanders drew water together and shared the last dry ground.

Captain Brann waited below. "Stories do not fill buckets," he said. But he listened anyway.

Mira held up the ledger. "The old rule says the well gives water. The well is salt. So we need a new rule that keeps everyone safe."

Sela nodded. "A rule must protect every person. No one may go thirsty while another has water."

Tobin counted on his fingers. "Three buckets for the north lane. One for the boat. One for the sick."

"Two for the sick," said Mira. "And we carry water from the rain barrels first."

Pip raised a hand. "The rain barrels are behind the bell rope. Nobody looks there."

They went down together. The rain barrels were full. The dust from the paper tubes lay on the handles, soft and white.

Captain Brann lifted the first bucket. "I will fix the tube," he said. "A bell that cannot speak is no good to anyone."

Sela wrote in the ledger with her slow, sharp hand. Mira watched the words appear.

The bell above them stayed silent. But the village moved.

**Ledger entry, second night: When the bell is dust, the hands must speak.**
