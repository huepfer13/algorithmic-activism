# Abschnitt 6: The Dust in the Dial

Mira climbed the spiral stair. Her boots made soft thuds on the stone. Pip followed close. His lantern swung a golden circle on the wall.

The speaking tube waited at the landing. Its brass mouth gaped like a sleeping bird. Mira remembered the whine. A sound cut through the tower. A high thin whine. It had bitten the air last watch.

She knelt. Her fingers found the relay box. Fine powder coated the contacts. White as flour. Soft as chalk. Paper dust. The pneumatic tube had chewed a message. The paper had shredded inside the pipe.

"Blocked," Mira said. Her voice was calm. "The tube ate the letter."

Pip set the lantern down. Light pooled on the metal. "Parameter twelve," he whispered. He had heard Sela read it. "No one reads the mail but the name on the front."

Mira brushed the contacts with her sleeve. The dust fell away. The brass shone dull. "The wire carries words," she said. "Words belong to the sender. And the receiver."

She lifted the tube cap. A scrap of paper lay wedged in the throat. Blue ink. A child's hand. *Dear Mama, The water tastes sweet today.*

Mira's throat tightened. She felt a sting behind her eyes. Sadness. Sharp and quiet. She did not pull the paper free. She did not read further.

"The message waits," she said. "The tube holds it safe."

Pip nodded. His face was serious. "We guard the path," he said. "Not the story."

Mira replaced the cap. She wiped the last dust from the relay. The contacts clicked. A green light blinked on the panel. *Parameter twelve: Channel clear. Privacy intact.*

The bell rope trembled once. A single deep note rolled down the stair.
