# Abschnitt 5: The Whine in the Wire

The bucket under the drip was half full. The plink was steady. Mira watched the water rise. She counted each drop. One. Two. Three.

A sound cut through the tower. A high thin whine. It came from the speaking tube on the wall. The tube was broken. But the wire inside still lived.

Tobin flinched. His pen left a jagged mark on the ledger page. "The signal," he said. "It bites."

Brann climbed down the ladder fast. He pressed his ear to the tube mouth. The whine rose. It fell. It rose again. "Interference," he said. "The damp eats the contacts."

Mira remembered the white dust. Fine and soft. It had covered the bell's relay contacts. She had wiped it away with her sleeve. The dust had come from the sand. The sand came from the sea.

Sela did not move. She read the ledger. Her finger traced a line. "Parameter nine," she said. "No name removed without cause. No bed empty by force. No child sent to the mainland alone."

Pip stood on tiptoe. He could not reach the tube. He tugged Brann's coat. "Does it hurt?" he asked. "The noise?"

Brann shook his head. "It warns us," he said. "The wire knows the rule. The wire keeps the rule."

The whine stopped. Silence fell heavy. The drip plinked. The lamps hummed.

Tobin capped his pen again. He blew on the wet ink. "Parameter nine holds," he said. "The ledger binds the officer too."

Sela nodded. She turned a fresh page. "We write the watch schedule," she said. "Two hours each. Day and night. The tube must be heard."

Mira felt the care in her chest grow warm. She picked up the small bucket. It was heavier than it looked. "I take first watch," she said. "I know the sound."

Brann handed her a cloth. "Wipe the contacts if the whine returns," he said. "Salt builds fast."

Pip held out his hand. "I carry the lantern," he said. "My turn to walk with you."

The bell rope stayed still. The tube stayed quiet. The ledger lay open on the table. The ink dried black and final.
