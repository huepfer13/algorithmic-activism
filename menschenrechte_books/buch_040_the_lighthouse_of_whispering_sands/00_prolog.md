# The Lighthouse of Whispering Sands

The sea came closer every year.

Mira stood at the edge of the village and watched the water nibble the shore. It was a slow nibble. But it never stopped.

"Two steps less than last spring," said Tobin beside her. He held a stick and measured the same spot every morning. He liked numbers. He liked them to stay the same. They never did.

Behind them, the lighthouse bell rang once. It was a low, round sound. Everyone in the village knew what it meant. Time to check the ledger.

Old Sela kept the ledger. She had kept it for forty years. Her hands were slow now, but her eyes were sharp. "Water for the north lane," she read aloud. "Three buckets. No more."

"But my roof leaks," said Captain Brann. He had a boat, a big voice, and a small patience. "I need fresh water to fix it."

"The ledger says three buckets," said Sela. "The bell says three buckets."

The bell always agreed with the ledger. That was the trouble.

Pip tugged Mira's sleeve. Pip was small and quick and asked questions like other children asked for bread. "Why does the bell decide?"

"Because someone has to," said Mira. But she was not sure.

That night, the tide rose higher than anyone had seen. It slipped under doors. It stole the path to the well. When morning came, the well was salt.

"No water in the well," said Tobin. His voice was quiet. "The ledger has no rule for that."

Old Sela opened the ledger. She turned the pages. Her face grew long. "It says the well gives water. The well does not give water."

Captain Brann crossed his arms. "Then the ledger is wrong."

"The ledger is never wrong," said Sela. But she said it softly.

Mira looked at the bell. It hung still and silent. "The bell watches over every rule," she said. "But who watches over us?"

Pip raised a hand. "The old stories say the island once shared everything. Even the water."

Everyone went quiet. The sea breathed against the shore.

"We will read the old stories," said Mira. "And we will write a new rule. A rule that keeps everyone safe."

That night, the children lit a lamp in the lighthouse window. It was small. But it was a start.

**Ledger entry, first night: When the sea takes one thing, we share the next.**
