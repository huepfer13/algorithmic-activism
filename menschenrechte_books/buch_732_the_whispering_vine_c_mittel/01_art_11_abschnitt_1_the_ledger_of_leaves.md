# Abschnitt 1: The Ledger of Leaves

The creature of shadow and smoke did not attack. It tilted its head, coal-eyes narrowing, and then it spoke again in that ancient voice that seemed to rise from the roots themselves.

"You carry a ledger," it said, looking at Kael. "Show it."

Kael's hand went to his satchel. He drew out a folded page—the list his village elder had pressed into his palm, names of the sick, names of the dying, written in a cramped hand. The creature did not touch it. It only breathed, and the page grew warm.

"These names," the creature said, "are charges. Each one a claim against the forest. The forest does not accept a claim until it has heard the defense. Until then, every name is held innocent of the rot that took them."

Elian frowned. "Innocent? They are already—"

"They are held. Not judged." The creature's form rippled. "The vine keeps the ledger. It presumes nothing. It weighs nothing until the trial. This is the oldest law of Eldoria, and it is older than your villages, older than your grief."

Aria stepped forward, her poet's tongue sharp. "And who speaks for the dead in this trial?"

The creature turned to her, and for a moment its coal-eyes softened. "The living. Always the living. But the living must come with clean hands, and the path to the trial is narrow."

Kael folded the page and tucked it away. His jaw was set. "Then we go to the trial."

"You go to the trial," the creature agreed. "But know this: the vine will not hold a name guilty on account of an act that was not yet a crime when it was done. It will not weigh a heavier penalty than the one that stood at the time. This is the calibration. Break it, and the ledger breaks, and the forest with it."

Elian felt the air change. Somewhere above them, a pneumatic pipe groaned—one of the old root-conduits the forest used to carry its whispers—and then fell silent. He glanced up and saw fine paper-dust drifting down from a cracked relay-joint, settling like pale snow on the moss. The mechanism had jammed. The whispers would not come through that line tonight.

"The conduit is blocked," he said quietly. "The dust is on the contacts."

The creature inclined its head. "Then you will have to listen with your own ears. The vine does not repeat itself."

Aria looked at the two of them, then at the dark path that opened beyond the glade. "Protection first," she said. "We do not reach the trial if we do not reach it together."

Kael nodded once and stepped forward. Elian followed. Behind them, the creature of shadow and smoke did not move, but its voice carried, thin and precise, like a clerk reading from a docket.

"The trial convenes at the third bell. Bring your names. Bring your defense. And bring each other—or the ledger will close without you."

The path narrowed. Somewhere ahead, a bell that had not rung in a hundred years began, faintly, to toll.
