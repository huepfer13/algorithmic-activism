# The Ministry of Adequate Happiness

The Central Bureau of Emotional Compliance occupied nine floors of a building that had been designed for seven, and the discrepancy had never been formally resolved. The lift had been condemned in the spring as a security risk, which meant that every citizen ascending to the happiness-declaration window on the fourth floor arrived already out of breath, and the Bureau had ruled that shortness of breath did not constitute grounds for a lowered joy quota. Ondine Vex had drafted that ruling herself, in triplicate, and had felt nothing about it, which she logged as adequate.

The morning the shortfall was announced, Bartholomew Quill stood at the declaration window with his Form 7-B held between two fingers, as was his habit, and refused to release it. "The box for *current emotional state*," he said, "offers three options. Content, adequately content, and provisionally content. I am none of these. I am a fourth thing."

"There is no fourth thing," said Ondine, without looking up. "The form is exhaustive. It was exhaustively reviewed."

"Then I require a new form."

"You may file Form 22-C to request a new form. Processing takes eleven weeks. In the interim you will select an option."

Sister Perpetua Grange, who had queued behind him since dawn and whose particularity was that she prayed only in bureaucratic Latin, murmured something about *fructus procedendi* and began, quietly, to stamp her own forehead with a rubber date. Tobias Mudd, the Bureau's sole remaining archivist, had discovered the shortfall six days before anyone else and had told no one, because the correct procedure for discovering a shortfall was to discover it in the presence of a witness, and he had been alone. He now carried the knowledge in his chest like a swallowed stone and took the stairs two at a time to avoid conversation.

The shortfall itself was simple and unbearable: the Bureau could no longer afford to distribute the full allotment of joy. Rationing would begin Monday. Each citizen would receive, in place of the standard two minutes of sunlight, one minute and forty seconds, and the missing twenty seconds would be recorded on a new form — Form 7-B, Revised — which did not yet exist, and which Ondine had been instructed to design by Friday, and which she had been staring at for two days without result, because every version she drafted contained a fourth thing.

Bartholomew Quill lowered his Form 7-B at last. "Then I will appeal," he said. "Within the nine-floor stairwell. As is my right."

Ondine looked up for the first time. "The stairwell is not a venue," she said. "The stairwell is a corridor."

"Everything is a corridor," said Sister Perpetua, stamping her wrist, "if you appeal long enough."
