# The Glass Room Protocol

The glass does not fog. That is the first thing Elena Voss notices as she takes her seat—the surface stays perfectly clear, temperature-regulated, indifferent to her breath. Beyond it, three hundred faces in the gallery seating, already watching. Beside her, Marcus Reed arranges his papers with the precision of a man who has never once been late.

"Seventy-two hours," he says, not looking up. "And you've brought a single folder."

"I brought what I need."

Dr. Aris Thorne's voice arrives through the ceiling speaker, flat and clinical. "The chamber is sealed. Climate at twenty-one degrees. Lighting will shift every six hours. Communication to the exterior is disabled for the duration. You may address the gallery, but they cannot address you." A pause. "Your first drafting session begins now."

Lila Chen, seated at the operator's console outside the glass, touches her earpiece. She is the only person in the building who can hear both chambers of the machine—the negotiators' microphones and the operator's private channel. She has been told not to intervene. She has been told many things.

Marcus opens his folder. "Article One. Security of the person. Without it, every other right is decoration."

Elena sets down her pen. "Article One. Freedom of expression. Without it, you cannot name the harm that security pretends to prevent."

"That is a slogan, not a draft."

"It is a position. You've had yours pre-printed."

The lights dim by a fraction—Thorne's hand on the dial. Marcus's jaw tightens; he does not like being observed while he thinks. Elena notices, files it away. She has been in rooms like this before, but never one that watched her back.

"Let us be precise," Marcus says. "Dignity. Define it."

"Define life first," Elena says. "Yours is the clause that suspends it."

Outside the glass, Lila watches a red indicator blink on her console—a pressure reading from inside the chamber. It should not be rising. The seal is holding. The seal is holding, she tells herself, and reaches for the log.

"Article Two," Marcus says. "Protection of life and physical integrity takes precedence over all other provisions."

Elena's pen stops moving. "Over all?"

"Over all."

The temperature in the chamber drops a single degree. Neither of them mentions it.
